# Ambala development plans, ring road, IMT, airport and waste management

## Development plan 2031 or 2041: verification result

A notified Ambala Development Plan 2031 or 2041 was not verified in this research. Do not rename the available Ambala Final Development Plan 2025 as a 2031 or 2041 plan. The perspective year is not the publication year.

A reproduced Haryana Gazette map is explicitly titled "Final Development Plan 2025-AD" for controlled areas 1 to 5 under section 5(7) of Act No. 41 of 1963. It bears the Gazette date 6 September 2012. The accompanying third-party plan index identifies notification `CCP(NCR)/AMB/FDP/2012/2916`, dated 6 September 2012, and lists amendments dated 29 November 2016 and 15 December 2017. The index counts seven controlled areas, whereas the reproduced map title says areas 1 to 5. This discrepancy requires the original notification set; it is not resolved here.

The map labels drainage installations, the Ghel drain, Model Town drain, and solid-waste disposal at Patvi. It also depicts a proposed peripheral road. A road shown on a land-use map is not proof of the as-built alignment or current status of the NHAI ring road.

The NCR Planning Board's 69th Planning Committee agenda discusses Draft Regional Plan 2041 and Ambala's identification as a counter-magnet area. That regional planning reference is not an Ambala statutory Development Plan 2041. Plot-level decisions require the applicable notified plan, zoning regulations, amendments, controlled-area boundary and approved sector plan from the competent planning authority.

## Ambala ring road

Hindustan Times reported on 12 September 2026 that the six-lane ring road under NHAI's Bharatmala programme was nearly 90 percent complete, with completion targeted for December 2026. The report describes a roughly 40 km project costing about Rs 691 crore, using 657 acres across 30 villages, including 27 in Haryana and three in Punjab.

The reported connections include Ambala-Chandigarh, Ambala-Kala Amb, Ambala-Saha, Ambala-Hisar and Ambala-Shamli routes. The bypass is intended to divert through traffic away from Ambala City and Cantonment. Sadhopur is identified as a starting point for traffic arriving from Chandigarh, Punjab and Himachal Pradesh.

An earlier report of 3 June 2021 described a 39 km proposal worth Rs 1,500 crore and a proposed acquisition of 177.75 hectares. These older proposal figures differ from the 2026 report. Do not treat them as the same cost or land-accounting basis without NHAI package documents. The September 2026 progress estimate is a dated statement, not evidence that the whole road subsequently opened.

## Proposed IMT Ambala

Hindustan Times reported on 20 March 2025 that Haryana's FY 2025-26 budget proposed an HSIIDC Industrial Model Township on at least 800 acres in the first phase near Ambala City. The report says that in November 2024 HSIIDC had found about 850 acres suitable in Naggal, Nadiali and Khaira villages.

The then deputy commissioner was quoted as saying that 426 farmers had offered approximately 2,076 acres through the e-Bhoomi portal. Land offered by farmers, land found suitable, purchased land and developed industrial plots are different stages. The source does not establish completed acquisition, serviced plots, allotment or industrial operations. IMT Ambala must also be distinguished from the existing industrial estate at Saha.

## Ambala domestic airport and civil enclave

The Indian Express reported on 20 September 2026 that commercial flights had not begun at the Ambala Civil Enclave. Its account describes a terminal site of about 20 acres, use of the Indian Air Force runway, and pending Bureau of Civil Aviation Security approval. The foundation stone was laid in October 2023.

The report quotes a February 2026 parliamentary answer stating that development works were complete and security approval was in progress. It records Rs 25 crore sanctioned under UDAN, and the Ambala-Srinagar-Ambala route awarded under UDAN 4.2 for a 19-seat aircraft. The route, initially awarded to FlyBig, had been novated to Skyhop Aviation Private Limited.

Completion of civil works is not confirmation of passenger operations. The article also separately mentions defence land costing approximately Rs 133 crore and an earlier terminal construction estimate of approximately Rs 20 crore. These refer to different components and should not be combined as a verified final project cost. No flight booking or operational schedule was independently verified for this archive.

## Swachh Survekshan and Patvi waste management

The Tribune's 30 July 2025 report records Ambala's recently announced Swachh Survekshan rank as 212th. Amar Ujala's 25 July report also gives national rank 212 and Haryana rank 17. These are attributed news figures; the precise population-category table was not independently reconciled here, so "212th" should not be read as a rank across all Indian ULBs.

The Tribune reports that HSPCB imposed Rs 62 lakh environmental compensation on Municipal Corporation Ambala for non-compliance at the Patvi solid-waste plant. It describes unscientific or unauthorised operation and reports that payment remained pending according to board sources at that time. This is not evidence of a later payment, appeal outcome or current compliance status.

Mayor Shailja Sachdeva questioned plant records showing around 220 tonnes of garbage being dumped while roadside heaps remained. She also identified non-segregation of wet and dry waste and called for an audit. That quoted quantity is not a verified plant processing capacity or independently measured city waste generation rate.

The official municipal register lists RFP `2026_HRY_559513_2` for door-to-door collection, segregation, transportation and processing of waste, published 10 November 2026 with expiry 25 November 2026. It separately lists tender `2026_HRY_557712_1` for SBM Urban 2.0 capacity building, dated 31 October 2026, covering Ambala Corporation, Ambala Sadar, Barara and Naraingarh. Neither listing proves contract award or improved sanitation outcomes.

## Flood resilience and jurisdiction

The Tribune's 25 June 2025 account describes recurring Tangri flooding, riverbed encroachments and delayed flood-control works in Ambala Cantonment. A 2023-24 arrangement between the Irrigation Department, NHAI and Municipal Council Ambala Sadar proposed deepening the riverbed by six feet and using excavated earth for the ring road. It reports ongoing desilting and pump-house upgrades, not a completed flood-protection guarantee.

Hindustan Times reported renewed inundation on 29 August 2025 after Tangri overflow and an embankment breach, with evacuations by NDRF, SDRF and police. These events demonstrate that construction activity or desilting alone cannot establish flood safety.

For Ambala City, a 28 July 2025 report of the Chief Minister's announcements lists Rs 10 crore for concreting the Manmohan Nagar section of Ambala drain, Rs 35 crore for Model Town drain sections, Rs 12 crore for Old Delhi Road drain, and Rs 1.50 crore for stormwater disposal at the Deputy Commissioner's office campus. These were announcements, not verified expenditure or completion certificates. Tangri works in Sadar and City drain works should remain separately attributed to their responsible agencies.

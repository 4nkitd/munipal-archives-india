# Bahadurgarh development plans, KMP and Metro connectivity, and industrial estate jurisdiction

## Development Plan 2031 and the 2041 planning record

The statutory document commonly called the Bahadurgarh Master Plan 2031 is the Final Development Plan 2031 AD for controlled areas I, II, III and IV part. Haryana notified it on 13 July 2012 through notification No. CCP (NCR)/BGH/FDP-2031/2012/2213 under section 5(7) of the Punjab Scheduled Roads and Controlled Areas Restriction of Unregulated Development Act, 1963. Its plan drawing is DTP (JH) 425/12, dated 14 May 2012. [1]

The plan uses a projected population of 4.84 lakh for 2031, not a measured current population. It identifies 4,960 hectares of total urbanisable area, including 235 hectares of existing town and villages.

| Land use in the 2031 plan | Area in hectares |
| --- | ---: |
| Residential | 1,700 |
| Commercial | 241 |
| Industrial | 876 |
| Transport and communication | 613 |
| Public utility | 215 |
| Public and semi-public | 297 |
| Open space | 783 |
| Total proposed land uses | 4,725 |

TCP also hosts a Final Development Plan 2041 AD drawing. Its PDF yielded no extractable text during this research, so this record does not assign it an unverified notification date, population target or area schedule. [Scanned map/PDF, visual source linked in source 2.]

The 25 June 2025 public notice independently confirms use of FDP 2041 for revised sector plans. Notice No. RCP-3/JD(GK)/2025/24123 identifies drawing DTP(JH)/741/2025 dated 12 March 2025 for Sectors 28, 28A and 29, and DTP(JH)/714/2024 dated 10 October 2024 for Sector 3B. The Government had approved these revisions "in-principally" and invited objections within 30 days of publication. Representations from Gram Panchayats Sankhol and Kasar concerned Panchayat land, revenue paths, water bodies and drains. This was a consultation on proposed revisions, not evidence of their final disposal or of completed infrastructure. [3]

## Delhi Metro Green Line and proposed extensions

HMRTC records commercial operation of the Mundka to Bahadurgarh extension from 24 June 2018. The extension measures 11.182 km, comprising 4.875 km in Haryana and 6.307 km in Delhi. These figures describe the extension, not the full Green Line. HMRTC gives the project cost as Rs 2,028.96 crore. [4]

The three elevated Haryana stations are Pandit Shree Ram Sharma, Bahadurgarh City and Brigadier Hoshiar Singh. They provide Bahadurgarh access to Delhi's Metro network through the Green Line. A Green Line station is not a direct Metro service to Manesar or Gurugram. Passengers must check the current DMRC network and interchange arrangements for their destination.

The HMRTC page for Bahadurgarh to Sampla describes a proposed corridor of tentative length 17.40 km and a request to DMRC for a techno-feasibility study. It links a study report. That page does not establish an operational extension, a construction completion date or a direct Gurugram connection. [5]

The 2012 development plan also mentioned a future regional rapid transit proposal. That historical planning reference must not be treated as proof that an RRTS service to Bahadurgarh has opened. Likewise, a development-plan alignment is not a station access road, sanctioned building plan or guarantee that an industrial plot has convenient last-mile transport.

## NH-9 and KMP access towards Manesar and Gurugram

The 2031 gazette places Bahadurgarh on the Delhi to Hisar National Highway 9 corridor. Some older estate descriptions call this road NH-10. Both labels appear in the official sources, and users should retain the road number used in the underlying document when tracing older approvals. [1, 6]

The Supreme Court's 13 May 2016 judgment in M.C. Mehta v. Union of India describes the 135.650 km KMP alignment as crossing the old NH-10 west of Bahadurgarh and the old NH-8 near Manesar before reaching Palwal. The judgment identifies HSIIDC's role in procuring the expressway. PIB records inauguration of the Kundli to Manesar section on 19 November 2018. [7, 8]

For road travel and freight, the linkage is the Bahadurgarh highway approach to KMP, followed by the southbound expressway towards Manesar and onward road connections into Gurugram. This is an orbital road connection outside Delhi, distinct from the Delhi-bound Metro corridor. The cited judgment uses historical highway numbering, not a current toll or interchange operating schedule.

No fixed journey time, present toll rate or vehicle entitlement is asserted here. Those depend on the chosen access point, current restrictions and destination. A parcel beside the expressway does not automatically have lawful direct access to an access-controlled road.

The 2031 plan reserves Sector 17A between the railway and NH-9 for an inland port-cum-Transport Nagar, with truck parking, warehousing and transport-related uses. This is a land-use proposal in the 2012 document, not confirmation that every listed facility has been built. [1]

## HSIIDC industrial estates and Municipal Council responsibilities

The Directorate of MSME's Footwear Park page identifies Sector 17, an area of 316 acres and status "Planned and Developed". Its listed plot categories total 368 plots. The page was last updated on 14 August 2020; the figures are a published estate description, not current vacant-plot availability. [6]

| Plot size in square metres | Number listed |
| --- | ---: |
| Above 4,050 | 6 |
| 4,050 | 76 |
| 1,800 | 69 |
| 1,012.50 | 97 |
| 450 | 120 |

The Government of India's Jhajjar industrial profile separately lists the Government Industrial Area, MIE estates I and II, HSIIDC Sector 16, HSIIDC Sector 17 and HSIIDC Sector 4B. Its historical areas and plot counts should not be merged with the Footwear Park description as if all sources covered an identical estate boundary or date. [9]

| Matter | Authority or record to check first |
| --- | --- |
| Development-plan land use, sector-plan revision and applicable planning restrictions | TCP Haryana and District Town Planner, Jhajjar |
| HSIIDC plot allotment, transfer, possession and estate dues | HSIIDC and its e-Sewa allottee records |
| Industrial estate waterworks or internal infrastructure | Estate engineering authority and any later handover order |
| Municipal property records and municipal services within notified municipal limits | Municipal Council Bahadurgarh, subject to the particular asset and service jurisdiction |
| Metro operations or station matters | DMRC; HMRTC for Haryana project records |
| Industrial pollution permissions and complaints | Haryana State Pollution Control Board |

HSIIDC's published 2021 tender, ID 2021_HBC_169189_1, concerned annual operation and maintenance of the 11 MLD water treatment plant, Sector 17 boosting station and Sector 4B waterworks. It is concrete evidence of HSIIDC estate engineering work at that time, not proof of unchanged responsibilities in 2026. [10]

An industrial estate boundary, controlled-area boundary and Municipal Council boundary answer different questions. Estate development by HSIIDC does not by itself prove exclusion from municipal limits or exemption from municipal obligations. Conversely, inclusion in municipal limits does not establish that the Council owns every estate road or operates its waterworks. Obtain the latest municipal boundary notification, estate layout, allotment conditions and any service-transfer order before deciding responsibility for a specific plot or complaint.

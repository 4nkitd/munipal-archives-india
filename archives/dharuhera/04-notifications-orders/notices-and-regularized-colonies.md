# Dharuhera official notices, notified colonies and Bhiwadi effluent proceedings

## Source scope and dates

This record indexes official material affecting Dharuhera rather than treating statewide notices as local committee orders. The frontmatter date, 13 October 2025, is the publication date shown for DULB's “Municipalities Status of 836 Colonies Data Notified 2025” listing. It is not a single gazette date for all colonies. DULB's index retains the heading “List Of Regularized Colony 2023 & 2024” while also carrying 2025 documents.

Environmental case history below comes from an HSPCB status report on the NGT's official website. A regulator's report can reproduce allegations, summarize earlier orders and report field observations. Those three kinds of statement are distinguished here. This archive does not certify that a proposed drainage project was built, that effluent standards now comply, or that a court stay has since ended.

## Bhiwadi-origin effluent and waterlogging case history

The primary source is the [HSPCB status report in OA 617/2023, Prakash Yadav versus State of Haryana](https://www.greentribunal.gov.in/sites/default/files/news_updates/REPORT%20BY%20HSPCB%20IN%20OA%20617%20of%202023%20PRAKASH%20YADAV%20VS%20STATE%20OF%20HARYANA.pdf), including its referenced annexures. The complaint alleged untreated Bhiwadi industrial effluent entering agricultural land at Khaliyawas, Khatawali, Rajpura and Alamgeerpur through an abandoned HSVP treatment installation. The report does not accept all aspects of that allegation as fact.

The report cites later monitoring correspondence through **1 June 2024**. Its exact filing date is not inferred from that latest cited letter. The HSIIDC letter quoted in the report prints an inspection date of 7 March 2023, whereas the report's inspection narrative and HSVP account identify 7 March 2024. This discrepancy should be checked against the original annexure if the date is material.

## Inspection findings and limits of municipal responsibility

HSPCB reported that during the inspection no wastewater was being released onto the lands of the four named villages or into the Sahibi River. It also reported that HSVP had no STP in the Dharuhera industrial area at the inspected location. There was a pumping unit that was not working. This is narrower than a finding that the entire cross-border pollution issue had ceased.

The same report states that effluent from Bhiwadi passes through Dharuhera town toward NH-8 and the industrial area and adjacent villages. It estimates approximately **12 MLD** entering Dharuhera, based on the pumps deployed by MC Dharuhera near NH-8 during non-rainy days. It says the natural gradient could carry this water to the villages during heavy rain. It also records joint HSPCB and RSPCB monitoring and analysis results exceeding prescribed limits.

For record requests, distinguish MC Dharuhera's pumping and local drainage expenditure from HSVP assets, HSIIDC industrial-estate infrastructure, and pollution-control enforcement by HSPCB and RSPCB. A municipal waterlogging complaint alone does not establish which Rajasthan industry discharged effluent. Relevant evidence includes sampling location, date, analysis report, drain route, pumping log, agency correspondence and the applicable judicial order.

The report's 2024 observations and its account of the 2022 stay are historical evidence. No later official compliance closure, final appellate disposition or verified permanent drainage completion is established by the sources summarized in this record.

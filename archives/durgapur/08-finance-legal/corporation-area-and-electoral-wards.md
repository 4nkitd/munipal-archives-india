# Durgapur: corporation limits and electoral wards

## Municipal territory

Durgapur’s corporation records 154.20 km², five boroughs and 43 wards. Its city profile distinguishes the corporation from the larger urban agglomeration, which also includes Bamunara and Arrah census towns. The corporation replaced the Durgapur Notified Area Authority on 7 October 1996. See the [state corporation directory](https://udma.wb.gov.in/modal-content/5) and [local authority record](https://durgapurmunicipalcorporation.org/about.php).

## Which record defines the boundary?

Section 403 specifically names the Durgapur Municipal Corporation Act, 1994. Its transitional provisions cover property previously vested in the corporation and contracts or liabilities legally subsisting immediately before commencement. These provisions connect Durgapur’s older municipal records to administration under the 2006 Act.

The corporation’s [composition register](https://durgapurmunicipalcorporation.org/composition.php) dates the Notified Area Authority to 1 October 1962. It records the first corporation election on 25 May 1997 and the first Mayor-in-Council taking charge on 11 June 1997. These institutional milestones are separate from the 1996 change of status.

The Kolkata Gazette Extraordinary of 29 May 2008 published West Bengal Act XXXIX of 2006 under notification 957-L. Chapter II distinguishes the declaration of intention, its publication, consideration of objections and constitution of the corporation area (sections 3–6). Section 7 concerns the number of wards; section 8 deals with abolishing or altering the limits of a corporation area. The publication date is that of the Act, not a redrawing of Durgapur’s wards.

The [SEC Municipal Election Manual 2015](https://wbsec.wb.gov.in/writereaddata/Upload/Portal/Publication/Municipal_Election_Manual_201520241203083924.pdf), printed page 19, reproduces section 3 of the West Bengal Municipal Elections Act, 1994. The State Government determines the ward count; the Commission divides the area by notification, considering population, dwelling pattern, geography and economic conditions. For an existing municipality it considers the municipality’s views. Each ward is a single-member constituency. To establish which side of a street was in a ward at a particular election, use the delimitation notification for that election. Neither the ward count nor this Act supplies that street schedule.

# MCF House meetings, committees and citizen oversight, 2025-2026

## Scope and evidence

This report was compiled on 22 November 2026. Its date is the compilation date, not the date of a single municipal resolution. The Haryana Municipal Corporation Act, 1994 supplies the statutory framework. Contemporary newspaper reports supply the meeting chronology below. Signed MCF agendas, attendance registers, resolution numbers, committee-constitution orders and action-taken reports were not obtained for these events.

An announced meeting is not evidence that it occurred. A budget allocation does not establish contract award, expenditure or completion. The same distinction applies to a committee's reported agreement on an agenda item. Obtain the signed resolution and relevant sanction or work order before treating a proposal as an executable project.

There are material inconsistencies in press coverage. An August 2025 report anticipated the Finance Committee's formation, a January 2026 report said it had been formed, and a May 2026 report described its continued absence. A June 2026 report then described its first meeting. These accounts are retained with their dates rather than combined into an unsupported formation date. [S3-S6]

## Finance and Contracts Committee

Section 40(3) of the Act provides for a Finance and Contracts Committee comprising the mayor, the two deputy mayors, two members elected by the members from among themselves, and the commissioner. That is six statutory participants, including the commissioner. Section 40(4) makes the mayor its ex-officio chairperson. Section 40(3) assigns the committee the Corporation's powers concerning contracts and purchases. [S1-S2]

The June 2026 report names councillors Pradeep Tongar and Manoj Nasawa as newly appointed Finance Committee members. Commissioner Dhirendra Khadgata, Additional Commissioner Paramjit Chahal, Corporation Secretary Amit Mann, Chief Engineer Vivek Gill, Medical Health Officer Dr Nitish Parwal and Finance Controller Pradeep Punia were also reported present. Attendance is not proof of committee membership. The report does not establish whether the two deputy mayor posts had been filled or reproduce the committee's constitution order. [S6]

Section 40(2) also provides for Water Supply and Sewerage Disposal, Buildings and Roads, and House Tax Assessment committees. Section 40(1) permits ad hoc committees, including experts, for delegated functions or inquiries. The material reviewed does not establish MCF's complete committee roster for 2025-2026. The August 2025 press account described a Rs 50 lakh operational approval threshold, but no current delegation notification was obtained. Do not use that press figure as a verified legal limit. [S1-S3]

The same report said the House reduced the proposed mayor's annual discretionary development allocation from Rs 30 crore to Rs 5 crore and retained Rs 2 crore per councillor. It also reported decisions concerning waiver of interest on colony development charges, municipal operation of community buildings and work on parking arrangements. The operative conditions require the actual resolutions. [S7]

Jagran's 29 March account said the sanitation spending roadmap was questioned during the budget discussion and officers agreed to prepare a report. Its drainage and town-planning figures differ from Hindustan's figures. Those disputed heads are not reconciled here without the approved budget schedule. [S8]

### Committee discussion, 24 June 2026

Waste-to-energy, solid-waste management and development works were among the subjects discussed at the reported first Finance Committee meeting. The source gives only an aggregate outcome of nine agreed items and two deferred items. It does not say which waste proposal was approved, its contractor, capacity, cost or implementation deadline. It therefore cannot support a claim that a particular waste-to-energy contract was sanctioned. [S6]

### Separate state-level approval, August 2026

Jagran reported on 22 August that a chief-minister-chaired high-power purchase committee had approved a Rs 815.72 crore door-to-door waste-collection tender for Faridabad. The reported arrangement covered east and west zones for five years, with a possible two-year extension. The report expressly said the municipal work order was still pending. This was a reported state-level approval, not evidence of an MCF House or F&CC resolution, contract execution or service commencement. [S9]

## Citizen governance under the 1994 Act

The following provisions are drawn from the Act's substantive text. Some reproduced contents pages mislabel the sections on minutes; the section numbers below follow the operative text. [S1-S2]

| Provision | Mechanism and practical use |
| --- | --- |
| Section 10 | The government constitutes Wards Committees for one or more wards. The ward councillor is a member; the Act specifies how the chairperson is selected. Ask MCF for the applicable constitution notification before assuming that a particular committee is operational. |
| Section 52 | The Corporation should ordinarily meet at least monthly. A written requisition by at least one-fourth of the total membership triggers the obligation to convene a special meeting. Citizens can raise ward issues through councillors, but this requisition right belongs to members. |
| Section 54 | The business list ordinarily goes to members at least five days before the meeting. Urgent meetings can have shorter notice; supplementary business has its own notice and chair-permission conditions. |
| Sections 55 and 57 | The ordinary quorum is one-third of total membership; decisions normally use a majority of members present and voting. Special rules govern adjourned meetings. |
| Section 58(4)-(5) | Meetings are open to the public unless a majority present decides that an inquiry or deliberation should be in camera. Admission and removal are subject to regulations. Public attendance is not an unrestricted right to address the House. |
| Section 59 | A member must not vote or participate in specified matters affecting that member's pecuniary or property interest. |
| Section 60 | Members can question the commissioner about municipal administration during the first half-hour, subject to statutory restrictions. This is a councillor's questioning mechanism, not a public question period. |
| Sections 63-64 | The secretary records attendance and proceedings; minutes go before the next meeting for signature. House minutes circulate to members and are available at the Corporation office for inspection at reasonable times. Public inspection is subject to the prescribed fee provision. |

For a sanitation or development complaint, submit a dated written representation identifying the location, ward, problem and requested remedy to the ward councillor and Corporation Secretary. Ask for the agenda item, resolution number and action-taken status. To check a claimed approval, request the signed minutes, detailed estimate, financial sanction, tender reference, award and work order. RTI under the separate Right to Information Act, 2005 is another route to existing records; it does not itself sanction a work.

Area Sabhas should not be attributed automatically to the 1994 Act. Haryana also has a separate Municipal Citizens' Participation Act, 2008. No Faridabad-specific Area Sabha constitution notice, membership list or meeting record was obtained for this report, so no claim is made that such bodies were operational in MCF during this period.

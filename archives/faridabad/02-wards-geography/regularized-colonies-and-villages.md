# Regularized colonies and 24 merged villages in Municipal Corporation Faridabad

## Scope and legal distinctions

This record combines the Haryana Urban Local Bodies department's colony notification index and parcel schedule with the municipal boundary notification and dated reporting on works. The frontmatter date is the verified 25 July 2023 colony notification date, not the date of village merger or the completion of infrastructure.

Three different processes affect properties in Greater Faridabad, also called Neharpar:

1. Municipal boundary expansion brings a revenue estate or area under Municipal Corporation Faridabad, abbreviated MCF.
2. A colony notification identifies a specific layout and khasra schedule for civic amenities in an infrastructure-deficient municipal area.
3. The Abadi Deh or Lal Dora property-certificate process records ownership or occupation in an old village habitation where individual revenue rights were not traditionally recorded.

These processes are not interchangeable. Inclusion of a village in MCF does not establish that every colony in its revenue estate has been notified. A named colony must be matched to the relevant layout, colony ID and khasra numbers. Likewise, an infrastructure announcement is not evidence that a sewer or drinking-water network is operational.

The official ULB schedule is titled "Civic Amenities And Infrastructure Deficient Areas." News reports commonly call these colonies "regularised." This record retains that public terminology but does not treat the schedule as a blanket title certificate, building permission or approval of land outside its notified boundary.

## The 24 merged villages and the expansion date

## Lal Dora and Abadi Deh properties

Haryana ULB issued the policy and SOP for property certificates in municipal Abadi Deh or Lal Dora areas by its **12 March 2024** communication. It invokes section 250 of the Haryana Municipal Act, 1973 and section 398 of the Haryana Municipal Corporation Act, 1994.

The policy concerns old village habitation, generally enclosed by the phirni or Lal Dora and represented by a common khasra, where individual rights records were not traditionally maintained. It excludes land held under specific khasra numbers from that description. The policy allows an acceptable ownership document or other documents indicating possession for the preceding ten years to support the certificate process.

Municipalities must identify the relevant properties, publish preliminary ownership and occupation information and distribute individual details for verification under the SOP. The certificate links the record to the existing municipal property ID. The policy describes a rebuttable presumption for the property record, not an unconditional adjudication of every title dispute.

An official Faridabad PDF is explicitly labelled "Property List of Possession Format 1: Publication of Draft List" and "Lal Dora Faridabad." That is a draft publication, not a list of final certificates for all 24 villages. No inference is made here that every property in a merged village is Lal Dora land, or that every owner has received a certificate.

Citizens can consult the [official Lal Dora property search](https://property.ulbharyana.gov.in/PropertyList/SearchProperty). For a disputed record, first establish the village, property ID, old abadi boundary and whether the entry is draft or final. Extended abadi colony notifications and Lal Dora certificates must be checked separately.

| Reporting date | Reported action or condition | Status supported by the source |

| 1 October 2024 | The Tribune reported no comprehensive infrastructure-upgrade project in the 24 incorporated villages. Residents of Chandawali, Sahupura, Malerna and Mirzapur described sanitation, drainage, water and streetlight problems. | Reported service gaps, not an engineering audit. |
| 1 December 2024 | Following a 22 November meeting chaired by ULB Minister Vipul Goel, MCF was directed to prepare a DPR covering 81 colonies and 24 villages. | 21 January 2025 | The Tribune reported work initiated in 72 colonies, a submitted estimate of Rs 116.29 crore, and nine colonies excluded from that work package because of existing amenities or restrictions. | Initial work was mainly street tiling. | 4 March 2025 | Hindustan reported continuing waste, streetlight and drainage complaints in merged villages, including Kheri Kalan, Palwali and Chandawali. | Post-announcement complaints remained. |
| 23 October 2025 | Amar Ujala reported an approximately Rs 400 crore plan for roads, sewers, drinking-water lines, lighting, parks and community buildings in the 24 villages. | Proposed programme; not evidence of completion or expenditure. |
| 30 September 2026 | Amar Ujala quoted MCF Executive Engineer Ajay Panghal on an approximately Rs 360 crore sewer programme for 81 colonies and 24 villages. The DPR was nearly ready and tendering was to follow. | Reported budget approval and pre-tender preparation, not an awarded contract or functioning network. |

The January 2025 report also mentioned restrictions on works within 100 metres of the Air Force station. That is a reported constraint affecting the package, not a substitute for the applicable defence-area order or a property-specific clearance.

The Rs 116.29 crore estimate, Rs 400 crore village plan and Rs 360 crore sewer proposal describe different reports and scopes. They are not added together here. No project completion certificate, expenditure statement or end-to-end service verification was obtained for this record.

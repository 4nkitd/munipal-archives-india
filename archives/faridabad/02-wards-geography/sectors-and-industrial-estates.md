# Faridabad sectors, industrial estates and municipal service responsibilities

## Scope and limits

This record groups localities for service enquiries. It is not a ward-delimitation schedule, an approved land-use certificate or a complete asset-handover register. Sector numbers are planning identifiers, not municipal ward numbers. No ward assignment is made here.

The older sector belt between Sectors 2 and 37 includes residential neighbourhoods, industrial estates, commercial land and institutional uses. It is incorrect to describe every sector in this numerical range as residential. Greater Faridabad, also called Neharpar, includes the Sectors 75 to 89 belt across the canals. A numbered sector there can contain HSVP plots, licensed private colonies and apartment projects with different internal maintenance arrangements.

HSVP is the Haryana Shehri Vikas Pradhikaran, formerly HUDA. HSIIDC is the Haryana State Industrial and Infrastructure Development Corporation. FMDA is the Faridabad Metropolitan Development Authority. Development by one authority does not establish that it still operates every road, sewer or water connection.

## Older sectors, Sectors 2 to 37

HSVP's October 2023 layout-plan index lists Faridabad sectors including 2, 3, 7, 19, 20A, 28, 30, 31 and 37. Its July 2024 index also lists 9, 12, 14 and 15A. These are useful starting points for locating a plot or sector road. HSVP expressly advises users to confirm the latest layout with the concerned Estate Officer or District Town Planner. The indexes are not evidence that all land within a listed sector is residential. [1]

Residential sector names must retain their suffixes, particularly 15A, 16A and 21A, 21B, 21C and 21D. A complaint stating only "Sector 21" may not identify the correct location. HSVP's August 2024 residential auction schedule independently identifies residential properties in Sector 2 and Sector 21B. [2]

| Locality group | How to use the name | Service qualification |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Sectors 2 and 3; residential neighbourhoods within the older sector belt | Give sector, plot or house number and nearest dividing road | Check whether the affected asset is municipal, HSVP-retained or metropolitan infrastructure |
| Sectors 7 to 11 and 14 to 19, including lettered subdivisions | Retain suffixes and distinguish an internal street from a sector-dividing road | An HSVP allotment does not itself identify today's maintenance agency |
| Sectors 20A, 20B and 21A to 21D | Use the full sector and pocket designation | Planning and estate matters may remain with HSVP after service transfer |
| Sectors 28 to 37 | Identify the particular residential, industrial, institutional or other pocket | Do not infer residential land use from the number |
| Sectors 4, 5, 6, 13, 24, 25 and 27A to 27D | Check the industrial estate and plot records | Official industrial records establish industrial uses within the older numbered belt |

HSVP's *Information at a glance*, updated to 12 December 2021, states that maintenance of all services in transferred Faridabad sectors, including water supply and sewerage, went to Urban Local Bodies. Its transfer table records 32 Faridabad sectors and says all 24 sectors transferred statewide in 1992 were in Faridabad. This is historical aggregate evidence, not a parcel-level list or proof that every sector from 2 to 37 was transferred. [3]

## Greater Faridabad and Neharpar, Sectors 75 to 89

The locality range comprises Sectors 75, 76, 77, 78, 79, 80, 81, 82, 83, 84, 85, 86, 87, 88 and 89. Treat the sector as the location and the colony or project as a separate maintenance unit. HSVP itself lists Faridabad Sectors 75, 76 and 77 in its layout indexes. Neharpar is therefore not synonymous with a single private developer's estate. [1]

FMDA's official tender record establishes metropolitan water infrastructure work across this belt. Tender **2021_HRY_192950_1**, listed on 22 September 2021, concerns the balance water-supply scheme in Sectors 75 to 89, including DI K-9 pipes, sluice valves and trenchless pipe work, at an advertised amount of Rs 770 lakh. This establishes a works remit, not completion of the entire scheme. FMDA's short-tender index separately lists maintenance of master sewer lines in Sectors 78 to 89 and greenbelt cleaning across Sectors 75 to 89. [4]

A 27 September 2025 *Hindustan* report describes a proposed transfer of BPTP residential colonies to MCF. It reports that the Town and Country Planning Directorate sought MCF's consent and that the developer would retain other licence obligations. The report does not supply a final takeover order. It must not be treated as proof that all fifteen sectors, all private projects or every internal asset were transferred. [5]

For an internal colony road, park or distribution pipe, obtain the colony licence number and the completed handover schedule. For a master water main, trunk sewer or sector-dividing infrastructure, identify the FMDA asset or contract. An RWA maintenance bill and a municipal property-tax demand are not substitutes for those records.

## Industrial belts and estate identity

The Government of India's *District Industrial Profile of Faridabad 2016-17*, using DIC Faridabad data, records the following industrial areas. Its plot counts and acreages are historical, not a current availability register. HUDA in that source is today's HSVP. [6]

| Belt or estate | Verified description | Responsibility check |
| --- | --- | --- |
| NIT Industrial Area | The industrial profile lists a large or medium unit at Industrial Area, NIT | NIT is not a single estate-management designation. Identify the street, plot and original allotting authority before assigning maintenance |
| Sector 24 | HUDA industrial area, 409 plots and 664 acres in the historical table | Separate plot and lease matters from maintenance of transferred public roads and drains |
| Sector 25 | HUDA industrial area, 173 plots and 540.15 acres | Check the particular road, sewer and water connection authority |
| Sector 27A | HUDA industrial area, 81 plots and 114.60 acres | Keep the letter suffix |
| Sector 27B | HUDA industrial area, 15 plots and 141.63 acres | Do not merge with 27A, 27C or 27D |
| Sector 27C | HUDA industrial area, 76 plots and 108.75 acres | Use the industrial plot number and access road |
| Sector 27D | HUDA industrial area, 4 plots and 147.15 acres | Historical estate identity does not prove current maintenance ownership |
| Sector 58 | The profile records HUDA and HSIDC involvement and a separate electroplating zone | Confirm the estate pocket and effluent-management arrangement |
| Sector 59 | The profile records HUDA and HSIDC industrial plots and sheds | The sector number alone cannot distinguish the allotting or servicing agency |
| DLF Industrial Area | The profile lists units in DLF Industrial Estate and Phase I | Use phase, block and plot. Do not classify it as HSIIDC merely because it is industrial |

## IMT Faridabad and the Sector 70 qualification

HSIIDC's current official IMT Faridabad description identifies **Sectors 66, 67, 68 and 69** as planned and developed. It locates the estate across the Agra Canal, linked to the former National Highway 2 by a 90-metre-wide periphery road, and gives 2012 as the establishment year. Listed infrastructure includes metalled roads, water supply, sewerage disposal, a common effluent treatment plant, power distribution and provision for commercial, institutional and residential uses. [7]

The Haryana government's Final Development Plan 2031 notification of **14 March 2018**, No. **CCP(NCR)/FDB/FDP-31)/2018/694**, also identifies HSIIDC's developed IMT in Sectors 66 to 69 east of the Agra Canal. [8]

Sector 70 is included in this locality index because the requested industrial search belt extends through it. Neither of these official descriptions establishes Sector 70 as part of the developed HSIIDC estate. An address using "IMT, Sector 70" is not sufficient evidence of an HSIIDC allotment or service obligation. Check the allotment letter, approved estate boundary and municipal boundary for that site.

HSIIDC has commissioned estate-level sanitation as well as industrial infrastructure. Haryana tender **2020_HBC_140190_1**, published 19 August 2020, specifies sweeping Sector 69 roads and footpaths and carrying IMT solid waste to an MCF-approved disposal point. This is evidence of an HSIIDC-to-MCF disposal interface, not evidence that MCF sweeps every IMT road. [9]

## Municipal obligations and complaint routing

The Haryana Municipal Corporation Act, 1994 separates municipal duties from the practical question of which agency controls an asset. Section 43 requires adequate provision for drainage, water supply, scavenging and removal of rubbish. Section 42 lists functions that government may entrust, including roads, water supply, sanitation, parks and street lighting. Sections 268 to 272 address street cleaning, rubbish collection and waste accumulated at factories and workshops. Sections 195 and 205 concern prohibited inputs to municipal drains and permission for water or drainage connections. [10]

| Problem | First record or authority to check |
| --- | --- |
| Public street sweeping, ordinary municipal waste or a municipal drain | MCF sanitation or engineering branch, with exact location and asset details |
| Internal road, park or sewer within an untransferred private colony | Developer's licence and maintenance obligations, with DTCP and MCF asked to confirm takeover status |
| Master infrastructure in Sectors 75 to 89 | FMDA asset or tender record; distinguish master infrastructure from internal colony networks |
| IMT internal road, estate water supply or common treatment infrastructure | HSIIDC estate office and allotment or connection conditions |
| HSVP plot title, allotment, demarcation or layout | Concerned HSVP Estate Officer or planning office |
| Industrial effluent or hazardous industrial waste | HSPCB requirements and the estate's treatment and authorised disposal arrangements, not an ordinary municipal garbage complaint alone |

A service-transfer claim should identify the order date, effective date and asset schedule. Older aggregate transfer statements do not settle later FMDA allocations. Where agencies disagree, request written identification of the road, pipeline, drain or disposal facility and the authority currently responsible. This record does not certify a current citywide division of sewer maintenance.

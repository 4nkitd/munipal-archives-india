# Faridabad wards 1 to 15: NIT belt and adjoining industrial colonies

## Notification and reading rules

Haryana's Urban Local Bodies Department issued notification 6/73/2023-5CI on 11 January 2024, published in Gazette Extraordinary 8-2024/Ext. It publishes Faridabad ward boundaries under rule 10 of the Haryana Municipal Corporation Delimitation of Ward Rules, 1994, following the draft notification dated 7 November 2023. The source is a reproduction of the government gazette, not a municipal GIS download. [1]

The schedule describes boundaries through roads, drains, railway walls, individual houses, schools and shops. A named sector or village can be a boundary landmark rather than an area wholly contained in that ward. The following table is therefore a locality index, not a replacement for the legal boundary traverse. Colony names alone do not establish the ward of a property.

Do not substitute the older 40-ward map for the 46-ward election geography. Contemporary reporting describes the January 2024 increase to 46 wards. The district administration's election page provides final electoral rolls and ward-wise election documents for checking individual addresses. [2][3]

NIT 1 to NIT 5, also called NH-1 to NH-5 in local addresses, are township neighbourhood labels, not municipal ward numbers. The evidence above establishes specific NIT 1 and NIT 2 blocks and an NH-3 approach. It does not establish a complete block-by-block allocation for all five neighbourhoods. In particular, no blanket NIT 4 or NIT 5 allocation is asserted.

## Sector 21, Sainik Colony and the Pali-Badkhal corridor

Do not force these northern and western localities into wards 1 to 15 merely because they belong to the wider NIT/Badkhal urban belt.

The same gazette's ward 17 schedule explicitly references Sector 21D Labour Chowk, its waterworks and police post. Ward 18 starts at Kumhar Wali Puliya in Badkhal village on Badkhal-Pali Road, then references Badkhal lanes, Aravalli hills and Badkhal Lake. Those are outside this record's ward-number range. [1]

The ward 10 description reaches Dabua-Pali Road. That road reference must not be expanded into an assignment of Pali village to ward 10. Likewise, a Badkhal-Pali Road boundary point does not place the whole corridor in one ward.

Sainik Colony H Block is described as ward 19 in a 15 June 2026 local report. That is secondary evidence only, included to flag the scope mismatch, not to establish the whole colony's legal ward boundary. Sector 21 subdivisions and Sainik Colony require their own official ward-map or electoral-roll checks. [10]

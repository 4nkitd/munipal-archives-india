# MCF administrative zones: NIT, Old Faridabad and Ballabgarh

## Scope and verification date

Municipal Corporation Faridabad uses the administrative zone names NIT, Old Faridabad and Ballabgarh. NIT means New Industrial Town or New Industrial Township. Official RTI disclosures identify a Joint Commissioner for each zone and separate the municipal functions handled within them. The Haryana ULB disclosure also lists smaller taxation units, including NIT Zones I, II and III and Ballabgarh Zones I and II. These taxation subdivisions are not additional city-level administrative zones. [1][2]

This record was compiled on 22 November 2026. Its frontmatter date is the compilation date, not a notification creating the zones. Officer postings and ward assignments have changed over time. The available material establishes the three-zone structure but does not establish a complete, current zone-to-ward allocation for all 46 wards. Historical ranges below must not be used as a current jurisdiction certificate.

The district's official municipal-office listing gives B.K. Chowk, Faridabad, and telephone 0129-2416464. It is the verified central contact for confirming a property's responsible zone before visiting a counter. A property's municipal zone, electoral ward, engineering division and revenue subdivision are different classifications. [3]

## Joint Commissioners and responsibility

The Smart City Faridabad RTI table identifies the respective Joint Commissioner as the first appellate authority for zonal sanitation, building-related enforcement and tax/licensing matters. Engineering appeals in that table go to the Superintending Engineer. The Haryana ULB RTI disclosure similarly names Joint Commissioner, Ballabgarh, and Joint Commissioner, Old Zone, against their respective branches. This is evidence of administrative responsibility, not a guarantee that every citizen service follows the same appeal route. [1][2]

| Post | Named-officer evidence | Status at compilation |

| Joint Commissioner, NIT | Reports of the Haryana transfer order dated 10 November 2026 name Ranbir Singh, HCS, replacing Anil Kumar Yadav. [7] | Recent secondary-source posting evidence. The original posting order and assumption of charge were not independently verified. |

Do not substitute the Joint Commissioner of Police for a municipal Joint Commissioner. Likewise, the SDM Ballabgarh heads a revenue subdivision, not MCF's Ballabgarh municipal zone. Address applications to the municipal designation when the incumbent's name is uncertain.

## Ward ranges and changes in numbering

A 2019 research paper describes the following allocation under the 40-ward arrangement introduced in 2016-17. This is a secondary-source historical mapping, not a current MCF allocation order. [9]

| Zone | Reported wards in the 40-ward arrangement | Count |
| --- | --- | --- |
| NIT | 5 to 21 | 17 |
| Old Faridabad | 22 to 34 | 13 |
| Ballabgarh | 1 to 4 and 35 to 40 | 10 |

There is evidence that branch-level allocations did not always match that summary. The official ULB Ballabgarh park inventory labels parks in wards 1, 2, 4, 33, 34, 35, 36, 37, 38 and 40 as Ballabgarh Zone assets. It lists Sector 9/10/11 parks in ward 33 and Sector 7/8 parks in ward 34. Its date and relationship to later administrative orders are not established. It therefore cannot validate a current, uniform zone-to-ward crosswalk. [6]

The Haryana Gazette notification dated 11 January 2024, number 6/73/2023-5CI, publishes revised electoral ward boundaries. The ULB notification index lists a Faridabad final wardbandi entry dated 23 April 2024. These are different date fields and should not be silently treated as the same notification date. The 2025 election reporting describes 46 wards. [5][10][11]

Election nomination locations also do not define administrative zones. For example, the February 2025 report assigns wards 21 to 24 to an ARO at the Old Faridabad municipal office and wards 25 to 27 to an ARO at the Ballabgarh municipal office. Those are election-counter assignments, not proof that those wards belong to the named municipal zones. [11]

For current routing, obtain MCF's latest zonal charge/allocation order and match it to the applicable wardbandi schedule and the property's address or property ID. This municipality-wide record leaves the frontmatter ward array empty rather than encoding an unverified current mapping.

## Road corridors and regional connections

### Mathura Road and the western connection

Delhi-Mathura Road is the main north-south highway reference through Faridabad and Ballabgarh toward Palwal, Mathura and Agra. Government project documentation identifies this corridor as NH-19, formerly NH-2. The 2024 delimitation descriptions repeatedly use Delhi-Mathura Road as a boundary landmark. Its presence in a ward description does not make the road a continuous boundary between the three municipal zones. [5][12]

Faridabad-Gurugram Road provides the westward regional connection across the Aravalli area toward Gurugram. The National Institute of Solar Energy's official address confirms Gwal Pahari on Faridabad Gurugram Road. Manesar is an onward destination through the Gurugram/NH-48 road network, not the direct terminus of this road. Do not confuse this inter-city connection with a municipal boundary or with KMP itself. [12][13]

### KMP and the Delhi-Mumbai Expressway spur

The DND-Faridabad-Ballabgarh-Sohna corridor is NH-148NA, a spur connecting the Delhi side through the Faridabad-Ballabgarh bypass to the Delhi-Mumbai Expressway near KMP. The Ministry of Road Transport and Highways' 2020 appraisal records three packages. Package III runs from the Sector 62/65 dividing-road junction to the junction near KMP with NH-148N, a design length of 26.063 km. [12]

PIB's 28 February 2019 project announcement describes an overall corridor of more than 59 km, starting at Ring Road-DND junction, passing through Kalindi bypass and Faridabad-Ballabgarh bypass, and ending at the Delhi-Mumbai Expressway interchange at KMP. KMP means Kundli-Manesar-Palwal Expressway. These are regional connections toward Sohna, Manesar and the wider NCR, not additional MCF zones. The cited announcements and appraisal establish alignment and project scope; they do not by themselves verify every section's traffic-opening status on the compilation date. [14]

### FNG is a separate corridor

The Faridabad-Noida-Ghaziabad corridor, commonly FNG, proposes an eastward connection across the Yamuna. It is not another name for NH-148NA or the Delhi-Mumbai Expressway spur. A 1 July 2026 report describes incomplete sections and a proposed Yamuna bridge between the Lalpur side of Faridabad and Chak Mangrola near Noida Sector 168. It reports only 11.6 km complete within Noida's 23 km portion. Do not present FNG as a verified continuous operational route. [15]

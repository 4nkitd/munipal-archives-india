# Encroachment removal, sealing, demolition and street-vending enforcement in Faridabad

## Record scope and documentary status

This is a compiled enforcement register, not a single consolidated MCF order. The frontmatter date identifies MCF's public notice of 21 August 2021, described in paragraph 7 of the Supreme Court judgment in *Narinder Singh and others v. Divesh Bhutani and others*, Civil Appeal 10294 of 2013 and connected matters. The linked PDF is a judgment copy hosted on government infrastructure, not the original municipal notice. Its upload path does not establish the notice date.

That notice gave the public two days to remove illegally constructed farmhouses, banquet halls and structures on forest land. It stated that MCF and the Forest Department would otherwise undertake removal on 23 August 2021. The judgment records the challenge by landholders in Anangpur, Ankhir and Mewla Maharajpur to this notice and to special orders dated 18 August 1992 under section 4 of the Punjab Land Preservation Act, 1900.

Signed municipal orders, individual property notices and complete demolition inventories were not recovered for the press-reported actions below. They are identified as reported actions, not transcriptions of operative orders. No present-day clearance, stay, title finding or demolition deadline for an individual property can be inferred from this register.

The Supreme Court judgment explains that land covered by special orders under section 4 of the Punjab Land Preservation Act has the character of forest land for section 2 of the Forest Conservation Act, 1980. Diversion to non-forest use requires prior Central Government approval. This finding concerns land covered by the relevant special orders. It is not a finding that every parcel in Faridabad is forest.

The 2025 PTI report cited a drone-survey inventory of 6,793 small and large illegal structures and identified Anangpur, Lakkarpur, Ankhir and Mewla Maharajpur as areas of further action. That survey count is not a verified count of completed demolitions. Khori rehabilitation was a separate process. Indian Express reported a policy announced on 13 July 2021 proposing EWS flats in Dabua Colony and Bapu Nagar for eligible households.

## NIT and Ballabgarh market encroachment

An 8 April 2025 Jagran report quotes Joint Commissioner Hitendra Sharma describing a proposed change in enforcement against goods placed on roads and footpaths. MCF would first videograph markets and then seal offending shops, even if goods had been moved inside by the time the team arrived. Reopening would require the Commissioner's orders. The report identifies NIT 1 market and Tikona Park car market as repeat encroachment locations. It supplies no signed policy number, fee schedule or universal sealing timetable.

For Ballabgarh, Hindustan's 20 November 2025 report describes carts being broken during a drive on the previous Wednesday. It records vendors' objections and Joint Commissioner Karan Singh's statement that action followed discussion with trader representatives and would continue if encroachments were not voluntarily removed. The report does not establish whether each affected vendor held a vending certificate or received statutory notice.

Following a fire in Ballabgarh's main market, reports of 8 September 2026 describe MCF clearing permanent and temporary obstructions in the main market and Sihi Gate market. Hindustan names Junior Engineer Aslam Khan as leading the squad under Joint Commissioner Karan Singh Badauria's directions. Jagran reports that access for fire engines had been obstructed and quotes instructions for regular clearance. These are accounts of local operations, not evidence of a citywide prohibition on licensed vending.

## Street-vending law and procedural safeguards

The Street Vendors Protection of Livelihood and Regulation of Street Vending Act, 2014 regulates vending as well as protecting livelihoods. Its procedures must be distinguished from building demolition and forest-land litigation.

- Section 3 requires a survey of existing street vendors and subsequent surveys at least once every five years. Section 3(3) protects vendors against eviction or relocation until the specified survey is completed and certificates are issued to all street vendors.
- Section 18(1) permits a local authority, on the Town Vending Committee's recommendation, to declare a no-vending zone for a public purpose and relocate vendors in accordance with the scheme.
- Section 18(3) requires thirty days' notice before relocation or eviction from the place specified in a vending certificate. Physical removal under section 18(4) follows failure to vacate after the notice period.
- Section 19 requires a signed copy of the list of seized goods. On a claim made under the statutory procedure, non-perishable goods must be released within two working days and perishables on the same day.

Haryana ULB's indexed scheme page records Street Vendors Rules notified on 31 January 2017, amended Rules on 27 May 2020, and the Haryana Street Vendors Scheme notified on 12 June 2020. The page returned a 404 during retrieval. A separate ULB PDF titled as a draft dated 27 April 2020 has blank notification particulars. It must not be represented as the final gazetted scheme or used to assert a verified Faridabad fee, vending-zone boundary or eviction order.

No signed MCF Town Vending Committee resolution, approved city vending plan or complete current no-vending-zone schedule was recovered for this record. Obtain those instruments and the affected vendor's certificate before applying a reported market-clearance instruction to a particular case.

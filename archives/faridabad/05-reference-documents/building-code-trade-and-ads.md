# Faridabad building code, trade licences, advertisements and telecom permissions

## Scope and document dates

This record brings together separate permission regimes affecting premises and infrastructure within Municipal Corporation Faridabad, or MCF. A building sanction, trade licence, advertisement permission and telecom right-of-way permission are not interchangeable.

The frontmatter date is the first revision date printed in the Haryana Building Code 2017, 6 January 2017. Its preface records the original publication of the Haryana Building Code 2016 on 30 June 2016. This is a reference to the original 2017 text, not a consolidated statement of all subsequent amendments. The DULB town planning notifications index separately lists adoption of amendments and a final 2017 Code with a letter under the portal date 4 January 2021.

For advertisements, the corporation-specific 2018 instrument is historical. The statewide Haryana Municipal Advertisement Bye-Laws, 2022 expressly repealed the 2018 and 2019 advertisement bye-laws, subject to savings for existing permissions. For telecom infrastructure, the older tower bye-laws must be read against later state policy and the national Telecommunications Right of Way Rules, 2024. Current fee demands and conditions should therefore identify the instrument actually applied to the particular application.

## Building plan approval under the Haryana Building Code 2017

Chapter 2 of the [Code published by Invest Haryana](https://investharyana.in/content/pdfs/The-Haryana-Building-Code-2017.pdf) requires a person proposing erection, re-erection, alteration or demolition to give notice to the competent authority in Form BR-I. The submission includes ownership documents, a site plan, building drawings, specifications in Form BR-II, structural drawings and the applicable structural-safety certificates. Fire-safety design, public-health service plans and HVAC plans are required where applicable. The applicant must appoint the relevant architect or engineer.

The [DULB building approval service page](https://www.ulbharyana.gov.in/Home/ApprovalRevisionOfBuildingPlan) routes industrial and commercial cases through Invest Haryana and provides a separate Haryana Online Building Plan Application System, or HOBPAS, link for residential and institutional cases. It lists ownership records such as sajra, intakal, jamabandi, mutation and lease deed, the property-tax receipt, site plan and site report, and the change-of-land-use permission where applicable.

| Item | Verified source provision |
| --- | --- |
| Scrutiny fee | Rs 10 per square metre of covered area, under Code 2.1 and the DULB checklist |
| Other charges | Applicable development charges and composition charges are separate |
| Original Code sanction validity | Two years for buildings below 15 metres; five years for buildings 15 metres or above, under Code 4.3 |
| Revised plans | Code 4.6 requires approval before making an intended deviation, subject to the stated exception for compoundable alterations |
| Standard approval service | DULB's published procedure specifies 60 days from receipt of complete documents for the identified original-municipal-limit service |

NOCs depend on the site and proposal. The DULB checklist identifies access permission, pollution control, fire, Airport Authority of India, National Monument Authority, water and sewerage, conservation-zone, environmental-clearance and HAREDA requirements where applicable.

The original Code's low-risk definition and the service page's later risk categories differ. Do not select self-certification from the original PDF alone. Confirm the current category, municipal boundary status and land-use position in the application system. Building sanction does not replace the occupation certificate required at the relevant stage.

## Trade and health-related premises licences

The [Haryana Municipal Corporation Act, 1994](https://ulbharyana.gov.in/Website/DirectorateofUrbanLocalBodies/Images/2d98f87d-c6f6-44c2-920e-8fb13fede849.pdf) distinguishes factory permission under section 330, use of premises for specified purposes under section 331, hawking under section 334 and eating-house licensing under section 335. These provisions are relevant when an applicant refers generally to a trade licence or health licence. The correct permission depends on the actual activity and premises.

MCF's [published business trade licence rate list](https://ulbharyana.gov.in/Website/DirectorateofUrbanLocalBodies/Images/7d0f8f5e-942a-4dac-8d72-5055e0dce82c.pdf) cites Haryana ULB memo No. 14/26/2012-5CI dated 10 April 2012. Its section 331 Part I schedule includes the following annual fees:

| Activity | Annual fee in the archived schedule |
| --- | --- |
| Eating house or catering establishment | Rs 2,500 |
| Hairdressing salon or barber's shop | Rs 1,000 |
| Laundry shop | Rs 2,500 |
| Sweetmeat shop, except premises already licensed as an eating house | Rs 500 |
| Lodging house or hotel | Rs 1,000 per air-conditioned room and Rs 500 per non-air-conditioned room |

These are historical schedule amounts, not verified current quotations.

The [DULB new trade licence procedure](https://ulbharyana.gov.in/Website/DirectorateofUrbanLocalBodies/Images/57d2aab6-88a7-45b8-a860-5b3f6bf337aa.pdf) names the Zonal Taxation Officer, specifies a 15-day timeline after application submission and lists Rs 100 in its fee field. It also says the verifier determines fees before payment. The Rs 100 entry should not be read as replacing MCF's activity-specific annual charges.

The procedure permits application through [SARAL Haryana](https://saralharyana.gov.in/), a CSC or SARAL Kendra. It requires premises ownership, rent or lease proof and applicant identification. Its individual-applicant checklist includes Family ID, or Parivar Pehchan Patra. After verification and payment through the ULB system, the approver issues the licence online. A separate current MCF health-licence checklist was not verified for this record. Confirm activity-specific requirements with MCF rather than treating the generic trade checklist as sufficient for every health-related business.

## Hoardings, unipoles and kiosks

The historical Haryana Municipal Corporation Advertisement Bye-laws, 2018 were notified on 28 March 2018, No. 2/4/2017-R II. The [2022 final notification](https://ulbharyana.gov.in/Website/DirectorateofUrbanLocalBodies/Images/7336bd48-2b21-4880-9677-50294926d62f.pdf), No. 9/24/2022-4C-II dated 15 July 2022, applies to municipalities throughout Haryana. Bye-law 30 repeals the 2018 and 2019 instruments but allows previously granted permissions to continue until their contract periods expire.

Under the 2022 text, a hoarding or billboard is an outdoor media device, or OMD, with an advertisement panel supported on a structure. A unipole carries its panel on a single ground-founded column. Annexure 5 also covers advertising on public utility kiosks and other street furniture.

Third-party advertisements require the appropriate municipal advertisement rights. Bye-law 9 provides for e-auction of government, municipal or private sites, or permission fees for private property. Registration is distinct from approval of a particular display. Annexure 2 describes registration valid across Haryana municipalities for five years.

Schedule 1 limits hawker self-advertisement on a stationary kiosk or handcart to 3 square metres, with dimensions of 3 metres by 1 metre. Shop self-advertisement boards have a one-metre height limit, a width limited to the building width and a maximum projection of 150 millimetres. These provisions do not authorise occupation of a footpath or installation of a kiosk. The schedule prohibits ground OMDs on the right of way and pedestrian pathway, and prohibits rooftop OMDs.

Applicants must check the current schedule, site category, structural requirements, property documentation and permission charges. DULB's policy index labels a document as a 2025 amendment, but the linked Gazette dated 25 September 2025 is explicitly a draft inviting objections. It is not evidence by itself that the proposed amendments became law. A later final amendment was not verified for this record.

## Mobile towers and optical-fibre right of way

MCF's [rules and bye-laws index](https://ulbharyana.gov.in/Faridabad/7390) retains historical tower bye-law links. The DULB policy compendium also indexes adoption of the Communication and Connectivity Infrastructure Policy instead of the 2013 bye-laws on 30 January 2024. These older materials should not be used alone to calculate a present-day permission or rental liability.

The [DULB connectivity permission procedure](https://ulbharyana.gov.in/Website/DirectorateofUrbanLocalBodies/Images/8457cf85-78e4-488e-900a-4a9f2171261f.pdf) distinguishes overground infrastructure in Form 1 from underground telegraph infrastructure or fibre-to-home right of way in Form 2.

| Infrastructure | Documents identified in the published procedure |
| --- | --- |
| Tower or other overground installation | DoT licence or registration, location plan, technical and foundation drawings, structural-safety certification, ownership and consent or lease documents |
| Tower-specific clearances | Applicable fire clearance, SACFA clearance or application acknowledgement, and the stipulated electromagnetic-radiation self-certification acknowledgement |
| Underground OFC or fibre to home | DoT credentials, route map with latitude and longitude, nature of land, landowner consent, stretch-wise work schedule and indemnity |
| Protected or forest area | Applicable forest or environmental clearance |

The procedure directs applications to Invest Haryana, names the Joint Commissioner as designated officer for a municipal corporation and gives a 45-day timeline from receipt of complete documents. It describes document assessment and site reporting by the Junior Engineer, engineering recommendation, approval, fee demand and issue of the permission letter.

The [DULB Right to Service page](https://ulbharyana.gov.in/Home/RightToService) continues to list this 45-day municipal service and the Invest Haryana application route. However, national Telecommunications Right of Way Rules, 2024 took effect on 1 January 2025. The [DoT circulars index](https://preprodeservices.dot.gov.in/circular-notifications-others) lists a Haryana implementation order published on 8 April 2025. Confirm the operative national rules, state order and current portal workflow before relying on older municipal fees, forms or timelines. This record does not certify that a portal submission or permission was completed.

# Faridabad solid waste and sanitation works, 2024 to 2026

This is a composite record, not an invitation to bid. Its frontmatter date is the date on the MCF request-for-proposal template published by Haryana Urban Local Bodies. Tender estimates, reported award results and actual service commencement are separate milestones.

## Bandhwari landfill and diversion of fresh waste

The Bandhwari landfill is in Gurugram on the Gurugram-Faridabad corridor. It received municipal solid waste from both cities, while the Municipal Corporation of Gurugram (MCG) managed legacy waste remediation at the site. In Original Application 172/2021, *Poonam Yadav v. Ecogreen Energy Pvt. Ltd. & Ors.*, MCG's [9 April 2025 affidavit](https://www.greentribunal.gov.in/sites/default/files/news_updates/Report%20by%20Additional%20Commissioner,%20MC,%20Gurugram%20in%20OA%20No%20172%20of%202021%20(Poonam%20yadav%20Vs.%20Ecogreen%20Energy%20Pvt.%20Ltd.%20&%20Ors.).pdf) recorded 11.32 lakh metric tonnes still to be processed on 31 March 2025. It listed 22 February 2025 work orders for 9 lakh tonnes to Green Tech Enviro Management and 5 lakh tonnes to Aadarsh Bharat Enviro. Both started work on 24 March, according to the affidavit.

The [CPCB report of 9 March 2026](https://www.greentribunal.gov.in/sites/default/files/news_updates/Report%20by%20CPCB%20in%20OA%20No%20172%20of%202021%20Poonam%20yadav%20Vs%20Ms%20Ecogreen%20Energy%20Pvt%20Ltd%20and%20Ors%20WITH%20OA%20No%20109%20of%202022%20WITH%20OA%20No%20221%20of%202025.pdf) recorded an MCG estimate of 16.60 lakh tonnes on 31 January 2026 and identified MKG Green Energy and Daya Charan and Company as the agencies then engaged to process 14 lakh tonnes. CPCB's 17 February inspection found continued fresh dumping, no site risk assessment or detailed on-site emergency plan, and the use of untreated leachate for sprinkling. These were landfill remediation contracts, not Faridabad household collection contracts.

MCF officials told [Hindustan Times on 28 June 2026](https://www.hindustantimes.com/cities/gurugram-news/faridabad-stops-dumping-waste-at-bandhwari-landfill-after-13-years-of-use-101782619457841.html) that Faridabad stopped sending fresh waste on 26 June. They cited listed capacities of 150 tonnes per day (TPD) at Mujheri and 250 TPD at Pratapgarh, a proposed 600 TPD plant at Sotai and plans to expand Pratapgarh. The diversion did not mean full processing. In [November 2026](https://www.hindustantimes.com/cities/gurugram-news/faridabad-identifies-new-waste-dumping-site-after-hc-halts-disposal-in-aravallis-101794595377978.html), MCF identified Mirzapur as a temporary disposal site after the Punjab and Haryana High Court's 5 November interim order restrained further dumping on 152 acres near Bhakri. MCF said it planned to increase Pratapgarh's capacity from 200 to 650 TPD. Separately, [MCG reported to the NGT](https://www.hindustantimes.com/cities/gurugram-news/ngt-seeks-mcg-s-plan-to-handle-fresh-waste-at-bandhwari-landfill-101794076985108.html) that about 14.7 lakh tonnes remained at Bandhwari on 31 October 2026 and proposed a 31 July 2027 clearance target.

## Pali processing proposal and the separate Mothuka charcoal plan

Pali was considered for processing fresh municipal waste. In the [26 May 2023 oversight committee minutes](https://www.mcg.gov.in/assets/NotificationDocuments/Minutes%20of%20the%20meeting%20of%20the%20Oversight%20Committee%20constituted%20in%20OA%20No.%20172.pdf), the MCF commissioner reported that site development works had been allotted but tree-felling permission was pending and the Supreme Court had ordered status quo on 18 May 2023 in SLP 9953/2023. In March 2024, [The Tribune](https://www.tribuneindia.com/news/haryana/faridabad-mc-operationalises-two-waste-segregation-processing-plants-602376/) reported that MCF had dropped Pali as a processing location after local opposition and was using trommels at Mujheri and Pratapgarh instead. Separate [March 2024 reporting](https://timesofindia.indiatimes.com/city/faridabad/trash-makes-its-way-to-2-villages-from-bandhwari/articleshow/108294076.cms) described disputed deposits of material trucked from Bandhwari to Pali and Dabua. The deposits do not establish that an authorised Pali plant opened.

The charcoal proposal named **Mothuka**, not Pali. On [20 July 2024](https://newsonair.gov.in/ntpc-vidyut-vyapar-nigam-ltd-haryana-govt-sign-mou-to-set-up-green-charcoal-plants/), NTPC Vidyut Vyapar Nigam Limited and the two corporations signed an MoU for torrefied charcoal projects at Mothuka and Bandhwari. MCG's April 2025 affidavit gave input capacities of 1,200 TPD for Faridabad and 1,500 TPD for Gurugram, with commissioning planned in 2027. The Faridabad Zila Parishad [called for relocation or abandonment](https://www.tribuneindia.com/news/haryana/faridabad-zila-parishad-passes-resolution-against-waste-to-charcoal-plant/) in late 2024. NTPC's board [approved the Faridabad proposal through Aravali Power Company Private Limited](https://www.investywise.com/ntpc-ltd-fc0eb8-2025/) on 26 September 2025 at an estimated ₹365.40 crore. In January 2026, [Amar Ujala](https://www.amarujala.com/delhi-ncr/faridabad/waste-to-charcoal-plant-to-be-built-on-41-acres-land-transfer-process-expedited-faridabad-news-c-26-1-fbd1020-61290-2026-01-26) reported that transfer of 41.17 acres at Mothuka was in process. These records do not establish commissioning.

Haryana announced a separate [OIL Green Energy MoU on 29 August 2026](https://prms.prharyana.gov.in/press-release/4638). It described a 1,600 TPD Faridabad district cluster project with compressed biogas from wet waste and at least 16 MW of projected power from non-recyclable dry waste. October 2028 was the state's target for the four projects announced that day. The announcement does not establish that the OIL project uses the Mothuka site or that the charcoal proposal was cancelled.

## Door-to-door collection after Ecogreen

Ecogreen Energy held the shared integrated waste concession. MCF reported that the government had terminated the agreement in its [September 2024 response in NGT OA 33/2024](https://research.lawsathi.in/national-green-tribunal/2024/original-application-no-33-2024-ajay-lal-malik-0701116000462024-2024091001/). The termination was contested, and [December 2024 reporting](https://www.tribuneindia.com/news/haryana/waste-collection-in-faridabad-stalled-amid-contract-issues/) described disrupted household pickup and interim collection by local vendors. Secondary transport from transfer stations continued under separate arrangements. The [17 October 2025 MCF model RFP](https://ulbharyana.gov.in/Website/DynamicUploadContent/CONTENTFILES/ee9d4617-fef8-4386-9523-e20a7a040d4e.pdf) proposed five years of door-to-door collection, source segregation and delivery to a processing site. The processing-site name is blank on the template.

## Construction and demolition waste

The [Faridabad annual action plan filed with CAQM](https://caqm.nic.in/WriteReadData/LINKS/Faridabad%20action%20plan3d93066f-2b30-4297-9eff-41f6b7935aae.pdf) estimates 300 TPD of construction and demolition (C&D) waste and records zero processing at Riwazpur. It says MCF terminated the RAW2Road contract with effect from 4 May 2025 for non-performance. The plan lists the following tentative temporary storage points, not a verified list of open drop-off locations:

| Area | Proposed interim collection point |
| --- | --- |
| Badkhal and NIT | Behind Nursery Bagh Boosting Station, near the railway line, NIT-5 |
| Old Faridabad | Baselwa Rakba, Kacha Kheri Road, near Dairy Yojna Scheme |
| Tigaon | Near Mawai cow shed |
| Ballabgarh | Ward 46, Pragati Vihar and Bapu Nagar |
| Mirzapur | Sinhi Rakba |

The plan's project table proposes a 300 TPD central plant at ₹38.33 crore with a March 2027 commissioning target and a 50 TPD mobile crusher at ₹3 crore with a December 2026 target. A narrative note in the same plan mentions October 2026 for a new facility, so the plan is not evidence of an opening. The November 2026 [Sector 25 civil works notice, 2026_HRY_558467_1](https://www.tendershark.com/details/haryana-tender/haryana-government/71c14a3c-2712-40f5-8fd9-a7531565f41d), estimated at ₹73.94 lakh, covered a boundary wall, shed, 100-tonne weighbridge, tubewell, portable cabin, CCTV and sprinklers near Hanuman Mandir Chatghat in NIT Ward 2. It does not establish a processing capacity or opening date.

## Mechanical road sweeping

On 15 November 2025 MCF published [2025_HRY_483611_1](https://etenders.hry.nic.in/nicgep/app?component=%24DirectLink&page=FrontEndLatestActiveTenders&service=direct&sp=SR33Z2%2BXY3nFJzjujZpEvWA%3D%3D) to hire one CNG or electric mechanised road sweeper in Old Faridabad. The portal lists ₹2.50 lakh in earnest money and a cancellation and recall corrigendum. The notice is not an award or a fleet count.

CAQM [inspected 127 MCF road stretches on 13 January 2026](https://www.pib.gov.in/PressReleasePage.aspx?PRID=2214588&lang=1&reg=3). It found high visible dust on 17 stretches, moderate dust on 25, low dust on 66 and none on 19. It called for more mechanical sweeping and water sprinkling at hotspots, prompt removal of municipal and construction waste, and enforcement against dumping and burning. In [November 2026](https://www.livehindustan.com/ncr/faridabad/story-faridabad-municipal-corporation-tightens-waste-management-and-air-pollution-monitoring-201794920757294.html), MCF reportedly directed machine sweeping on roads wider than 10 metres, route monitoring with GPS and cameras, and payment checks against video and GPS records.

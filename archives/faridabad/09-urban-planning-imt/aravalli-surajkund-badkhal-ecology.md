# Faridabad ecology: Aravalli corridor, Badkhal Lake, Surajkund and Yamuna

## Scope and evidence dates

Faridabad's environmental planning includes the Aravalli hills and lake catchments on its western side, the urban sewerage and drainage network, and the Yamuna river corridor to the east. These are connected through runoff, groundwater recharge and wastewater disposal, but they have different legal boundaries and responsible agencies.

This report was compiled on 22 November 2026. That date is the archival compilation date, not a common observation date for all environmental conditions. Court orders from 1996 and 2018, the Haryana Yamuna status report dated 4 December 2023, the Smart City project description published under an October 2022 URL, and later reporting retain their own dates. No current lake-water laboratory results, parcel-level forest clearance, floodplain boundary map or same-day GRAP invocation was verified for this record.

The report is a reference for locating permissions and evidence. It is not a land-use clearance, a monument boundary survey or a certificate that a treatment plant complies with its consent conditions.

## Aravalli corridor and development controls

The Supreme Court's 11 September 2018 judgment in the Kant Enclave matters concerns land in Anangpur, Faridabad. The Court held that the notified land at issue under the Punjab Land Preservation Act, 1900 must be treated as forest land. A town-planning permission cannot be read as a substitute for the applicable forest-law permissions. The judgment records the relevant Anangpur notification, No. S.O.104/P.A.-2/1900/S.3/92 dated 18 August 1992, and the earlier Section 3 notification dated 10 April 1992. This is evidence about the notified land considered by the Court, not proof that every plot in Anangpur has the same status. [1]

The lake-specific rules require particular care. On 10 May 1996 the Court prohibited mining within 2 km of Badkhal Lake and Surajkund and initially prohibited construction within 5 km. Its 11 October 1996 clarification sets out the construction regime more precisely:

| Area or activity | Direction recorded in the 11 October 1996 order |
| --- | --- |
| Green belts shown in Exhibits A and B | No construction, with a narrowly framed provision for essential recreational or tourism purposes subject to specified prior approvals |
| Outside those green belts, up to 1 km measured from the respective lake | No construction, subject to the order's express exceptions for earlier allotments, sanctioned works and specified personal residential use |
| Construction permitted under the first two directions | Clearance from the specified authority, CPCB and HSPCB before occupation certificates |
| Development schemes and building plans between 1 km and 5 km, excluding Delhi areas | Prior approval of CPCB and HSPCB |

The original order's mapped green belts and exceptions must be consulted. Describing the law simply as an unconditional present-day "5 km construction ban" loses the clarification. Equally, an exception in that order does not remove separate forest, heritage or planning restrictions. The Court linked these controls to protecting rainwater drains, lake water quality, aquifers and local hydrology. [2]

For an individual site, obtain its khasra details, applicable forest notifications, sanctioned land use, lake-distance assessment and the relevant orders before proposing construction, earthwork or tree removal. Do not assume that the 7 May 1992 Aravalli notification applies to every Faridabad parcel merely because the site is in the Aravalli hills.

## Badkhal Lake rejuvenation and water-quality safeguards

Faridabad Smart City Limited's published project description proposes replenishing the dry lake with treated sewage and developing its precincts. The treatment component includes a 10 million litres per day wet well and sewage pumping station, an SBR sewage treatment plant, tertiary treatment and treated-water pumping. The precinct proposals include vending, play, yoga and resting areas, a lookout point, an amphitheatre, jetties and a landscaped promenade. These are project-description components, not confirmation that every component was completed. [3]

An HSPCB submission in NGT Original Application No. 599 of 2022 records MCF's January 2023 statement that the Sector 21A 10 MLD plant had been commissioned and was treating 3 MLD at trial stage. The intended diversion of additional sewage by 28 February 2023 was a stated target. It should not be mistaken for a verified subsequent operating flow. [4]

The Tribune reported on 19 February 2025 that the approximately Rs 100 crore lake rejuvenation project remained unfinished and was expected to take another four to five months. That forecast does not establish an actual completion date. [5]

Down To Earth reported on 25 June 2026 that residents and environmental campaigners alleged tree deaths and possible groundwater contamination near the Parson Temple forest area in Ankhir after treated water entered the lake and adjoining land. An FSCL executive engineer disputed non-compliance and said the water met prescribed norms and was monitored. The report attributes the allegations and the official response separately. It is not an independent laboratory determination of causation. [6]

A defensible assessment needs dated influent and outlet test reports, actual daily flows, tertiary-treatment and disinfection records, lake-water and groundwater samples, and a survey of affected vegetation. Water in the lake or an installed treatment plant alone does not establish ecological restoration. The condition of feeder channels, sewage bypasses, water hyacinth, lake margins and the catchment also matters.

## Surajkund and Anangpur heritage

The Faridabad district administration describes Surajkund as an artificial reservoir with a semicircular, amphitheatre-shaped embankment in the Aravalli hills. It attributes the reservoir to the Tomar king Suraj Pal in the tenth century and gives its bed diameter as about 130 metres. These are the district portal's historical descriptions, rather than a new archaeological finding. [7]

The same portal describes Anangpur Dam, also presented as Anandpur Bandh, about 2 km southwest of Surajkund and attributes it to Anangpal of the eleventh century. Haryana Tourism identifies the dam as under Government of India protection. Its drainage channels and stone structure are part of a historic water-management work, so drain alteration or landscaping should not be treated solely as a municipal beautification job. [7][8]

The Ancient Monuments and Archaeological Sites and Remains Act provides a prohibited area ordinarily extending 100 metres from the protected limits of a centrally protected monument or site. The regulated area ordinarily extends another 200 metres beyond that prohibited area. Distances begin at the protected boundary, not a visitor gate or the centre of a water tank. Site-specific notifications and heritage bye-laws must also be checked. [9]

New construction is generally prohibited in the prohibited area. Applications for eligible repair or renovation there, and for construction, reconstruction, repair or renovation in the regulated area, follow the statutory competent-authority and National Monuments Authority process. A municipal sanction or tourism-department permission is not an archaeological clearance. Before work near Surajkund or Anangpur Dam, confirm the protected limits with ASI and check the lake, forest and planning controls independently.

## Yamuna floodplain and drain mitigation

Haryana's 4 December 2023 status report in NGT Original Application No. 06 of 2012 identifies Budhiya Nala and Gaunchi Drain as major wastewater routes affecting the Yamuna. Its abstract gives the following historical quantities:

| Drain and reporting area | Treated effluent | Untreated effluent | Total |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Budhiya Nala, Faridabad | 25 MLD | 68 MLD | 93 MLD |
| Gaunchi Drain, Ballabgarh | 8 MLD | 98 MLD | 106 MLD |
| Gaunchi Drain, Palwal, separately listed | 22 MLD | 0 MLD | 22 MLD |

These are dated state submissions, not measured 2026 flows. The report's detailed Gaunchi source schedule totals untreated Ballabgarh flow at 98.5 MLD, while its abstract rounds it to 98 MLD. Its wider action plan listed the 80 MLD Mirzapur and 100 MLD Pratapgarh plants as under construction at 80 percent progress, with a 31 December 2023 target. Current commissioning and compliance require later evidence. [10]

The report also identifies monthly monitoring of drains and the river, including Budhiya Nala near Manjhawali. Useful parameters include biochemical oxygen demand, dissolved oxygen and faecal coliform. Comparing river samples upstream and downstream of an outfall helps distinguish incoming river pollution from the additional local load. [10]

Drain mitigation requires sewer interception and conveyance to functioning treatment capacity, control of industrial discharges, and checks for untreated bypasses. Desilting alone does not treat sewage. Monsoon flood mitigation additionally needs clear drainage paths, removal of dumped material, functioning pumping arrangements and protection of flood conveyance space.

Do not infer a legal floodplain boundary from a visible riverbank, a sector boundary or the distance to a drain. For land development, obtain the applicable delineation and written position from the Irrigation and Water Resources Department and planning authority, together with relevant NGT and court orders. This record does not establish a universal Faridabad setback distance or certify any parcel as outside the floodplain.

## AQI monitoring and GRAP

CPCB's all-India continuous monitoring station list names four HSPCB stations in Faridabad: New Industrial Town, Sector 11, Sector 30 and Sector 16A. Nathu Colony is separately listed under Ballabgarh. A station reading should retain its location, observation time and data-availability status rather than being labelled as a uniform reading for every neighbourhood. [11]

The CAQM-hosted Annual Action Plan for Faridabad records four continuous and two manual stations as of 31 December 2025. It includes 2026 reduction targets. A plan target is not a measured improvement or proof that national ambient standards have been achieved. [12]

The CAQM GRAP schedule revised on 21 November 2025 uses Delhi AQI thresholds for NCR-wide staged action:

| Stage | Classification | Delhi AQI |
| --- | --- | --- |
| I | Poor | 201 to 300 |
| II | Very poor | 301 to 400 |
| III | Severe | 401 to 450 |
| IV | Severe plus | Above 450 |

CAQM may invoke stages in advance on forecasts. Higher stages add to earlier-stage measures. Faridabad's own AQI does not independently establish which stage is legally in force. Consult the latest CAQM invocation or revocation order and applicable Haryana implementation directions before travel, construction or school decisions. [13]

The cited schedule assigns road sweeping, sprinkling, dust containment and construction-waste controls to municipal and other responsible agencies. Stage III includes restrictions on specified dust-generating construction activities, closure of stone crushers and mining activities, and specified vehicle restrictions covering Faridabad. Exceptions and later amendments must be checked in the operative text. No stage is asserted to be active on this record's compilation date.

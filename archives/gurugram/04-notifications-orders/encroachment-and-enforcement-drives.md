# Gurugram enforcement powers and encroachment drives, 2024 to 2026

## Record status and jurisdiction

This is a compiled enforcement-order reference, not a reproduction of one signed MCG order. The date field is the publication date of the latest encroachment-drive report included below, 18 August 2026. Individual inspection, notice and drive dates appear separately. News reports establish reported action, not the legal validity of each action or the current status of a property.

MCG acts through municipal enforcement, town-planning, street-vending, health and tax teams. Gurugram Metropolitan Development Authority, or GMDA, Haryana Shahari Vikas Pradhikaran, or HSVP, the Department of Town and Country Planning, or DTCP, and police also participate. A GMDA-led operation is not automatically an MCG order. Ownership of the land, road or green belt and the applicable statute matter.

The official Haryana Urban Local Bodies copy of the Haryana Municipal Corporation Act, 1994 is the principal statutory reference [1]. Its original enactment received presidential assent on 20 October 1994 and was published on 22 October 1994. Subsequent amendments and court directions must also be checked. The archive does not infer ward numbers from market names.

## Building demolition, stoppage and sealing powers

Chapter XIV of the Haryana Municipal Corporation Act, 1994 governs building regulation. Sections 250 and 254 concern construction sanction. Section 261 is titled "Order of demolition and stoppage of building and works in certain cases and appeal". It addresses buildings or works undertaken without sanction or contrary to the applicable requirements. The process includes an opportunity to show cause before a demolition order. Section 262 concerns stoppage of building work, and Section 263 concerns required alterations [1].

Building sealing has a separate provision, Section 263-A. It should not be described simply as a Section 261 sealing power. In *Krishan Lal Gera v. State of Haryana*, decided on 28 February 2017, the Punjab and Haryana High Court identified Sections 261, 262, 263 and 263-A as distinct municipal remedies [2]. Dangerous-building action under Section 265 is another separate route.

A 16 May 2026 report concerned a 1.4-acre Chakkarpur parcel where MCG had issued a demolition order. The report identified an appeal under Section 261(2) before the commissioner, Gurugram division. The Supreme Court questioned reliance on private arbitration to postpone statutory action against allegedly unauthorised structures [3]. This case is evidence of a reported Section 261 proceeding, not a general authorisation to demolish without notice.

For an affected property, obtain the show-cause notice, hearing record, reasoned order, applicable delegation and any appeal or stay. Do not apply a short deadline quoted in an older statute or a market-drive report without checking later court directions and the actual notice. Building enforcement, recovery of tax and removal of public-road encroachments are different legal processes.

## Property sealing for tax default

Tax sealing is a revenue-recovery measure, not proof of unauthorised construction. On 16 September 2025, reported the next day, MCG's Zone 1 tax team sealed five properties after outstanding dues remained unpaid despite reminders. The reported liabilities ranged from Rs 17,24,867 to Rs 3,34,78,864. Sites included Bestech Cyber Park in Narsinghpur and an industrial/commercial property in Kadipur [12].

On 28 November 2025, the tax branch sealed the Pind Balluchi restaurant property in Sector 29 over reported dues of Rs 50,44,901 and Saicom Limited premises in Sector 16 over Rs 75,46,116. The Indian Express attributed the action to the Haryana Municipal Corporation Act and repeated non-compliance with notices [13].

These reports do not supply the signed recovery orders or establish that the properties remained sealed on the verification date. An owner should obtain the property ID assessment, arrears statement and recovery notices from the Tax Branch, and use the official property-tax correction and payment process. Payment of property tax does not itself sanction a building, regularise land use or confer a vending entitlement. Do not remove an official seal without the competent authority's written permission.

## Illegal meat shops, dairies and cattle shifting

A directive reported on 30 August 2025 required chicken, fish and mutton shops to hold valid MCG licences. MCG commissioner Pradeep Dahiya warned of sealing for unauthorised operation or failed hygiene checks. Reported requirements included cold storage, daily cleaning, scientific waste disposal, glass covering at the shopfront, no uncovered display, procurement from authorised slaughterhouses, and display of jhatka/halal status and procurement source [14]. The signed circular and a uniform fine schedule were not obtained, so this record does not invent a licence fee or sealing penalty.

On 5 November 2024, MCG's additional commissioner Subhita Dhaka was reported to have directed notices to illegal dairy owners and penalties of Rs 5,000 per animal released onto roads, rising to Rs 10,000 for repeat offences. Disconnection of water was described as a proposal, not a completed or universally applicable rule [15].

The August 2025 cattle campaign set a target of catching at least 100 cattle daily for one month and shifting them to gaushalas with police coordination. MCG warned that obstruction of cattle-catching teams could lead to FIRs [16]. A February 2026 report, citing municipal data, recorded 2,433 cattle shifted during January to December 2025 and 24 challans totalling Rs 2.78 lakh [17].

These sources support notices to illegal dairies, action against owners who release cattle and shifting of stray cattle to shelters. They do not establish a completed citywide relocation scheme for every dairy, a notified dairy-colony allotment rule or a right to reclaim impounded cattle merely by paying a quoted historical penalty.

## Street vending zones and livelihood safeguards

Street vending must be read alongside the Street Vendors (Protection of Livelihood and Regulation of Street Vending) Act, 2014. A Certificate of Vending, or CoV, its stationary/mobile classification, the authorised site and the Town Vending Committee's decisions matter. A PM SVANidhi Letter of Recommendation, or LoR, is not automatically an allotment of any chosen road or footpath.

The Hindu's 10 December 2024 report recorded competing accounts. Vendors holding CoVs or LoRs said they had been displaced at Holi Ground near Sadar Bazar. The district nodal officer said valid CoV holders were not being targeted and LoR holders were being given 10 to 15 days to obtain CoVs. MCG's city project officer reported 148 vending zones identified in 2016 to 2018, about 2,500 CoVs and 49,000 LoRs. These were historical reported figures, not a verified 2026 zone inventory [18].

On 2 February 2025, reported 3 February, MCG discussed relocation of 41 Sector 14 vendors whose vending zones had been disturbed. A special committee included the joint commissioner, chief planning officer, RS Batth, HSVP and vendor representatives. The meeting also approved 33,198 LoRs and proposed identifying additional vending zones [19].

At the February 2025 Sadar Bazar operation, officials said vendors with valid LoRs or final permissions would not be disturbed if their carts did not obstruct traffic [6]. This reported assurance differs from the December account and must not be treated as a blanket exemption. Seek the current written CoV, site permission, applicable zone map and any relocation order. No authenticated, comprehensive Sector 14/29/56 vending-zone map was obtained for this record.

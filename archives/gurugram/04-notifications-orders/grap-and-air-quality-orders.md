# GRAP and air quality orders in Gurugram, 2024 to 2026

## Scope and legal status

This is an archival consolidation, not a separately issued MCG order. The primary source is CAQM's GRAP schedule revised on 29 September 2026. Historical enforcement figures below come from dated reports quoting municipal officials or municipal data. They are not a current fleet inventory or a statutory penalty schedule.

Gurugram falls within the National Capital Region. CAQM invokes and revokes GRAP stages for the NCR; MCG implements municipal measures, while HSPCB carries out pollution-control inspections and enforcement alongside other responsible agencies. GMDA and other road-owning bodies retain responsibilities for their own assets.

The schedule uses Delhi's AQI and forecasts, not a separate Gurugram AQI trigger. Measures can be invoked before the numerical threshold is reached. Higher stages add to lower-stage measures. A local AQI improvement does not itself cancel an NCR order. Check the [CAQM GRAP orders register](https://caqm.nic.in/landingpage/pages/grap/grap-orders) for the applicable invocation or revocation. This record does not certify which stage is active on the verification date.

## Stages I to IV and municipal measures

| Stage | Delhi AQI band in the September 2026 schedule | Municipal enforcement relevant to Gurugram |
| --- | --- | --- |
| I, Poor | 201 to 300 | Enforce dust-control and C&D waste rules, prevent open waste burning, remove accumulated waste and road dust, and inspect pollution sources. |
| II, Very poor | 301 to 400 | Intensify mechanical or vacuum sweeping and water sprinkling, particularly at dust hotspots and busy roads. Continue lower-stage measures and construction-site checks. |
| III, Severe | 401 to 450 | Restrict the specified dust-generating construction and demolition operations across NCR, subject to the schedule's project exemptions. Continue sweeping, sprinkling and enforcement. |
| IV, Severe plus | Above 450 | Extend the Stage III C&D restrictions to linear public projects such as highways, roads, flyovers, overbridges, power transmission, pipelines and telecommunications. Continue earlier-stage measures. |

The bands must be read with the applicable order and court directions. For example, CAQM's 27 December 2024 release recorded that, under the then applicable Supreme Court orders, Stages III and IV kicked in at Delhi AQI levels of 350 and 400 respectively. Those historical trigger arrangements should not be substituted for the September 2026 schedule.

Mechanical sweeping must be accompanied by removal and proper disposal of collected dust. Sprinkling alone does not resolve accumulated C&D waste or municipal garbage. MCG deployment reports describe treated sewage-treatment-plant water used in tankers and anti-smog guns.

## Construction bans and dust-control duties

Under Stage III of the September 2026 schedule, prohibited dust-generating activities include excavation, filling, boring, drilling, piling, demolition, open-trench utility work, masonry, RMC batching, major welding and gas cutting, painting, polishing and varnishing. Restrictions also cover cement and plaster coatings, cutting and fixing flooring materials, road construction and major repairs, loading or unloading dusty materials, and movement of construction-material or C&D-waste vehicles on unpaved roads.

The schedule permits specified minor indoor repairs and maintenance, minor welding for mechanical, electrical and plumbing works, and pothole repairs. Other less-polluting construction activities may continue only with strict compliance with waste-management and dust-control requirements.

Stage III project exemptions include railway and metro projects, airports and inter-state bus terminals, national-security and defence projects, hospitals, specified linear public projects, sanitation and water-supply projects, and associated ancillary work. Exemption does not waive dust-control obligations. Stage IV additionally restricts the linear public projects identified in its construction clause. The actual project and activity must be checked against the operative schedule, rather than treating all public construction as exempt.

At operating sites, cover loose material and loads, prevent debris dumping, and use required dust suppression. An anti-smog gun does not authorize work prohibited under an invoked stage. CAQM's 27 December 2024 release also states that sites with specific closure orders cannot restart merely because GRAP restrictions are relaxed; a specific reopening order is required.

## Challans and open-burning penalties

MCG challans cover illegal debris dumping, uncovered transport or storage, waste burning and construction contrary to active restrictions. HSPCB inspections and environmental enforcement are separate from routine municipal sanitation challans.

The Rs 5,000 waste-burning fine reported in November 2024 and the December 2025 aggregate of Rs 1.40 lakh across 28 waste-burning cases are dated examples. They should not be read as a universal maximum penalty, an exhaustive tariff or permission to burn waste on payment. Likewise, aggregate C&D fines cannot establish a fixed charge per site. The challan or environmental-compensation order must identify the applicable provision and assessment.

Open garbage burning should be reported even when Stage III or IV is not active. Dust-control and waste-management duties do not begin only with a construction ban.

## Citizen reporting through Sameer and MCG

Use CPCB's Sameer app to report air-polluting activities and view AQI information. CPCB describes complaint submission using photographs, descriptions and location information, with routing to pollution-control boards or other authorized agencies. The [CPCB Delhi-NCR complaint form](https://cpcb.nic.in/query-form1.php) also lists open or garbage burning, construction and demolition activity, road dust, leaf burning and open dumping among its categories.

For MCG matters, call **1800-180-1817**, published on the municipal portal as **18001801817**, or use the [MCG grievance page](https://mcg.gov.in/grievance). The November 2024 municipal enforcement report specifically asked residents to report violations to this number.

Provide the sector or colony, nearest landmark, exact site location, date and time, nature of the activity, and a photograph where safe. For construction during a ban, identify the project rather than assuming every visible activity is prohibited. Keep the complaint reference for follow-up. These are reporting channels, not a guarantee of immediate inspection or a verified response deadline.

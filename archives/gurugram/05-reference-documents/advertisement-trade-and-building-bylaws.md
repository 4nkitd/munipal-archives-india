# Advertisement, trade licensing and building approval rules in Gurugram

## Scope and legal instruments

This record covers premises within Municipal Corporation Gurugram jurisdiction. A Gurugram district address does not by itself establish that MCG is the competent municipality or building authority. Confirm municipal limits, land-use permission and the authority responsible for the site before applying.

| Subject | Principal source | Date context |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Outdoor advertisements | Haryana Municipal Advertisement Bye-laws, 2022, notification No. 9/24/2022-4C-II | Notification dated 15 July 2022; DULB library entry dated 18 July 2022 |
| Municipal trade permissions | Haryana Municipal Corporation Act, 1994, particularly sections 330, 331, 335 and 336 | Apply the relevant section and schedules, not a generic licence label |
| Building plans and occupation | Haryana Building Code, 2017, as amended | Code states statewide applicability from 6 January 2017 |
| Fire safety | Applicable fire legislation, building code and fire-service approval requirements | DULB separately lists the Fire and Emergency Services Act, 2022 |

The frontmatter date identifies the advertisement notification, not the enactment date of every instrument in this compilation. This is a source-based reference, not a consolidated reproduction of all subsequent amendments.

## Advertisement bye-laws, unipoles and hoardings

The 2022 bye-laws apply to municipalities across Haryana. They regulate outdoor media devices, or OMDs, including billboards or hoardings, unipoles, electronic hoardings and other display formats. A unipole is an advertising panel supported on a single column with its foundation in the ground. A hoarding includes an advertising board and its supporting structure on land or a building.

The regulations distinguish self-advertisement, third-party advertising, property listing and registration of advertising entities. Ownership of the building or a private lease does not itself grant municipal advertising rights. Determine whether a proposed display is exempt, falls within permitted self-advertisement limits, or needs municipal permission and portal listing. Schedule I and the device typologies must be checked for the particular installation.

The [Haryana e-advertisement portal](https://ulb.project247.in/) supports registration of advertising agencies, publication of sites, self-advertisement applications, auction participation, earnest-money payments and payments against awarded sites. Its entity-registration manual describes municipal approval of registration before the entity can use the approved account. Registration is not a site-specific approval.

For a unipole, hoarding or digital display, verify the approved site, permitted dimensions, structural requirements, road-safety restrictions, display tenure and payment conditions before installation. Do not infer permission for an LED screen from permission for a static panel. Retain the approval and any auction allotment with the property and structure records.

## Advertisement amendments and source limitations

DULB's town-planning policy index lists an advertisement amendment under 1 October 2025. The linked official Gazette text located during research is notification No. 9/100/2025-4CII dated 25 September 2025, explicitly published as draft bye-laws for objections or suggestions. Its proposed changes include OMD clusters, auction eligibility, identification plates and revised site-selection rules.

That draft is not evidence that each proposal became operative in the same form. Reports published in December 2025 describe a final amendment notified on 18 December 2025, but the final official Gazette text was not verified for this record. Accordingly, this record does not present draft dimensions, distances, auction conditions or penalties as current law. Obtain the final amendment and current municipal conditions before designing or bidding for a site.

Likewise, the portal manual's historical registration amount should not be treated as the current fee schedule. DULB's town-planning compendium indexes advertisement fee orders dated 21 September 2022, 21 November 2022 and 7 April 2023. Use the applicable notified schedule and the actual portal demand.

## Trade licences and health-related premises

MCG licensing derives from the Haryana Municipal Corporation Act, 1994. The relevant provisions address different activities.

| Provision | Subject |
| --- | --- |
| Section 330 | Permission to establish a factory, workshop or trade premises using mechanical power, and relevant alterations or enlargement |
| Section 331 | Use of premises for specified purposes and storage of scheduled articles; read with the Second Schedule |
| Section 335 | Eating houses and related food or lodging premises requiring the Commissioner's licence |
| Section 336 | Licensing and control of theatres, circuses and places of public amusement |

DULB's published "Issue of new trade license" checklist names the Zonal Taxation Officer as designated officer and gives a 15-day service timeline after submission. It lists a fee of Rs. 100, but its procedure also requires the verifier to assess fees and notify the applicant for payment. Do not read Rs. 100 as the complete annual licence charge for every business category.

For an individual, the checklist requires Family ID or Parivar Pehchan Patra and rent, lease or ownership proof. For a firm, it lists applicant identity proof and rent, lease or ownership proof. Applications may be filed through [Antyodaya SARAL](https://saralharyana.gov.in/) or a CSC/SARAL Kendra. The verifier checks documents, raises deficiencies if necessary and communicates the payment demand. Payment is directed to [ULB online services](https://online.ulbharyana.gov.in/); the approver then considers the application and the licence is issued online after approval.

MCG's older combined application form separately covers sections 330, 331, and 335/336. For food and hospitality premises, it asks for a fire NOC, Civil Surgeon NOC, occupation certificate for the commercial building or hotel and a lease agreement where relevant. This explains the health-clearance connection, but the form contains turnover years 2008-09 to 2010-11 and references superseded waste rules. It is historical evidence, not a reliable current upload checklist or fee schedule. Confirm category-specific health requirements with MCG. A municipal licence does not establish building-use legality or replace applicable food-safety registration.

## Building plan approval and Invest Haryana

The Haryana Building Code, 2017 governs building proposals subject to amendments and applicable land-use conditions. DULB's building-plan page directs industrial and commercial applicants to [Invest Haryana](https://investharyana.in/). It directs residential and institutional applicants to [HOBPAS ULB portal](https://haryanabpas.gov.in/ULBPORTAL/). Invest Haryana is therefore not the published route for every building category.

The published checklist includes Forms BR-I and BR-II, plans, sections and elevations, ownership documents, property-tax receipt, site plan, site report and change-of-land-use permission where applicable. It lists scrutiny fees at Rs. 10 per square metre of covered area, development charges where applicable and composition charges at occupation-certificate stage under the relevant policy.

Required external clearances depend on the site and proposal. The DULB list includes road-access permission, pollution-control consent, fire clearance, airport-height clearance, monument-related permission, water and sewerage, environmental clearance and HAREDA requirements where applicable.

DULB's service page and the archived amended code use different summaries of risk categories. Do not determine self-certification eligibility from the abbreviated webpage alone. The architect and competent authority should use the applicable amended code. The published allocation also distinguishes old municipal limits, extended limits, CLU status and plot size. A project within Gurugram is not automatically sanctioned by MCG alone.

## Occupation certificate and fire NOC coordination

Building-plan sanction permits approved construction. It is not an occupation certificate. Code 4.10 requires an occupation-certificate application with the relevant completion certifications, as-built drawings, photographs and applicable supporting clearances. It identifies Forms BR-IV(A) or BR-IV(B), completion certification in BR-VI and an occupation certificate in BR-VII for the ordinary route. Code 4.11 provides a separate self-certification route for eligible buildings using BRS forms.

For industrial and commercial projects, use the applicable occupation-certificate service in the Invest Haryana workflow and confirm the responsible department. Retain the original building sanction, approved revisions, completion drawings and compliance documents. Do not apply the ordinary route's forms or timelines automatically to a self-certification case.

Fire coordination occurs at more than one stage. Code 2.1 calls for fire-safety design where applicable at building-plan stage. Code 4.10 lists the fire-safety NOC from the concerned Chief Fire Officer or authorised officer with occupation-certificate documentation. A design approval does not establish that the completed premises have received the required fire NOC. Trade or health-related licensing may also require evidence of fire compliance.

The archived code contains deemed-approval and self-certification provisions with conditions. This record does not certify that a particular project qualifies. Confirm the applicable amended provision, complete application status, deviations, inspection requirements and fire clearance before occupation.

# Bandhwari landfill and Gurugram sanitation tenders, 2024 to 2026

This is a procurement and oversight record, not a live tender notice. The frontmatter date is the publication date of the Bandhwari Phase XIII bid notice. MT means metric tonnes and TPD means tonnes per day. One lakh equals 100,000.

## Bandhwari biomining and National Green Tribunal monitoring

Bandhwari received municipal solid waste from Gurugram and Faridabad. In its [March 2026 filing to the National Green Tribunal (NGT)](https://www.greentribunal.gov.in/sites/default/files/news_updates/Report%20by%20CPCB%20in%20OA%20No%20172%20of%202021%20Poonam%20yadav%20Vs%20Ms%20Ecogreen%20Energy%20Pvt%20Ltd%20and%20Ors%20WITH%20OA%20No%20109%20of%202022%20WITH%20OA%20No%20221%20of%202025.pdf), the Central Pollution Control Board (CPCB) recorded MCG's estimate of 2,200 TPD of fresh dumping, split between Gurugram at 1,200 TPD and Faridabad at 1,000 TPD. MCG subsequently [reported to the NGT](https://www.hindustantimes.com/cities/gurugram-news/bandhwari-landfill-faces-710k-mt-waste-backlog-by-mar-2027-mcg-tells-ngt-101787201535493.html) that Faridabad stopped sending fresh waste on 25 June 2026. Gurugram's fresh waste continued to add to the backlog.

Biomining separates refuse-derived fuel (RDF), soil-like material, inert material and construction debris. MCG's April 2025 affidavit reported dispatch of RDF to cement plants and other authorised users. A [joint CPCB and Union environment ministry inspection on 27 August 2025](https://www.greentribunal.gov.in/sites/default/files/news_updates/Inspection%20Report%20by%20CPCB%20in%20OA%20No%20172%20of%202021%20(Poonam%20yadav%20Vs.%20Ecogreen%20Energy%20Pvt.%20Ltd.%20&%20Ors.).pdf) found leachate flowing into adjoining plantation or forest land, no perimeter collection drain and no on-site treatment. The CPCB's 17 February 2026 inspection found no completed fire risk assessment or on-site emergency plan. These inspection findings are distinct from MCG's reported processing totals.

## Ecogreen termination and interim collection

The 14 August 2017 integrated concession covered waste collection, transport, processing and disposal for the Gurugram and Faridabad cluster. According to the [23 April 2025 GMDA minutes, item 5.2](https://gmda.gov.in/download.html?code=generalResource&fid=4de6f715-4aeb-47b8-8ae9-f0567ad2383d&identifier=1751513430967&key=attachment), MCG issued its first final termination letter on 15 June 2024. Ecogreen challenged it and the Punjab and Haryana High Court required fresh notice because the mandatory two months had not been given. MCG issued the replacement notice on 15 January 2025 and told GMDA the agreement ended on **17 March 2025**. The minutes record arbitration as pending at that meeting. June 2024 marks the operational handover, not the effective termination date in those minutes.

MCG gave Bimalraj Outsourcing the primary doorstep collection work from 18 June 2024. The one-year contract was [not extended after 17 June 2025](https://timesofindia.indiatimes.com/city/gurgaon/stopgap-measures-fall-short-waste-pickup-faces-setback-in-gurgaon/articleshow/124219985.cms). Secondary haulage was a separate job: [R.S. Enterprises covered Zones 1, 3 and 4 and Lord Shiva Enterprises covered Zone 2](https://timesofindia.indiatimes.com/city/gurgaon/mcg-ends-contract-with-ecogreen-3-companies-to-collect-waste-take-it-to-bandhwari/articleshow/111028607.cms) under contracts already in place by June 2024.

The next four-zone, six-month doorstep collection arrangement began on [9 January 2026](https://www.hindustantimes.com/cities/others/four-private-agencies-to-pick-up-household-waste-now-in-gurugram-101767898539781.html). The following assignments were [reported on 10 March 2026](https://www.hindustantimes.com/cities/gurugram-news/haryana-orders-pay-by-weight-model-for-door-to-door-waste-collection-101773106747392.html). They are not a current service directory.

## Long-term collection and secondary transfer points

The earlier five-year procurement was cancelled after rate negotiations. For example, the [22 April 2026 Cluster II entry, 2026_HRY_516956_1](https://etenders.hry.nic.in/nicgep/app?component=%24DirectLink&page=FrontEndViewTender&service=direct&sp=SposVlLQYJpe4ASSgg67VIw%3D%3D), lists a cancellation corrigendum. After retendering at an [estimated ₹647.78 crore](https://www.hindustantimes.com/cities/gurugram-news/gurugram-finally-gets-long-term-garbage-collection-agencies-after-months-of-delays-101787460559243.html), Haryana's [26 October 2026 release](https://prms.prharyana.gov.in/press-release/9909) confirmed Delhi MSW Solutions Ltd. for Cluster 1 and BVG India Ltd. for Cluster 2, for five years with a possible two-year extension. The planned fleet was 875 vehicles: 675 light commercial vehicles, 184 e-rickshaws and 16 compactors. The planned 28 October and 15 November rollout dates passed without service starting. On [19 November](https://timesofindia.indiatimes.com/city/gurgaon/new-doorstep-waste-pickup-plan-put-off-again-in-gurgaon-mcg-eyes-dec-end-rollout-875-vehicle-collection-fleet-yet-to-be-deployed-agencies-wait-essential-facilities/articleshow/136001955.cms), MCG cited pending transfer points and charging facilities, with interim collection still in place.

Secondary collection points receive waste from doorstep vehicles before onward haulage. MCG's [17 October 2025 model RFP, clauses 4.2.23 to 4.2.25](https://mcg.gov.in/PDF/Model_RFP_MCG.pdf), called for fixed or mobile compactors, direct mechanical transfer and same-day onward transport. The model assigns basic infrastructure to MCG and transfer machinery to the contractor. This is a design requirement, not proof of installation. In August 2026 officials [reported 12 of 14 points operating](https://www.hindustantimes.com/cities/delhi-news/garbage-piles-up-at-gurugram-waste-points-amid-delay-in-lifting-101786990186245.html), with Prem Puri and the Tulip Chowk site in Sector 70 closed. On 17 November the Finance and Contract Committee [approved a ₹3,53,20,000 proposal](https://www.hindustantimes.com/cities/gurugram-news/municipal-corporation-of-gurugram-approves-development-projects-worth-rs-61-crore-101794943158131.html) for a new point near Hero Honda Chowk on NH-48.

## Charcoal and waste-to-energy plans

The Ecogreen concession included a waste-to-energy plant at Bandhwari that did not enter service. MCG's April 2025 affidavit records a 20 July 2024 memorandum with NTPC Vidyut Vyapar Nigam Ltd. for torrefied charcoal facilities sized at 1,500 TPD for Gurugram and 1,200 TPD for Faridabad. The affidavit proposed commissioning in 2027. The [NGT's 15 April 2025 order, as reported by The Hindu](https://www.thehindu.com/news/cities/Delhi/ngt-pulls-up-authorities-over-management-of-waste-at-bandhwari-landfill-in-gurugram/article69480133.ece), questioned the change from the earlier waste-to-energy plan and asked the Haryana State Pollution Control Board to disclose environmental compensation levied and recovered.

On [29 August 2026 Haryana announced a separate set of memoranda](https://prms.prharyana.gov.in/press-release/4638) with Oil Green Energy Ltd., an Oil India subsidiary. The proposed integrated waste-to-energy plants would handle 2,000 TPD in the Gurugram cluster at a minimum 20 MW and 1,600 TPD in the Faridabad cluster at a minimum 16 MW. Haryana named October 2028 as the expected operating date for these and two other plants. The announcement does not show either the charcoal facilities or the new power plants in operation.

## Basai construction and demolition waste

Basai handles construction and demolition (C&D) debris separately from household garbage. MCG identified [Indo Enviro Integrated Solutions](https://indianexpress.com/article/cities/delhi/gurgaon-civic-body-official-inspects-cd-waste-plant-8860972/) as its concessionaire for collecting and processing debris into aggregates, manufactured sand and other products. In August 2025 the state [approved an increase in the municipal processing mandate](https://timesofindia.indiatimes.com/city/gurgaon/wait-ends-haryana-govt-clears-plan-to-expand-demolition-waste-plant-in-basai/articleshow/123571775.cms) from 300 to 1,200 TPD. MCG reported a one-year increase in the collection radius from 15 to 30 km and an unchanged transport and processing rate of ₹360 per tonne. Those figures describe the municipal arrangement, not the plant's rated equipment capacity or a fee for every private load.

The earlier citywide enforcement, collection and transport [bid 2024_HRY_352826_1](https://etenders.hry.nic.in/nicgep/app?component=%24DirectLink&page=FrontEndViewTender&service=direct&sp=S7ju%2Ft4gMrzd1F3ISDbejOA%3D%3D), published 9 February 2024, lists a cancellation. On 20 April 2026 MCG [invited bids for seven debris collection points](https://www.hindustantimes.com/cities/gurugram-news/mcg-to-set-up-seven-c-d-waste-collection-points-in-gurugram-101776969125415.html) in Zones 1 and 3, with segregated storage bays and CCTV before transport to Basai. On 17 November the committee [approved proposals for two more processing units](https://timesofindia.indiatimes.com/city/gurgaon/rs-61cr-projects-cleared-by-finance-panel-for-waste-and-pollution-control-in-gurgaon-2-cd-waste-processing-plants-16-road-washers-10-anti-smog-guns-among-approvals/articleshow/135938309.cms), one mobile unit at Biodiversity Park and another at Wazirabad, estimated at ₹6.6 crore each.

# Gurugram roads, streetlights, parks, parking and community centre works, 2024-2026

## Scope and evidence

This is a consolidated research register, not a single notice inviting tender. The frontmatter date is the publication date of the Sector 27 community centre tender described below. Individual entries retain their own dates.

MCG procures internal roadworks, municipal lighting and local park improvements through Haryana eProcurement under the organisation chain `Haryana Government > Urban Local Bodies > MC Gurgaon`. HSVP and GMDA also undertake infrastructure works in Gurugram. A sector number alone does not establish MCG ownership.

Tender details below are identified by their Haryana tender IDs where available. The official deep link for the Sector 27 road tender returned only the eProcurement landing shell during this research. Consequently, notice amounts and schedules transcribed from commercial indexes are labelled secondary evidence. They require confirmation against the original NIT, bill of quantities, corrigenda and award record. A tender publication, administrative approval or forecast opening date does not prove completion.

The frontmatter leaves wards empty because this register spans the municipality and several ward-numbering contexts. Ward numbers quoted in work titles are retained as historical procurement metadata, not mapped to current boundaries.

BM and BC mean bituminous macadam and bituminous concrete. RMC refers to ready-mix concrete. The Sector 27 title distinguishes bituminous stretches from locations requiring M40 concrete; it should not be reduced to a single asphalt resurfacing package.

Do not treat every project discussed at that meeting as approved. Procurement notices marked "recall" also need to be checked for earlier failed or superseded bid rounds.

## LED maintenance and dark-spot elimination

On 9 August 2024, the Times of India reported four proposed two-year zone-wise operation and maintenance packages. Existing contracts were due to expire on 31 October, and new operations were intended to begin on 1 November. The reported package estimates were ₹3.91 crore for Zone 1, ₹4.23 crore for Zone 2, ₹2.55 crore for Zone 3 and ₹3.01 crore for Zone 4. Their sum is ₹13.70 crore. Executive engineer electrical Parveen Dalal was the named official. These are reported tender estimates, not verified final payments or evidence that all four appointments occurred. [5]

The 21 August 2025 repair campaign covered major roads, intersections, residential colonies and public spaces. Reported work included surveys, repair of burnt-out or broken fixtures and replacement of worn poles, wiring and LED fittings. Priority areas included bus stands, markets, railway-station approaches, DLF, Sushant Lok, Palam Vihar and Sector 37. The report quoted MCG's complaint helpline as **18001801817**. This is a historically published contact, not a fresh verification of helpline availability. [6]

"Mission Bright Gurugram", reported on 11 September 2025, began in 11 wards and was to proceed zone-wise. Executive engineer Sachin Yadav described daily inspections and repair or replacement of defective lights. This supports an account of dark-stretch repair activity, but not a claim that all dark spots were eliminated. [7]

For a particular complaint, record the road or park name, nearest property or pole number, failed fitting, missing pole or cable fault, and complaint reference. A new-light installation and an existing-light maintenance job may belong to different work orders.

## Multilevel and surface parking

### Sadar Bazar, Sohna Chowk and the post office site

A 9 December 2024 report described the first MCG multistorey facility serving Sadar Bazar, at Sohna Chowk, as a ₹54 crore project with capacity for 206 cars and 190 two-wheelers. It also reported pending gate and electricity-connection work and said the facility was not operational. The article's references to completed construction should therefore not be read as commissioning. [8]

The separate post-office-site proposal involved acquiring PWD B&R land. On 15 January 2025, the Times of India reported permission for ₹43.66 crore to purchase this land, to be routed from stamp-duty arrears owed to MCG. The stated site area was approximately 3,000 square metres and planned capacity 200 vehicles. The land amount is distinct from the construction cost. [9]

A 29 January 2025 Hindustan Times report cited a ₹48 crore construction tender and approximately ₹43 crore land approval, while also saying the DPR and possession process remained pending. These accounts do not establish a construction start or opening. Their procedural differences should be resolved from land-transfer orders, the approved DPR and the actual work order. [10]

### Kaman Sarai

The January 2025 report described a proposed Kaman Sarai multilevel facility with a multiplex, capacity for 1,200 vehicles and an estimated ₹150 crore cost. These were reported planning figures. [10]

A separate indexed MCG tender, `2025_HRY_454725_1`, published 26 June 2025, concerned interlocking tiles, a boundary wall and high-mast lighting at Kaman Sarai parking in Sadar Bazar. The index gives an estimate of ₹79.14 lakh, closing date 6 July 2025 and work period 240 days. It lists R.S. Enterprises against a ₹63.89 lakh contract dated 3 September 2025. This is a secondary-index award entry, not an independently verified MCG award. Crucially, the title describes surface-parking improvements, not construction of the proposed ₹150 crore multilevel complex. [11]

### Sector 29: HSVP responsibility

HSVP's July 2024 RFP sought a consultant for a vision and master plan, detailed layout, urban design guidelines, integrated infrastructure plan and preliminary engineering design for Sector 29. It describes a designated area of 482 acres and requires consideration of parking, non-motorised transport, electric vehicles and smart roads. The document gives a proposal due date of 12 August 2024. This is a planning consultancy procurement, not an MCG multilevel-parking construction tender. [12]

An official HSVP revised layout identifies multilevel parking sites, including a 2.20-acre site. Layout reservation does not prove construction, award or public opening. Older reports of two HSVP multilevel facilities near Kingdom of Dreams and Iffco Chowk must not be presented as newly commissioned MCG facilities during 2024-2026. No commissioning record for those projects was established in the sources reviewed. [13]

## Parks, open gyms and RWA maintenance

A September 2024 local report counted 1,272 MCG parks and described an existing maintenance rate of ₹4.20 per square metre, with monthly expenditure of ₹1,02,92,746. It reported broken open-gym equipment, swings, benches and grills in six Samaspur parks. These are reported conditions and expenditure figures, not audited accounts. [14]

On 24 June 2025, Hindustan Times described the rollout of a Haryana park-maintenance policy framed in 2022. Registered RWAs, NGOs and local associations could maintain municipal parks not covered by CSR. The reported base assistance was ₹4 per square metre per month, increasing 5% annually, with payment by the seventh after nodal-officer certification. Parks were to remain publicly accessible and municipally owned. Commercialisation, encroachment and restrictions on public access were prohibited. Violations could lead to termination. [15]

The ₹4 base policy rate and the separately reported ₹4.20 existing rate should not be silently substituted for each other. An RWA needs its current sanction, measured park area, applicable annual escalation, agreement and certification requirements to calculate a payable claim.

In July 2025, MCG inspections in Sectors 22, 22B, 23 and 23A found overgrown vegetation, scattered waste and missing maintenance staff. Reported notices required adequate staff, pruning, irrigation, proper waste disposal and compliance within five days. The article body described potential cancellation for non-compliance; it did not establish that every contract named in the headline had already been cancelled. [16]

Horticultural maintenance does not itself demonstrate that open-gym repairs, electrical repairs or major civil reconstruction are included in an RWA agreement. Check each agreement and BOQ for those items.

For a 2026 capital-work example, the secondary index for `2026_HRY_555854_1` records renovation of Braham Kumari park near house 1699, Sector 10A, Ward 10, Zone 1. Publication was 22 October 2026, closing 29 October, estimated value ₹12,98,562, EMD ₹25,972 and work period 120 days. No completion is established here. [17]

## Community centre renovations

Sector 38 illustrates the distinction between a resident request and a sanctioned tender. A 30 December 2024 report described broken doors and windows, damaged washrooms, seepage and plaster deterioration. RWA representatives said they had spent ₹92,000 without reimbursement and referred to ₹25,000 monthly municipal support for security, sanitation and electricity. Executive engineer Sanjeev Kumar said renovation was under consideration; no firm schedule was given. These statements do not constitute a renovation sanction or award. [18]

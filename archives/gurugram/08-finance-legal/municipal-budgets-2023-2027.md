# Municipal Corporation of Gurugram budgets, FY 2023-24 to FY 2026-27

## Annual estimates and approval status

All amounts are in Rs crore. Outlay means estimated expenditure, not estimated income or the sum of the two. The record date is the latest House budget vote covered here. These are MCG figures, not the budgets of the Gurugram Metropolitan Development Authority (GMDA) or Municipal Corporation Manesar.

| Financial year | Estimated income | Estimated expenditure (outlay) | Stage and source |
| --- | ---: | ---: | --- |
| 2023-24 | 2,802.6 | 2,074.6 | Haryana Urban Local Bodies (ULB) Department approval as reported on 26 April 2023. [1] |
| 2024-25 | 1,576.9 | 1,495 | ULB Department approval in March 2024, reported on 16 April. [2] |
| 2025-26 | 1,571 | 1,497 | MCG House vote on 8 April 2025. The ULB Department subsequently returned the submission with objections, as reported on 2 May. [3][4][5] |
| 2026-27 | 1,918 | 1,875 | Estimates approved unanimously by the MCG House on 18 March 2026. [6] |

The March 2026 budget reporting cited a different FY 2025-26 income projection of Rs 1,562.4 crore and the same expenditure estimate of Rs 1,497 crore. The table retains the April 2025 House figures because they have a known approval stage. Separate final state sanction orders were not verified for FY 2025-26 or FY 2026-27. [3][4][5][7]

## Revenue estimates and reported collections

| Receipt head | FY 2023-24 [1] | FY 2024-25 [2] | FY 2025-26 House [3][4] | FY 2026-27 House [6] |
| --- | ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: |
| Property tax | 500 | 300 | 275 | 325 |
| Stamp duty transfer | Not verified | 350 | 500 | 500 |
| Advertisement charges and fees | 85 | 80 | 100 | 120 |
| Building plan approval receipts | Not verified | 10 | 8 | Not verified |
| Development charges | Not verified | 30 (EDC) | 50 (EDC) | 75 (EDC/IDC) |
| Water and sewerage charges | 80 (reported as water charges) | 40 | 50 | 80 |

EDC means external development charges; IDC means infrastructure development charges. Building plan receipts are a separate line. For FY 2025-26, the chief accounts officer told the House that the Haryana Urban Infrastructure Development Board collects and disburses EDC and that MCG did not receive those funds in FY 2024-25. A budgeted transfer is not evidence that the money arrived. [4]

The 2% duty on eligible transfers of immovable property within municipal limits is not a 2% receipt for MCG. Reporting in 2023 and 2026 describes a split in Gurugram: one percentage point for MCG and one for GMDA. When the FY 2026-27 budget was tabled, *The Indian Express* reported that MCG had collected Rs 239 crore of the Rs 500 crore estimated for FY 2025-26. That was a March 2026 report, not an audited year-end total. [8][9][10]

For FY 2023-24, the ULB Department added Rs 225 crore in arrears to MCG's property tax proposal of Rs 275 crore. The commissioner reported collections of Rs 250 crore after year-end. For FY 2024-25, *The Times of India* reported Rs 240 crore, while the state objections reported by *Hindustan* cited Rs 232.93 crore; no audited reconciliation was verified. For FY 2025-26, officials reported more than Rs 350 crore in property tax and about Rs 56 crore in water bills after year-end. These are reported collections, not budget estimates. [1][5][11][12][13]

The advertisement head includes permission fees and auctioned rights for outdoor media such as unipoles. In June 2026 MCG began a cluster-wise e-auction and reported Rs 85.9 crore in advertisement revenue for the previous allotment cycle, against a Rs 100 crore projection. That cycle figure is not presented here as an audited financial-year total. [14][15]

## Sanitation, engineering and establishment

- **FY 2023-24.** *The Times of India* later reported a Rs 570 crore sanitation allocation. It reported a separate Rs 100 crore estimate for environmental projects, including solid waste plants and National Green Tribunal (NGT) work. In June 2023, officials said Haryana ULB had approved Rs 132 crore for hiring firms to treat legacy waste at Bandhwari. That was a project approval, not a separate annual outlay to add to the other heads. [2][16][17]
- **FY 2024-25.** At state approval, *The Times of India* reported Rs 350 crore for health and sanitation, Rs 60 crore for repairs and maintenance, Rs 15 crore for parks and Rs 50 crore for environmental projects including solid waste plants and NGT-directed works. Later reporting used Rs 325 crore for sanitation. The underlying schedules were not available to reconcile that figure with the approval-stage head. [2][16]
- **FY 2025-26.** House reporting put sanitation and solid waste management at Rs 350 crore. The broader health and sanitation figure reported separately was Rs 390 crore, covering work to clear legacy waste at Bandhwari. The House estimates included Rs 80 crore for roads and bridges, Rs 35 crore each for sewerage/drainage and water supply, Rs 40 crore for public lighting and Rs 102 crore for repairs and maintenance. [3][4]
- **FY 2026-27.** The House provided Rs 400 crore for sanitation and solid waste management, including Rs 250 crore for door-to-door collection, transport and processing. PTI, citing the official statement, reported Rs 400 crore for development projects and Rs 250 crore for operations and maintenance. Detailed budget reporting put road construction at Rs 110 crore, sewerage, treatment plants and water infrastructure at Rs 75 crore, and capital works for lighting, traffic signals and CCTV at Rs 60 crore. Maintenance of lighting, signals and CCTV had a separate Rs 25 crore allocation; park maintenance had Rs 30 crore. Establishment expenses, including salaries and pensions, were estimated at Rs 320 crore. These are overlapping totals and subheads, not amounts to add together. [6][7][10][18]

## Audit and the Account Code transition

Section 83 of the Haryana Municipal Corporation Act, 1994 sets out corporation adoption and state approval of the annual estimates. Sections 168 to 172 cover maintenance and audit of municipal accounts, reporting of irregularities and follow-up by the commissioner. The MCG House received the FY 2023-24 audit report on 11 August 2025, according to *Hindustan Times*. Reporting on the Haryana Local Audit Department's findings identified irregularities involving advances to other departments, works awarded on quotations instead of through mandatory bidding, and questions about recovery of Bandhwari subcontract costs. These were audit observations, not findings of criminal liability. A separate CAG performance audit covered solid waste management for an earlier period and should not be read as MCG's annual financial audit. [19][20][21][22]

Haryana notified the Municipal Account Code, 2025 on 20 May 2025; the DULB portal lists both English Gazette parts under 6 June 2025. It replaces the 1930 code with accrual-based double-entry accounting across Haryana municipalities. Accrual accounts record revenue when earned and expenses when incurred, rather than only when cash moves. Local reporting put the switch to annual budgets under the new code at 1 April 2026, and the Haryana Institute of Public Administration listed training for municipal staff in early 2026. The notification and training do not establish that MCG completed migration or published audited accrual accounts. For comparisons across the change, check whether the source reports billing or cash collections, and whether arrears are included. [23][24][25][26]

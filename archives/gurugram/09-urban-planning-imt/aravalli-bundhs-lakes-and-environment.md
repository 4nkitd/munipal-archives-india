# Gurugram urban ecology: Aravalli park, bundhs, wetlands and environmental initiatives

## Scope and institutional responsibility

This is a research synthesis, not a statutory notification or a certificate of present site condition. Its date is the compilation date. Project milestones and survey editions retain their own dates below.

Gurugram's bundhs are embankments associated with rainwater retention, flood moderation and groundwater recharge. Restoration involves their catchments and drains as well as public walking routes. Protecting a trail alone does not establish that the adjoining lake or drainage system has been restored.

MCG and GMDA have different responsibilities. GMDA's report to the National Green Tribunal in O.A. 325/2015 states that it cannot intervene unilaterally in water bodies owned by other agencies. Its Sikanderpur Ghosi and Wazirabad projects began with the consent of MCG and HSVP respectively. Municipalities, panchayats and other owner departments retain responsibilities for their water bodies. The report's district-wide inventory must not be described as an inventory solely of MCG property. [1]

## Aravalli Biodiversity Park

The park occupies a former quarry landscape at the Delhi-Gurugram border on Mehrauli-Gurugram Road. FAO's account, published on 5 April 2022, describes approximately 392 acres of ridges, valleys, seasonal streams and quarry pits. Degraded Nathupur panchayat land transferred to MCG in 2009. The citizen organisation iamgurgaon proposed restoration and subsequently received a formal conservation, native-plant nursery and research assignment for 2012 to 2020. Restoration had already begun before that assignment. [2]

The UNDP and National Biodiversity Authority compendium records the park's inauguration on 5 June 2010. It describes the project as a collaboration involving MCG, iamgurgaon, citizens and corporate supporters, with about 145,000 plants of more than 200 species planted over the reported restoration period. These are historical programme totals, not a current survival census. Published area figures differ: the compendium uses 380 acres in its overview and approximately 392 acres in its geographical description. Neither figure should substitute for a revenue demarcation. [3]

MCG signed an agreement with Hero MotoCorp in April 2021 for conservation and management support. The maintenance handover took place on 1 July 2021, with a ten-year arrangement extending to 2031. Contemporary reporting states that MCG retained park security responsibilities. [2][4]

On 2 February 2022 the park received recognition as India's first Other Effective Area-based Conservation Measure, or OECM, site. OECM recognition is not a national park declaration, a Ramsar designation or an automatic new statutory protection. FAO expressly distinguishes the recognition from legal protection under Indian law. [2][3][5]

## Chakkarpur-Wazirabad and Ghata bundhs

| Site | Documented intervention | Status and limitation |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Chakkarpur-Wazirabad bundh | iamgurgaon's project account describes a 5.2 km corridor restored with the Haryana Forest Department, MCG and the former HUDA. Works included drain clearance, sewage diversion, recharge chambers, trenches, pits and perforated pipes. | The project page says it has been handed over to MCG. Its reported removal of more than 200 truckloads of silt and plastic and planting of over 30,000 native trees and shrubs are implementer-reported outputs, not an independent current audit. [6] |
| Ghata bundh and lake catchment | The implementer describes a late nineteenth-century embankment, approximately five metres high, and a former submergence area of 60 to 100 hectares reduced by development. Proposed work covers catchment clearance, separation of contaminated water, slope stabilisation, native habitat and trails. | The same page calls restoration work in progress and also records handover to MCG. These statements do not establish completion of the entire lake, catchment or public-access programme. [7] |

Ghata's environmental issue also has a tribunal record. In its order of 20 July 2018 in O.A. 325/2015, the NGT required identification and restoration action for water bodies, including attention to demarcation and fencing at Ghata. GMDA constituted a committee on 5 September 2018 with an MCG representative. Its subsequent report states that 826 water bodies or features across 242 district villages were checked against records and ground conditions. The report warns that GIS areas, ownership particulars and restoration categories require further verification before site interventions. [1]

## Sikanderpur watershed and pond restoration

Sikanderpur pond restoration should be distinguished from announcements about a larger second biodiversity park. September 2020 reporting described a joint GMDA-MCG proposal for a park on reclaimed land, while the pond component involved GMDA, iamgurgaon and corporate social responsibility funding. The proposed park's area and programme were not proof of a completed park. [8]

That report cites GMDA's December 2018 concept paper, "Revival and Habitat Eco-restoration of Sikanderpur Ghosi Waterbody." The paper used 1976 Survey of India mapping to describe a gorge between Nathupur bundh and Chakkarpur-Wazirabad bundh, with seasonal runoff moving towards Najafgarh lake. The pond was a convergence point for streams. Catchment restoration, moisture retention and native vegetation were therefore part of the programme, rather than pond beautification alone. [8]

On 7 April 2023, the chief minister inaugurated the rejuvenated Sikanderpur water body. Contemporary reporting gives a project cost of Rs 9 crore and identifies GMDA as the public authority and iamgurgaon as the implementing organisation. GMDA's chief executive reported seven major pond revival projects taken up with NGOs, of which four were completed at that time. These counts describe April 2023, not the present city-wide total. [9]

## Najafgarh jheel and Basai wetland drainage interface

Najafgarh jheel is a transboundary water-management and conservation issue involving Haryana and Delhi. Haryana's State Wetland Authority affidavit dated 29 July 2024 in E.A. 16/2019, O.A. 153/2014, identifies Gurugram's Leg-2 Dharampur drain and Leg-3 Badshahpur drain as major contributors of city wastewater and monsoon runoff to the submergence area. It assigns GMDA the proposed connections of these drains to Najafgarh drain with pumping arrangements. The filing gave target dates of 6 September 2025 for Leg-2 and 31 March 2027 for Leg-3. These were submitted schedules, not completion certificates. [10]

The affidavit proposed consideration of a 75-acre strip along Najafgarh drain for declaration as a water body or wetland, followed by further procedural steps. This must not be recorded as a final notification of the entire jheel. INTACH disputed the limited area and the approach to reducing submergence, as reported in August 2024. State submissions and conservation objections are separate positions in continuing proceedings. [10][11]

Basai is a separate ecological site in the same wider urban drainage region, not another name for Najafgarh jheel. Its Key Biodiversity Areas factsheet, last assessed in 2004, describes a shallow wetland sustained by a breached channel carrying wastewater and reportedly treated sewage from Gurugram's water and sewage works. It identifies closure of that water source, urbanisation and sewage disposal as threats. The factsheet is a historical ecological baseline, not a 2026 wetland-area survey. [12]

Basai's ecological recognition does not establish statutory notification. In January 2018, reporting on the NGT's decision to lift a stay on a construction and demolition waste plant noted that the disputed land had not been identified as a wetland by Haryana. The plant required pollution-control permissions. The project was a C&D waste facility, not a sewage treatment plant. [13]

## Rainwater harvesting mandates and maintenance

On 23 May 2020, MCG announced directions requiring residential buildings, group housing societies, schools, hotels and industrial establishments within its limits to install rainwater harvesting systems before 15 June 2020. That deadline was specific to the 2020 direction. [14]

Reporting on that direction identifies the following Haryana Building Code 2017 provisions:

| Provision | Requirement reported |
| --- | --- |
| Clause 8.1(1) | Roof rainwater harvesting for buildings with rooftop area of 100 square metres or more. |
| Clause 8.1(2) | Groundwater recharge for all building types, including group housing, at the stated 500-square-metre plot threshold. |

Roof area and plot area are different tests. They should not be converted into a single "100 square yards" rule. August 2025 reporting of district administration instructions restated a rooftop threshold of 100 square metres and a plot threshold of 500 square metres or larger. It also called for MCG, GMDA and other agencies to assist inspection and restoration of non-functional systems. [14][15]

MCG reiterated functional rainwater harvesting requirements for societies in June 2025. Installation alone does not demonstrate operation. The cited official joint committee report on rainwater harvesting stresses exclusion of contaminated water, filtration, maintenance and certification of functionality. Current approvals should be checked against the applicable amended code and competent authority's requirements. [16][17]

## Swachh Survekshan results, 2023 to 2025

| Survey edition | Result announcement | Gurugram result and comparison basis |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Swachh Survekshan 2023 | 11 January 2024 | Rank 140 among 446 urban local bodies with populations above 100,000. Reporting gives 5,209.01 points. [18] |
| Swachh Survekshan 2024-25 | 17 July 2025 | Rank 41 in the population category of 3 lakh to 10 lakh. Reporting also describes Gurugram as seventh in Haryana. [19][20] |

The 2023 edition is not a separate 2024 survey merely because its results were released in 2024. The later edition is named 2024-25; these sources do not establish separate annual ranks for both 2024 and 2025. Population categories changed, so the numerical movement from 140 to 41 is not a like-for-like comparison.

The Indian Express reported 98% waste processing, 59% door-to-door collection and 10% source segregation for the 2024-25 assessment, along with Water+ certification and no Garbage-Free City star rating. These are reported assessment indicators. They do not certify that every drain, pond or public place was clean. [19]

## Urban pond rejuvenation and evidence limits

Individual ponds need separate records for ownership, catchment, sewage interception, desilting, water quality and maintenance. A walking track or inauguration is not evidence that sewage contamination has ended.

Basai village's approximately 4.1-acre municipal pond illustrates the distinction. June 2024 reporting says MCG developed it in 2017 with a walking track, but fish deaths subsequently prompted an investigation. Residents alleged sewage overflow; MCG had not established the cause in that report. This pond should not be conflated with the wider Basai wetland. [21]

At a November 2026 inter-agency meeting, MCG reported developing 28 ponds in the preceding ten months and proposed another 28 before the next monsoon. GMDA discussed deepening the existing eight-acre Wazirabad reservoir and expanding it by another 10 to 12 acres, and excavating the Ghata reservoir. These are reported agency statements and proposals as of 18 November 2026. They are not an independently verified inventory or proof of completion of the proposed works. [22]

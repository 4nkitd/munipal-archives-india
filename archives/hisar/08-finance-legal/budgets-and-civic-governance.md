# Hisar municipal budgets and civic financial governance, FY 2024-25 to 2026-27

## Coverage and evidence limits

This record covers the three financial years beginning on 1 April 2024, 1 April 2025 and 1 April 2026. It does not describe an FY 2027-28 budget. Municipal totals below come from attributed newspaper reports of budget preparation and House meetings. Signed municipal budget schedules, government sanction orders and audited outturns for these years were not located in the sources reviewed.

The official Haryana Finance Department publication separately establishes Hisar's FY 2024-25 Central Finance Commission allocation. That allocation is a component of municipal financing, not the corporation's entire budget. Neither a budget estimate nor a House resolution proves that money was received, a contract awarded or a work completed.

| 2026-27 | Rs 498 crore annual budget approved by the House, reported 5 March 2026 | The Tribune identifies Mayor Praveen Popli and Municipal Commissioner Neeraj. A corporation spokesperson separately gives expected next-year income of about Rs 373 crore and spending of Rs 471 crore. |

The Rs 498 crore headline, Rs 373 crore income estimate and Rs 471 crore spending estimate are retained as separate reported figures. The article does not supply the opening balance, closing balance or accounting schedules needed to reconcile them.

## FY 2025-26 proposals and civic priorities

An 8 April 2025 pre-meeting report in Amar Ujala described Rs 248 crore in proposed receipts and Rs 117 crore opening balance against Rs 365 crore proposed expenditure. Its proposed receipt heads included property tax of Rs 25 crore, registry-related income of Rs 22 crore, electricity duty of Rs 20 crore, development charges of Rs 7 crore, fire tax of Rs 1.50 crore and Improvement Trust shop sales of Rs 52 crore. These are advance estimates, not certified collections or a substitute for the approved line-item schedule.

Haribhoomi's 9 April account identified about Rs 190 crore for development works and Rs 7.70 crore for animal-related work, split into Rs 2 crore for catching monkeys, Rs 5 crore for cattle and Rs 70 lakh for dog vaccination. The same report put preceding-year aggregate income at Rs 248 crore. Readers should not merge that retrospective income figure with the pre-meeting forecast solely because the amounts match.

Amar Ujala reported on 3 April 2025 that headquarters had returned the earlier submission with instructions to obtain House approval. This explains why the February proposal and April House budget should be archived as separate stages.

## FY 2026-27 meeting and financial accountability

The Tribune's 5 March 2026 report records discussion of damaged roads, ward development proposals, dog sterilisation and vaccination, relocation of stray cattle to gaushalas and proposed action against unauthorised cattle sheds on public land. It reports about 3,375 dogs sterilised and 3,530 vaccinated at that time, with sterilisation costing around Rs 1,450 per dog including transport, shelter and food. These are dated statements by the corporation, not independently audited performance totals.

For expenditure verification, request the signed annual budget, House agenda and resolution, state approval, revised estimates, grant-release orders, head-wise ledger and annual financial statements from the corporation's Accounts Branch. For a particular road or sanitation work, also obtain the administrative sanction, tender, work order, measurement book and payment record. This archive does not assert a statutory deadline or approval section that the reviewed documents do not establish.

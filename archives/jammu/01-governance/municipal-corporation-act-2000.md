# Jammu and Kashmir Municipal Corporation Act, 2000: Jammu's municipal government

## Formation and territorial limits

[JMC's institutional history](https://jmc.jk.gov.in/jmc.html) dates the former Jammu Municipality to March 1886 and attributes its upgrade to corporation status to SRO 46, dated 18 February 2003. It reports 75 wards and an approximate municipal area of 240 square kilometres, without a reference date for the area figure. The 2003 date comes from JMC's account; the original SRO 46 was not located for this record.

Section 1 of the JMC-hosted Act excludes cantonment areas. Section 3 sets out which municipal limits the Act recognizes and allows the government to add or remove specified land by Gazette notification after consulting the corporation. The JMC portal hosts two examples: [SRO 83 of 9 February 2018](https://jmc.jk.gov.in/adminjmcpanel/noticefiles/10120190791149.pdf) for specified Hazuri Bagh land and [SRO 268 of 11 June 2018](https://jmc.jk.gov.in/adminjmcpanel/noticefiles/101201933976148.pdf) for parcels in five niabats. Neither notification assigns those parcels to electoral wards.

## Council, wards and administration

Section 4 constitutes the corporation as a body corporate and provides for direct election from territorial constituencies called wards, subject to the Act's nomination provisions. Section 5 describes a five-year corporation term measured from the date appointed for its first meeting, unless the corporation is dissolved earlier. Section 6 covers delimitation, territorial extent and reservation of wards. These provisions describe the statutory arrangement; they do not identify the current council or establish a parcel's ward.

The posted text of section 40 describes Standing Committees for General Functions, Finance and Planning, Public Health and Sanitation, and Social Justice. Sections 45 and 50 distinguish the government's appointment of a Commissioner by notification from the executive duties the Commissioner exercises under the Act. Section 43 lists obligatory functions, including municipal drains and public conveniences, waste removal, municipal markets, streets and street lighting, public parks and the survey of buildings and land. Section 44 distinguishes discretionary functions.

## Version and date

The date field records the statute's year. The municipal PDF does not establish the day it was published or commenced, and it is not presented here as a current consolidated text. The [JMC circular enclosing changes to the Act](https://jmc.jk.gov.in/adminjmcpanel/noticefiles/151202114731549.pdf), dated 15 January 2021, illustrates why the old text should not be used by itself to settle current election procedure.

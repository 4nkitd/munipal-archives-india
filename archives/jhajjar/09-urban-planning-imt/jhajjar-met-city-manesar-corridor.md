# Jhajjar development plans, MET City and the Manesar logistics corridor

## Scope and planning jurisdictions

This record covers a regional corridor, not a single municipal planning area. Jhajjar town, MET City in the Badli tehsil belt, Arogya Dham Badsa and IMT Manesar must not be treated as interchangeable addresses or approvals. Farrukhnagar is the adjoining Gurugram-side transport link. A regional connection to Manesar does not establish that a MET plot lies inside Municipal Corporation Manesar.

Two separate final development plans were verified in the Haryana gazette:

| Planning area | Perspective year | Notification | Date |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Jhajjar | 2031 | CCP (NCR)/FDP/JJR-2019/1522 | 2019-08-02 |
| Arogya Dham Badsa, district Jhajjar | 2041 | CCP(NCR)/FDP/Badsa/2019/1601 | 2019-08-09 |

The 2041 document linked here is for Badsa, not a replacement titled "Jhajjar town development plan 2041". No such town-wide replacement was verified for this record. The frontmatter date is the Jhajjar final-plan notification date, not the inauguration date of MET City or any transport project.

MET City is a privately developed township. IMT Manesar is a separate industrial estate. Their commercial and freight relationships do not merge their licences, land-use plans or local-government boundaries. Parcel-level adjacency to Farrukhnagar or Manesar requires revenue maps and boundary notifications. The sources below establish regional proximity and transport connections, not a shared municipal boundary with every township pocket.

## Jhajjar final development plan 2031

The final plan accommodates a projected population of 500,000 by 2031. This is a planning assumption, not a measured current population. The gazette records a 2011 population of 61,558 for the town and villages within the urbanisable limit, distinct from Jhajjar town's own census population of 48,424.

The industrial allocation is in Jhajjar plan sectors 15, 16, 18 and part of 19. These sector numbers are not MET City or IMT Manesar sector numbers. The plan reserves part of sector 32 for warehousing, storage and the heavy and building-material market. It proposes Transport Nagar in sector 30.

The transport section identifies NH-352, the Rewari-Jhajjar-Rohtak railway and proposed railway yards or sidings near the station. It proposes 75-metre-wide Jhajjar-Badli-Delhi and Jhajjar-Farrukhnagar-Gurugram roads. A land reservation is not proof that widening has been completed. Zoning regulation VI permits the Director to withhold development until water, drainage and other facilities are satisfactory. Regulations XI and XII require conformity with detailed plans and an approved layout or applicable relaxation.

## MET City in the Badli, Yakubpur and Dadri Toe belt

Model Economic Township Limited describes itself as a wholly owned subsidiary of Reliance Industries Limited and describes MET City as planned across 8,250 acres. That is the developer's township-scale description, not the licensed area of every phase or evidence that the full area is built.

Haryana RERA provides more precise project geography. Its MET CITY project page identifies Pocket J and K, Sector 8, Yakubpur and Sondhi, district Jhajjar, tehsil Badli, with Model Economic Township Limited as promoter. The registration is HRERA-PKL-JJR-320-2022. The portal records approval on 27 June 2022; later agenda entries refer to the certificate dated 29 June 2022. Those entries concern the particular registered pocket, not the whole township.

A DTCP licence-renewal document hosted by Haryana RERA identifies industrial colony land in Bir Dadri, Dadri Toe, Yakubpur, Sondhi, Fatehpur and Bamnola. It records licence 129 of 2019 dated 4 December 2019 for 105.86875 acres, in addition to earlier licences. The same uploaded bundle records licence 11 of 2021 dated 12 March 2021 for another 15.33125 acres. These are licence-specific areas and must not be added indiscriminately to a township marketing total.

RERA promoter filings also identify METROPOLIS BY MET CITY at Daryapur, Badli, and MET Habitat at Dadri Toe. Dadri Toe appears elsewhere as Dadri Toi or Dadri Toye. Revenue-estate names and the exact licence schedule should control a land search.

Sources: [MET City developer website](https://www.metcity.in/), [RERA project 1962](https://haryanarera.gov.in/view_project/searchprojectDetail/1962), [DTCP renewal bundle](https://haryanarera.gov.in/project/view_uploaded_Document_open/82b7c74e3eNzI1NDU=), and [promoter filing for project 2916](https://haryanarera.gov.in/view_project/project_preview_open/2916).

## AIIMS Jhajjar and Arogya Dham Badsa 2041

The National Cancer Institute at Badsa is part of AIIMS New Delhi's Jhajjar campus. The Union Health Ministry explicitly clarified on 26 February 2019 that it is AIIMS Delhi's second campus, not a separate new AIIMS institution. It was dedicated to the nation on 12 February 2019.

AIIMS states that NCI occupies 60 acres within the Jhajjar campus and that its first phase began in December 2018 with 250 beds, outpatient services and operating theatres. Radiation oncology and nuclear medicine services began in March 2019. The 2019 government release gives a planned capacity of 710 patient-care beds and an approved cost of Rs 2,035 crore. Those historical capacities should not be read as a live bed-availability statement.

The Badsa final plan 2041 uses the institutional campus as a planning anchor. It projects 127,000 people and a total urbanisable area of 1,265 hectares. Its proposed allocations include 366 hectares residential, 51 commercial, 120 transport and communication, 68 public utilities, 339 public and semi-public uses, and 124 open spaces and green belts. These total 1,068 hectares before the separately recorded 197-hectare existing built-up area.

The plan expressly proposes no industrial sector because Badsa is intended as an institutional hub. It places Badsa on the Gurgaon-Badli road, with the Gurugram district boundary to the south and KMP Expressway to the west. Its references to a medical hub and IIT are planning proposals, not proof that every proposed institution has opened.

Sources: [Badsa final notification](https://tcpharyana.gov.in/Development_Plan/Jhajjar/Badsa/Badsa_FDP%202041_Notification.pdf), printed pages 2856-2859; [AIIMS NCI introduction](https://www.aiims.edu/index.php/en/departments-and-centers/specialty-centers/258-nci_jajjar/13968-intro-nci-jhajjhar); [Health Ministry clarification](https://www.pib.gov.in/PressReleasePage.aspx?PRID=1566423&lang=2&reg=48); [PIB release of 14 November 2019](https://pib.gov.in/PressReleasePage.aspx?PRID=1591673).

## KMP, Farrukhnagar and freight links to IMT Manesar

The KMP Expressway provides a regional road connection between the Jhajjar-side development belt and Manesar. The Prime Minister inaugurated its Kundli-Manesar section on 19 November 2018. The government described the completed expressway as approximately 135 kilometres, with the newly opened section approximately 83 kilometres. It provides an alternative to routing through Delhi.

MET City's February 2026 developer presentation gives an indicative distance of about 23 kilometres to Manesar. It does not specify a cadastral boundary or guarantee a particular truck route or journey time. The developer website identifies Farrukhnagar railway station and a private freight terminal as connectivity assets. A developer's reference to freight access does not establish that a particular siding or terminal has been commissioned.

The Haryana Orbital Rail Corridor is a separate regional railway project. HRIDC's October 2025 investment document describes a roughly 126-kilometre Palwal-Sonipat route through Sohna, Manesar and Kharkhoda. Its connection schedule includes Sultanpur on the Garhi Harsaru-Farrukhnagar branch and a Sultanpur-Badsa connection.

There is verified commissioning at the Manesar end. HRIDC records the Patli-Manesar-Maruti Suzuki connectivity line as commissioned for freight on 17 June 2025. PIB confirms inauguration of Maruti Suzuki's Manesar Gati Shakti cargo terminal that day, with a stated annual loading capacity of 450,000 automobiles. This is not evidence that the entire orbital railway, the Badsa connection or MET City's own proposed terminal is operational. HRIDC's October 2025 schedule places the Sultanpur-Badsa connection in a June 2028 target group.

Sources: [KMP inauguration](https://pib.gov.in/PressReleasePage.aspx?PRID=1553220), [MET February 2026 presentation](https://www.metcity.in/sites/metcity/files/2026-02/taiwan-english.pdf), [HRIDC October 2025 project document](https://www.hridc.co.in/pdf/HORC%20EOI%2006102025%20%282%29.pdf), and [PIB Manesar cargo-terminal release](https://www.pib.gov.in/PressReleasePage.aspx?PRID=2136909&lang=2&reg=3).

## Checks before using this record for a parcel or project

Obtain the revenue village, khasra numbers and exact project pocket first. Check the applicable notified plan, DTCP licence and approved layout rather than assuming that a nearby township, hospital or expressway grants development rights. For MET property, match the RERA registration to the actual phase and inspect licence renewals, completion documents and service approvals separately.

For logistics decisions, establish the permitted road access, interchange route, truck restrictions and the operating rail terminal actually available to the consignment. Do not substitute a proposed orbital-rail connection or developer terminal for a commissioned freight route. For municipal services, verify the local-body boundary independently of the district name and sales address.

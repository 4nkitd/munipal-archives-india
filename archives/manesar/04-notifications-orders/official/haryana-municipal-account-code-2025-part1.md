# Haryana Municipal Account Code, 2025: Part I, accounting rules and procedures

## Notification and commencement

Haryana Government, Urban Local Bodies Department notification `S.O. 45/H.A.24/1973/S.257/2025`, dated 20 May 2025, appears in the Haryana Government Gazette dated 27 May 2025 (Jystha 6, 1947 Saka), pages 2321 to 2683. Paragraph 1.3 states: "It shall come into force on the date of its publication in the Official Gazette." The effective date is therefore 27 May 2025, not the notification date of 20 May or the Manesar portal listing date of 25 September 2025.

The notification invokes Section 257(1) and (3) of the Haryana Municipal Act, 1973 (24 of 1973), and refers to preliminary notification `S.O. 7/H.A.24/1973/S.257/2025`, dated 3 February 2025. The heading cites both that Act and the Haryana Municipal Corporation Act, 1994 (16 of 1994). It covers municipal corporations, councils and committees across Haryana. Paragraph 31.1 repeals the Municipal Account Code, 1930, but treats orders and actions under the repealed Code as made or taken under the corresponding new provisions.

## Accrual and double-entry accounting

Paragraph 1.4 defines the accounting period, generally 1 April to 31 March, and the accounting unit, which can be a zone, circle, division, project or ward office identified to maintain accounts. Paragraphs 4.1 to 4.3 provide for accrual-based books, kept separately for every financial year. The Cash Book, GEN-1, records cash and bank receipts and payments, with a separate book for each bank account. The Journal Book, GEN-2, records non-cash entries by debit and credit, including a property tax demand raised and a supplier liability incurred. The Ledger, GEN-3, classifies entries by account head; cash deposits or withdrawals and inter-bank transfers use contra entries. Under paragraph 28.10, every debit has a corresponding credit and the trial balance totals must agree.

Paragraph 2.1 calls for consistent accounting policies across years and funds, disclosure in notes, and disclosure of material deviations with reasons and their financial effect, where ascertainable. Where the Code is silent on an accounting policy, the National Municipal Accounts Manual applies. Paragraph 1.9 allows electronic records unless another applicable law requires otherwise; forms other than those prescribed require the approval of the Director, Local Audit, Haryana. Under paragraph 1.10, records may be destroyed only under the Haryana Municipal (Destruction of Records) Rules, 1976 or state instructions.

## Chart of accounts, budget and funds

Paragraphs 3.3 to 3.9 require function and account head classifications. Functionary (responsibility centre or branch), field (geographical centre) and fund are optional groups in the chart structure. Function, functionary and field identify budgeting centres. A primary account code has ten digits: three for major head, two for minor head, three for sub-minor head and two for detailed head. The first digit is 1 for revenue income, 2 for revenue expenditure, 3 for capital receipts and liabilities, and 4 for capital expenditure and assets. Examples include 110/01 for property tax, 210/10 for salaries and allowances, 310/10 for creditors and 410/20 for buildings. Statements use major heads; their schedules use minor heads. The Accounts Branch maintains a master index. Paragraphs 3.10 and 3.11 refer to the Part II Change Request and Change Authorized forms.

Paragraphs 25.2 to 25.6 distinguish revenue receipts, capital receipts, revenue expenditure and capital expenditure. They use working sheets BWS-1, BWS-2 and BWS-3 and the BUD form, including the BUD-4 proforma referred to in paragraph 25.4. Include all receipts and payments, classify estimates under the appropriate fund, function, field and account head, and balance expenditure against revenues and available carry-over. Preparation and deadlines follow the Department's applicable SOP. Paragraph 25.14 requires minimum working balance of **5 percent** of estimated receipts, excluding endowments, government grants, loans, advances and deposits, with specific grant or contribution balances excluded from cash balance for the test.

Paragraphs 24.2 and 24.3 permit earmarking from municipal funds or specified receipts for a special purpose. Transfer amounts to a separate special-fund account, debit related revenue expenditure to that fund, record the fund as a liability when created and credit investment income to it on accrual. When a fixed asset is completed or acquired using the fund, transfer the equivalent cost to Special Fund (Utilized). Under paragraph 28.17, if separate books are maintained for a fund, produce its trial balance and statements, then consolidate the results for the municipality.

## Revenue recognition and property tax

Paragraph 2.2 distinguishes demand from collection. Accrue property and other tax revenue in the period it becomes due. Accrue a new or revised assessment in the month the demand is served. Recognize interest and penalties on receipt, notice and warrant fees when billed, and property transfer charges when received. For property-tax arrears beyond two years, the accounting provision is 25 percent for over two through three years, 50 percent for over three through four, 75 percent for over four through five, and 100 percent for over five. The provision does not change the Demand and Collection Register balances or relieve the municipality of collection. Current-year refunds or remissions offset current income; prior-year amounts are prior-period items.

Chapter 7 places assessment and collection under the Zonal Taxation Officer, Tax Superintendent or officer authorized by the Chief Municipal Officer. Paragraph 7.4 describes the lands and buildings register TS-1. Paragraphs 7.7 to 7.9 govern TS-5, the Tax Demand and Collection Register: record authority and grievance number for corrections, carry prior-year arrears into the new register, then enter current demands. Paragraph 2.5 distinguishes water and sewerage bills recognized when due from water connection, tanker and road-damage charges recognized on receipt. Paragraph 2.6 generally recognizes assigned revenues on collection, with a year-end accrual where sanction exists and the amount can be ascertained.

## Receipts, vouchers and posting

Under paragraph 5.1, counter receipts use GEN-8 through government-approved software, and counter cheques or drafts go into GEN-9. Banks and payment aggregators credit the designated account immediately and provide GEN-12 on the same or next working day. A successful online payment generates GEN-8 immediately. Paragraph 5.2 requires deposits of cash, cheques and drafts in the designated account or with the designated collection officer on the same or next working day. Paragraphs 5.5 to 5.9 use the GEN-12 daily statement to prepare GEN-4, credit the collection to the relevant head and post from the Cash Book into the Ledger. The example debits main bank `450-21-000-01` and credits contractor security deposit `340-10-000-02`, ₹10,000 each.

Paragraph 4.3 requires serial numbering and separate number sequences for cash and each bank account as applicable. GEN-4 supports receipts, GEN-5 payments, GEN-6 transfers between cash and bank or between banks, and GEN-7 non-cash journal entries. See [Part II](haryana-municipal-account-code-2025-part2.md) for the Gazette forms and links to their governing procedures.

# 2026 empanelment for solid waste services to bulk waste generators

## What MCM published

The portal listed the file on 14 October 2026 as an RFP. The 24-page file calls itself an Expression of Interest (EOI) and explicitly says it is an empanelment framework, not a conventional rate-contract tender. Municipal Corporation Manesar (MCM) can empanel several agencies in a category. It does not guarantee work, waste quantities, customers, revenue, payment or exclusivity. The initial term is three years from issue of the empanelment letter. An extension depends on performance and the competent authority's decision. Renewal is not automatic.

The EOI applies within MCM limits and cites the Solid Waste Management Rules, 2026, the Environment (Protection) Act, 1986, relevant Haryana and MCM directions, and pollution control and waste-stream rules. Under section 1.1, a bulk waste generator (BWG) meets **at least one** of these thresholds:

| Criterion | Threshold |
| --- | --- |
| Building floor area | 20,000 square metres or more |
| Water consumption | 40,000 litres per day or more |
| Solid waste generation | 100 kg per day or more |

Examples include residential and group housing societies, institutions, industries, hotels, restaurants, hospitals, schools, malls, markets, warehouses and banquet halls.

## Applicant eligibility and costs

Applicants must be legally registered entities with valid PAN and GST registration where applicable, and at least ₹10 lakh average annual turnover over the last three financial years, certified by a chartered accountant. They must not be blacklisted or debarred at submission. They need category-specific experience, technical capability, vehicles, staff, equipment and lawful processing facilities or arrangements, as applicable. They must hold the necessary licences, authorisations, consents to establish or operate and other approvals. A proposed processing operator must show access to suitable land or a facility.

The application covers registration and financial records, a non-blacklisting affidavit, similar work, vehicle RCs, fitness and insurance, infrastructure and land documents, facility photos and coordinates, HSPCB approvals and downstream agreements. It also requires a work plan with deployment, waste flow, safety and fire response, tracking and reporting, a proposed implementation timeline, and undertakings on the 2026 Rules, EBWGR and excluded waste. MCM first screens legal and financial paperwork, evaluates technical capability, may inspect facilities and equipment, then decides which categories to empanel.

| Item | Published condition |
| --- | --- |
| Earnest money deposit (EMD) | No separate EMD is specified. |
| Application fee | Screening mentions fee or empanelment requirements, but the file specifies no separate application fee amount. |
| Bank guarantee or security | ₹5,00,000 in favour of MCM, valid throughout empanelment. MCM can forfeit it for breaches and release it after satisfactory closure and clearance of liabilities. |
| Empanelment fee | ₹1,00,000, one-time and non-refundable, payable by the successful agency. |

Annexure I addresses applications to the Commissioner, Municipal Corporation Manesar, Haryana. The EOI does not give a submission deadline, submission channel, named contact officer, or dedicated phone or email.

## Collection, segregation and processing

| Category | Service |
| --- | --- |
| I | Collect segregated waste at BWG premises and transport it to authorised facilities. |
| II | Process wet waste through composting, vermicomposting, anaerobic digestion, biomethanation, Bio-CNG or another approved method. |
| III | Sort, bale and store dry waste and send recyclables to registered operators. |
| IV | Operate a material recovery facility (MRF) or secondary sorting facility. |
| V | Handle sanitary and special-care waste separately through authorised facilities. |
| VI | Collect and shred, mulch, compost or otherwise process horticulture waste. |
| VII | Provide integrated collection, processing, recycling, recovery and disposal. |

Applicants may select multiple categories and must document capability for each. The EOI also describes digital waste services, including GPS, QR or RFID-based collection, weighment, records and portal reporting. Annexure I lists digital compliance and tracking as a separate application choice.

Agencies must collect regularly, take only segregated waste, and keep wet, dry, sanitary, special-care and horticulture waste separate throughout collection, transport and processing. Covered vehicles must prevent spills, provide separate compartments where required, display the agency name and helpline, and be registered and GPS-integrated on the MCM solid waste portal. Wet waste should be processed at source or at decentralised facilities where possible. Facilities must control leachate, odour and fire risks and keep input, output and disposal records. Dry recyclables must go to registered recyclers and residues to authorised destinations. Biomedical, hazardous, electronic, battery, construction and demolition, and radioactive waste fall under separate rules and must not be mixed into municipal solid waste.

## BWG compliance, timelines and service charges

BWGs must register through the centralised online portal, segregate waste at source, hand dry, sanitary and special-care streams to MCM or its authorised agency, and arrange decentralised wet and horticulture waste processing. New BWGs must provide adequate wet-waste capacity. Existing BWGs unable to process wet waste locally must obtain any applicable exemption and meet Extended Bulk Waste Generator Responsibility (EBWGR) requirements. BWGs submit their annual returns by the prescribed date and obtain EBWGR certificates where required. The EOI does not state a calendar date for those statutory returns. Agencies supply records and proof, but only the competent local body issues the certificates.

The BWG and agency must sign an agreement before service starts. The agency sends a copy to MCM within **15 days of execution**. The agreement specifies frequency, waste streams, facilities, reporting, duration, grievance and termination terms, charges and payment. The parties agree user charges directly, subject to MCM and government directions. The EOI sets no flat municipal tariff. Agencies must disclose all charges and issue invoices or receipts. MCM is not a party to the commercial agreement and is not responsible for non-payment or payment disputes.

Agencies must report collection, quantity, vehicle movement and destination daily or in real time. Monthly reports cover waste streams, processing, recycling, disposal, complaints and incidents. Quarterly reports cover performance and compliance. Annual reports consolidate volumes, disposal and EBWGR information. MCM can change the reporting frequency and inspect facilities, vehicles and records with or without notice. Indicative penalties include ₹5,000 per BWG per day for missed collection, ₹2,000 per vehicle per instance for mixing segregated streams, and ₹25,000 per instance for unauthorised disposal. Serious or repeated violations can lead to termination or blacklisting.

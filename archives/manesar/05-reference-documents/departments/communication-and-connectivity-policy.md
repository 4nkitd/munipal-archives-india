# Haryana Communication and Connectivity Infrastructure Policy 2017 and 2018 amendment

## Gazette sources and application portal

The [2017 notification](https://mcmanesar.in/sites/default/files/2025-09/2022021769.pdf), No. **Admn./368/1SIT/5627**, appeared on 6 October 2017 in Haryana Government Gazette Extraordinary No. 175-2017/Ext., starting at page 3946. It followed the Indian Telegraph Right of Way Rules, 2016. The [2018 amending notification](https://mcmanesar.in/sites/default/files/2025-09/Amd.pdf), No. **Admn/368/1SIT/7337**, appeared on **24 August 2018** in Gazette Extraordinary No. 140-2018/Ext., starting at page 2647. Its Annexure A is headed "Amended up to 31.07.2018". The [Manesar index](https://mcmanesar.in/communication-and-connectivity-documents) lists the amendment as 28 August 2018; 24 August is the Gazette date.

This record describes the 2017 policy as reproduced in the 2018 Gazette, not the fees or deadlines for a new application. Haryana later issued a [revised 2023 policy](https://haryanait.gov.in/communication-connectivity-infrastructure-policy/). The [Telecommunications (Right of Way) Rules, 2024](https://www.dot.gov.in/static/uploads/2026/10/0f73743dce535c25169a57a8dedeb027.pdf) took effect on 1 January 2025. The [Haryana Government's September 2026 update](https://prms.prharyana.gov.in/press-release/6541) identifies [Invest Haryana](https://investharyana.in/) as the state's upgraded portal for those rules. The 2017 and 2018 Gazettes describe a state ease-of-doing-business single window using a Common Application Form (CAF), with manual processing until the online link was introduced.

## Eligibility and permission terms

Under clauses 2 to 4, a Department of Telecommunications (DoT) registered infrastructure provider, a DoT-licensed telecom service provider, or an infrastructure provider authorized by a licensee could apply to the district Deputy Commissioner (DC). Coverage included Urban Local Body and other authority land, ground-based and rooftop towers, ducts and underground optical fibre cable (OFC). Domestic television and dish antennas were excluded. Right of Way (RoW) and Right of Use (RoU) permissions were non-exclusive, non-transferable and conveyed no ownership or perpetual rights. Permissions normally ran with the DoT licence; renewal applications were due at least 60 days before expiry, with documents and applicable charges.

The separate temporary procedure in clause 4.2 covered movable towers and Cells on Wheels for events for the duration of the event, up to 180 days. A reasoned request carried no fee and was deemed granted after one week without permission. No formal permission was required for movable units used in a disaster. Units installed in lieu of permanent infrastructure to cover gaps instead needed formal permission, documents and charges under clause 4.3.

## Mobile tower application and safety

Appendix 1 requires **Form 1** for overground installations and renewals. Submit the DoT licence or registration, a 1:1,000 location plan, design and foundation drawings with nearby power-line clearances shown, structural stability certification, proof of ownership and the lease or owner consent. Include the applicable high-rise fire No Objection Certificate (NOC), forest clearance where applicable, proof of prior written consent for Central Government or PSU premises and the ARAI type-test certificate if using a diesel generator. The application also requires a work schedule, indemnity bond, undertaking and proof of payment. The authority can ask for a separate NOC from the property or rooftop rights holder.

Clause 10.3 requires technical-design and structural safety certification from an authorized body or professional, such as an IIT, PEC Chandigarh, NIT Kurukshetra, CBRI Roorkee or a qualifying government-registered or government-employed structural engineer. Under Appendix 3, tower and mast heights need Standing Advisory Committee on Radio Frequency Allocation (SACFA) approval or certification and must meet DoT norms. Appendix 1 accepts the SACFA clearance or acknowledged application and an undertaking to correct or remove the installation if objected to or rejected.

Electromagnetic field (EMF) exposure in public areas must meet applicable DoT norms. A Telecom Enforcement Resource and Monitoring (TERM) Cell acknowledgement of the operator's peak-traffic exposure self-certificate may be submitted within 30 days of the tower starting to radiate. Base Transceiver Stations (BTS) are subject to periodic self-certification and TERM Cell audit.

## Underground OFC and processing timelines

Appendix 1 requires **Form 2** for underground infrastructure or fibre to the home. Include the DoT licence or registration, the route map with latitude, longitude and nature of land, owner consent, a stretch-wise work schedule, the indemnity bond and undertaking, proof of payment and forest clearance where applicable. Attach designs for aboveground fittings if the project includes them. Appendix 3 calls for a survey to identify buried utilities, a preference for micro-trenching or directional boring, and restoration alongside the work. Normally the top of the conduit must be at least 1.5 m below the surface and at least 0.3 m below the drain invert. Fix route markers every 300 m.

| Step under clauses 6 and 7 | Working days in clause 7.1 |
| --- | --- |
| DC forwards the application to the relevant nodal officers | 5 |
| Nodal officers review concurrently and send recommendations to the DC | 15 |
| DC issues a Letter of Intent (LoI) or communicates rejection with reasons | 3 |
| Applicant complies with LoI conditions or the LoI is cancelled | 7 |
| DC issues permission after receiving the required papers and charges | 5 |
| Applicant provides requested information or the application is rejected | 10 |

Both versions of clause 6.4 treat an application as cleared by a department that neither requests additional information nor communicates a decision within the prescribed process. The 2017 clause says the DC issues the agreement to be executed along with the LoI; the 2018 clause omits that wording but retains the separate applicable agreement step in clause 6.5. Clause 7.2 separately deems permission granted if no grant or rejection is issued within 45 days of submission. Clause 6.5 excludes ground-based masts and towers and communication or mobile towers from the listed restoration guarantee and agreement step. Clause 8.5 provides for a completion certificate within 30 days if there is no objection and gives the applicant 15 days to correct restoration deficiencies.

## Application charges and restoration security

Under Appendix 2, urban areas declared for the Gurgaon-Manesar Urban Complex were in the Hyper Potential Zone. The 2017 and 2018 schedules list the following charges. The one-time administrative charges are non-refundable.

| Purpose | Charges in the published schedules |
| --- | --- |
| Underground communication infrastructure | Rs 1,000 per km, all zones |
| Overhead communication infrastructure, one-time administrative charge | Rs 1,00,000 in the Hyper through Low Potential Zones; Rs 10,000 in other areas including rural areas |
| Ground-based mast or tower, rooftop tower or pole, or movable tower in lieu of permanent coverage | Rs 10,000 annually per site or unit in the Hyper through Low Potential Zones; Rs 5,000 elsewhere |
| Using a streetlight pole for OFC, aerial cabling or cable TV | Rs 500 annually per pole |
| Erecting a pole for overhead cable | Rs 1,000 annually per pole |
| Non-DTH dish antenna | Rs 5,000 annually per dish |

Clause 2.1.1 allows a one-time payment of five times the applicable table 2.1(B) annual rate in place of those annual charges during the permission term. Private-land use is exempt from the table 2.1(B) annual payment to the authority to that extent; the owner and operator agree their lease charges. On land or buildings of the Appropriate Authority, the annual charge is 10% of the collector rate per square metre. Manhole rent uses the same percentage and the actual number and size of chambers. The schedule lists Rs 1,000 annually for each Wi-Fi antenna or microcell unit with an attached utility box on the listed public properties.

| Restoration performance bank guarantee, per route metre | Concrete road or pavement | Concrete paver blocks | Metalled road or pavement | Unpaved road or rasta | Other |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Micro-trenching | Rs 50 | Rs 50 | Rs 30 | Not applicable | Rs 20 |
| Horizontal directional drilling or horizontal boring | Rs 100 | Rs 100 | Rs 100 | Rs 100 | Rs 100 |
| Open digging | Not allowed | Not allowed | Not allowed | Rs 500 | Rs 500 |

The refundable performance bank guarantee runs for the work period plus six months and must be extended when the work period is extended. On a satisfactory restoration report, Appendix 2 calls for inspection within 15 days and release within 15 days of inspection or 30 days of the request, subject to satisfactory restoration. The authority can encash the guarantee and restore the site at the applicant's risk and cost if the applicant fails to complete restoration within the agreed time.

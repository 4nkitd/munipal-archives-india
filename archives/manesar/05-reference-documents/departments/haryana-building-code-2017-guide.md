# Haryana Building Code 2017 guide, amended through May 2023

## Editions and how to read the limits

The [Manesar Town Planning documents index](https://mcmanesar.in/town-planning-documents) publishes two full compilations: [amendments dated 22 December 2017 and 8 May 2018](https://mcmanesar.in/sites/default/files/2025-09/Haryana%20Building%20Code%202017%20amended.pdf) and [amendments through 25 May 2023](https://mcmanesar.in/sites/default/files/2025-09/Haryana%20Building%20code%202017%20amended%20upto.pdf). The preface dates the original Haryana Building Code to 30 June 2016 and its first revision to 6 January 2017. The tables below use the May 2023 compilation unless noted. Identify the sanctioned land use, core or non-core designation, zoning plan and applicable scheme before applying the limits.

Code 1.2 defines ground coverage as built-up area covered at ground level. Floor Area Ratio (FAR) is the counted covered area of all floors multiplied by 100 and divided by the plot area; 165% is equivalent to a ratio of 1.65. Code 1.2 excludes designated areas, including unenclosed stilt space used only for parking or as a pedestrian plaza. Shafts, chutes, lift wells and the staircase from the stilt to the next floor are counted once at ground level. Code 7.1 separately excludes covered basement, stilt and multilevel parking from FAR but counts the footprint of separate parking blocks in ground coverage.

## Residential plots and setbacks

### Core areas, Code 6.3(2)(i) and (iii)

The residential schedule shows a single basement level and a 16.5 m maximum under its G+3/S+4 heading. The 2018 compilation gave 15 m with the same core residential coverage and FAR percentages.

| Plot area (m²) | Max. ground coverage | Max. FAR | Min. front setback (m) | Min. rear setback (m) |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Up to 60 | 85% | 220% | 0.5 | 0.5 |
| Over 60 to 150 | 80% | 200% | 1.5 | 1.0 |
| Over 150 to 225 | 70% | 180% | 2.5 | 2.0 |
| Over 225 to 450 | 60% | 160% | 3.0 | 3.0 |
| Over 450 | 50% | 140% | 4.5 | 4.0 |

Use the scheme's zoning lines where a zoning plan exists. The tabulated core setbacks apply where no plan has been prepared. The front setback can be adjusted to achieve permissible coverage; the rear setback is fixed. The single basement roof may project at most 1.5 m above ground within the permissible covered area and must be flush with ground beyond it up to the zoned area.

### Outside core areas, Code 6.3(3)(i)

| Plotted residential area (m²) | Max. ground coverage | Max. FAR before additional FAR | Basement |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Up to 100 | 75% | 165% | Single level |
| Over 100 to 250 | 75% | 145% | Single level |
| Over 250 to 350 | 66% | 130% | Single level |
| Over 350 to 500 | 66% | 120% | Single level |
| Over 500 to 1,000 | 66% | 100% | Single level |

The 2018 non-core table gave 66% coverage for the first two slabs and 60% for the last three, with the same FAR and a 15 m maximum. The May 2023 non-core table, as amended on 25 April 2022, lists 16.5 m in each G+3/S+4 row. Note V under the same table separately retains a 15 m condition for allowing parking stilts on residential and commercial plots. The compilation does not reconcile those two figures. Confirm applicable height and stilt treatment for the plot before relying on either figure. Note VI makes violation of the listed ground coverage limit non-compoundable.

| Non-core plotted residential area (m²) | Min. front setback (m) | Min. rear setback (m) |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Up to 75 | 1.0 | 1.5 |
| Over 75 to 150 | 1.5 | 2.0 |
| Over 150 to 250 | 2.0 | 2.0 |
| Over 250 | 3.0 | 3.0 |

No balcony may project beyond rear zoning. Code 6.4 ties front, rear and side building lines to the approved zoning plan and permits special zoning for unusual sites with reasons recorded and fire safety considered. Group housing outside core areas generally has 35% coverage, 175% FAR, up to four basement levels and height listed as unrestricted subject to applicable clearances. Transit Oriented Development (TOD) and Affordable Group Housing have separate planning rules in Annexure D.

## Industrial and IT development

Code 6.3(3)(iv), substituted on 25 April 2022, sets the following industrial and IT limits outside core areas. ITES means Information Technology Enabled Services.

| Use | Max. ground coverage | Max. FAR | Basement | Max. height and road conditions |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| General industry | 60% | 150% | Twin level | 30 m |
| Apparel and footwear | 60% | 250% | Unrestricted | Unrestricted; road Right of Way at least 15 m |
| Biotechnology other than pharmaceuticals and IT/ITES | 40% | 250% | Unrestricted | Unrestricted; road Right of Way at least 15 m |
| IT park, cyber park, cyber city, technology park | 40% | 250% | Unrestricted | Unrestricted; road Right of Way at least 30 m |
| Data centre industry | 60% | 500% | Unrestricted | Unrestricted, with structural and fire safety conditions and no mezzanine for the floor-to-ceiling height exemption |

"Unrestricted" basement levels depend on parking, storage or permissible habitable FAR. The common notes allow four basement levels on sites above 8,000 m² and otherwise as decided by the Competent Authority. "Unrestricted" height remains subject to any applicable No Objection Certificate (NOC) from defence, air force, airport or other appropriate authority. In mixed-use projects the approved zoning plan determines coverage, FAR, setbacks and other norms. Code 6.3(3)(v) gives different all-round setbacks for storage uses: 15 m for integrated inland container depots/customs bonded areas, 9 m for non-agricultural or mixed warehouses, 6 m for agricultural storage and as per zoning for gas godowns.

## Purchasable FAR, TDR and stilt-plus-four floors

The non-core plotted residential note in Code 6.3 allows additional FAR at rates approved by the Haryana Government from time to time. The common notes provide for prescribed payment for industrial FAR beyond the general 125% level and for an extra 25 percentage points over the 150% level in resorts, five-star hotels and motels. Code 6.3(3)(viii)(c) separately refers additional FAR obtained using Transferable Development Rights (TDR) to Annexure D, clause 3 and the 16 November 2021 policy as amended. Examples in Annexure D include a residential base FAR ratio of 1.0 to 1.65 and a maximum with TDR of 2.4 or 2.64 as applicable, and a commercial component ratio of 1.75 rising to 3.0 with TDR. The coverage, road, parking and siting restrictions still apply.

An S+4 entry in the May 2023 table is not a blanket permission. A later [Haryana Town and Country Planning direction, Memo No. Misc-2339(L)(S+4)/2024/19679-83 of 2 July 2024](https://www.hsvphry.org.in/documents/home/news_updates/newsUpdate_202408021626_638582128157811000.pdf), provided for S+4 on residential plots in sectors and colonies approved at 18 persons per plot, including licensed DDJAY colonies with services plans approved or revised to that density. For layouts approved at 13.5 persons per plot, the direction requires access from a road at least 10 m wide plus either an irrevocable mutual agreement with adjoining owners, subject to the stated exceptions, or 1.8 m side setbacks on all floors from the adjoining plots. It also addresses basements, common walls and enclosure of stilt parking. The direction states that it takes effect when the S+4 portal goes live.

The same 2024 memo lists a maximum ratio of 2.4 with Purchasable Development Rights (PDR) for the three plot slabs over 250 m². Its Hyper Potential Zone rates for extra FAR are Rs 4,500 per m² for over 250 to 350 m², Rs 6,500 per m² for over 350 to 500 m² and Rs 10,000 per m² for over 500 m². These are figures from the dated direction, not a current quote or an entitlement. Check the applicable zoning plan, agency approvals and later directions before buying FAR or designing an S+4 building.

## Parking and equivalent car spaces, Code 7.1

The May 2023 Code requires stilt parking on construction of the third or fourth floor in the Hyper Potential Gurugram-Manesar Urban Complex and on construction of the fourth floor in other towns. The requirement applies prospectively to new construction on vacant plots or reconstruction de novo. A service or guard room and toilet adjoining the stair or lift may occupy up to 16 m² on plots up to 200 m² and 20 m² on larger plots, counted in FAR and subject to parking and other norms. No commercial activity or storage is permitted in covered parking.

| Use or residential plot size | Minimum parking |
| --- | --- |
| Residential plotted, up to 100 m² | 0.5 equivalent car space (ECS) per floor |
| Residential plotted, next slab from 100 to 250 m² | 1 ECS per floor |
| Residential plotted, slab from 250 to 500 m² | 1.5 ECS per floor |
| Residential plotted, 500 m² and above | 2 ECS per floor |
| Group housing | 1.5 ECS per dwelling; at least 5% of total car parking area for EWS flats |
| Integrated or multistorey commercial, big box retail, restaurant, multiplex or mall | 1 ECS per 50 m² covered area |
| Offices, cyber/IT parks and cyber cities | 1 ECS per 75 m² covered area |
| Industry | 1 ECS per 300 m² covered area |
| Hotels and motels | 1 ECS per 75 m² covered area |

The plotted residential table prints overlapping end points; check how the authority assigns a boundary-size plot. It uses a 2.5 m by 5 m bay (12.5 m² per ECS) in stilt/open space within the plot. For the non-plotted categories listed in Code 7.1, the calculation uses 23 m² open, 28 m² on stilts and 32 m² in a basement per ECS.

The 2018 text instead measured residential parking per dwelling unit: no ECS up to 150 m², 0.5 over 150 to 250 m², 0.75 over 250 to 500 m² and 1 over 500 m². The two editions should not be combined when reading a historical approval.

## Building plan approval and self-certification

The [ULB building plan service page](https://www.ulbharyana.gov.in/Home/ApprovalRevisionOfBuildingPlan) directs industrial and commercial applications through [Invest Haryana](https://investharyana.in/) and lists a separate route for residential and institutional applications. The department's [Unified ULB OBPAS portal](https://obpas.ulbharyana.gov.in/) announces its 8 May 2026 launch. Confirm the competent agency and use the applicable route; Code 2.3 itself refers to the Competent Authority's electronic system rather than naming a website.

1. Appoint the appropriate Architect or Engineer. Code 2.1 requires both for supervision above 15 m including the stilt; for residential or commercial construction up to 15 m either may supervise.
2. For regular scrutiny under Code 2.1, digitally sign and submit Form BR-I, ownership or allotment papers, site and building plans with electronic DWG drawings, BR-II specifications, the applicable structural drawings and BR-V(A1) or BR-V(A2) certificates, public health drawings, and applicable fire and HVAC designs. Pay the non-refundable scrutiny fee of Rs 10 per m² of covered area. A returned application may be resubmitted without a fresh scrutiny fee at most twice within 60 days of first return.
3. Code 5.1 classifies plotted residential and commercial sites up to 1,000 m² and 15 m, and industrial buildings of all sizes and heights, as low risk. Commercial sites of 1,001 to 2,000 m² up to 15 m are moderate risk. Other buildings are high risk. For low and moderate risk under Code 2.2, submit BRS-I, the supporting Code 2.1 documents and the BRS-II structural conformity certification. The Code allows construction after complete submission, but the authority can check plans and works and require correction. The regular route applies to high risk.
4. Under Codes 4.2 to 4.5 the regular sanction or refusal uses BR-III. The Code sets a 20-day decision period on a complete submission and deemed sanction only if the plan meets the Code and permitted land use and all leviable fees have been paid. Sanction validity is two years below 15 m and five years at or above 15 m. At damp-proof course (DPC) level, file the conformity certification under Code 4.9; the authority has seven days to respond.

## Occupation certificate and completion forms

| Route | Submission | Decision and deemed issuance in the May 2023 text |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Regular, Code 4.10 | BR-IV(A) or BR-IV(B), applicable BR-V(1) or BR-V(2), and BR-VI completion certificate and drawings | BR-VII decision in 18 days. If no communication in 60 days, the filed BR-IV(A) or BR-IV(B) acts as the Occupation Certificate, subject to later enforcement. |
| Low-risk self-certification, Code 4.11 | BRS-III with BRS-IV, signed by the owner and Architect, and completion drawings | BRS-V within eight working days of complete receipt; filed BRS-III is deemed the certificate if there is no communication within eight working days. |
| Moderate-risk third-party certification, Code 4.11a | BRS-III with BRS-IVA from an independent Architect or Engineer and completion drawings | Same eight-working-day BRS-V or deemed BRS-III process. |

Submit photographs of the elevations, setbacks, shafts and cut-outs, details of deviations and any applicable composition charges, and clear construction debris. The regular route also calls for applicable fire safety, rooftop solar and Energy Conservation Building Code completion documents. The regular residential minimum is 25% of permissible ground coverage with at least one habitable room, a kitchen and a toilet complete. The Occupation Certificate (OC) precedes occupation and, under Code 4.10, water, sewer and electricity connections. Later inspection can result in action for violations, including cancellation of a self-certified or third-party OC.

## Rainwater harvesting and rooftop solar

Code 8.1 calls for roof rainwater harvesting inside the plot for roofs of 100 m² or more in areas notified by the Competent Authority. It lists Gurgaon among the districts and towns where its provisions are to be implemented strictly. Groundwater recharge applies to buildings including group housing above the stated 500 m² plot threshold and to common open areas such as parks and plazas. The building plan must show collection, conveyance and recharge. Separate the first flush, filter the runoff and keep it free of contaminated water. Keep the recharge well at least 10 m from sewage or industrial wastewater treatment structures; the distance exception for manholes and sewer lines requires them to be leakproof. The supervising Architect or Engineer certifies that the system works, and the required works form part of the OC.

Code 8.2 ties compulsory rooftop photovoltaic installation to Renewable Energy Department, Haryana order No. **22/52/2005-5 Power** of **21 March 2016**, as amended. The Code refers to that order for covered buildings and areas rather than imposing one universal system capacity. Applicable installations form part of OC compliance. Code 6.5 separately provides a solar FAR incentive outside plotted residential use: 3%, 6%, 9% or 12% additional FAR for solar generation of 15 to 25%, 26 to 50%, 51 to 75% or 76 to 100% of connected load, respectively.

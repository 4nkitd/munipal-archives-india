# Haryana Municipal Corporation Act, 1994 and its application to Manesar

## Statutory identity and local application

The Haryana Municipal Corporation Act, 1994 is Haryana Act No. 16 of 1994. India Code records its enactment date as 22 October 1994 and its department as Urban Local Bodies. The DULB text records presidential assent on 20 October 1994 and first publication in the Haryana Government Gazette Extraordinary on 22 October 1994. Its commencement provision deems it effective from 31 May 1994.

The Act supplies the legal framework for declaring municipal corporations, establishing their governing bodies, raising municipal revenue and performing civic functions. MCM's official General Documents page lists its formation notification dated 24 December 2020 and amended-area notification dated 16 December 2022. Those local notifications determine its territorial application; the parent Act alone does not identify every Manesar village or sector.

This is an archival navigation guide, not a certified consolidation of every amendment effective in 2026. The departmental PDF and latest Gazette amendments should be checked before quoting operative wording, election provisions, deadlines or sanctions in proceedings.

## Constitution, wards and division of powers

Sections 3 and 4 deal with declaration and constitution of a corporation. The State Government notifies municipal areas and boundary alterations. A corporation is a body corporate, with perpetual succession and powers to hold property and sue or be sued. Ward delimitation and elections operate through the Act and the Haryana Municipal Corporation Delimitation of Ward Rules, 1994 and Election Rules, 1994. MCM ward boundaries must be read from its notified wardbandi, not inferred from engineering divisions.

| Provision | Function and practical distinction |
| --- | --- |
| Section 36 | Mayor and deputy mayor framework; use amended election provisions rather than assuming the original election method remains unchanged |
| Section 41 | Municipal administration vests in the Corporation; it considers receipt/disbursement statements and progress reports |
| Section 45 | Appointment of Commissioner by the State Government |
| Section 49 | Executive power vests in Commissioner, subject to the Act and general supervision/control of the Corporation |
| Section 50 | Corporation may require records, returns and reports from Commissioner, subject to statutory qualifications |
| Section 51 | Expenditure powers remain subject to budget provision and required sanction |

The elected Corporation deliberates, adopts resolutions and exercises statutory budgetary oversight. The Commissioner administers implementation, supervises municipal staff and exercises powers specifically vested in that office. The Mayor's representative and meeting role does not make the Mayor interchangeable with the Commissioner. Conversely, executive authority does not eliminate House oversight or approval requirements.

## Civic duties: Sections 42, 43 and 44

Section 42 concerns functions that the Government may entrust to the Corporation. Its list includes urban planning, land-use/building regulation, roads, water supply, public health, sanitation and solid waste, fire services, urban forestry, weaker-section welfare, slum improvement, parks, burial/cremation facilities, vital statistics and public amenities.

Section 43 contains the obligatory functions. Its core requirements include drains and public conveniences, water-supply works, scavenging and refuse disposal, abatement of unhealthy conditions and nuisances, and securing or removing dangerous buildings and places. It requires adequate provision using lawful municipal measures.

Section 44, not Section 42, addresses discretionary functions. These permit additional civic and welfare activity within the statutory framework. Describing Sections 42 and 43 as the obligatory/discretionary pair would misstate the Act. A function listed in the statute also does not prove that MCM currently maintains every related asset. GMDA, HSIIDC, HSVP and private developers can retain responsibility under separate law, vesting or handover arrangements.

## Taxes, accounts and property assessment

Chapter VII concerns revenue and expenditure; Chapter VIII begins with Section 87, on taxes imposed by the Corporation and arrangements for certain taxes collected by Government. Section 88 deals with fees. Sections 89 to 93 address land/building taxation, water/fire taxation, valuation and incidence. The following sections cover assessment lists, notices and changes involving properties.

The Act authorizes the system. The current State notifications, tax policies, rates and assessment procedure determine an individual demand. A property ID, municipal tax receipt or sanitation bill is not by itself building-plan approval, colony regularization or proof of title. A citizen disputing a demand should obtain the assessment particulars, applicable rate notification, calculation and prescribed objection/appeal route. Do not substitute a generic tax slab from another Haryana municipality for Manesar's operative demand.

## Buildings, encroachments and public health

Chapter XIV is the building-regulation chapter. Section 250 prohibits erection without sanction; Section 254 addresses sanction/refusal; Section 256 concerns sanction obtained by misrepresentation. Section 261 provides demolition/stoppage powers and appeal, Section 262 stoppage, Section 263 alteration, Section 264 completion certification and Sections 265 to 266 dangerous buildings and vacation. Enforcement must follow applicable notice, hearing, appeal and court-order requirements; this summary does not authorize immediate demolition in a particular case.

Street obstruction and encroachment provisions must be read with the ownership of the affected road or land. Section 408 prohibits unauthorized encroachment or obstruction on corporation land, while Section 408A supplies an eviction framework for unauthorized occupation. Municipal enforcement is not a blanket authority over land vested in another agency.

Chapter XV covers sanitation and public health. Municipal bye-laws and delegated rules work alongside national solid-waste/plastic rules and pollution-control law. A bye-law's existence, commencement and penalty schedule should be verified rather than inferred from a general public-health duty.

## State oversight and source register

Haryana's Urban Local Bodies Department administers the municipal framework through legislation, rules, notifications and supervision authorized by the Act. State powers over boundaries, appointments and prescribed approvals constrain municipal discretion. Statutory State oversight and judicial review are separate from the municipal complaint process.

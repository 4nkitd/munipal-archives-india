# Municipal budgets and finances of Manesar

## Scope and documentary status

This record separates proposed budgets, House approvals, expenditure reported during a year and audited accounts. They are not interchangeable. The official officer directory verifies the accounts and audit contacts above, but does not publish the four annual budget schedules summarized here. The budget figures below come from identified contemporary newspaper reports quoting municipal officials. They are secondary evidence, not certified municipal budget documents. The record date is the House meeting of 16 March 2026, as reported the following day.

One crore rupees equals Rs 10,000,000. No amount below should be treated as an audited year-end collection or payment unless explicitly identified as such. No authenticated HSIIDC transfer, external development charges (EDC) transfer or stamp-duty sharing percentage for these four years was established from the sources consulted.

The March 2026 report describes a budget passed during the annual budget cycle, but its retrieved text does not explicitly label the financial year. Its attribution to the 2026-27 cycle is chronological, not a substitute for the signed cover sheet.

Later reporting by Times of India on 8 April 2025 describes the preceding year's allocations as Rs 506 crore expenditure and Rs 469 crore income, rather than the Rs 505 crore and Rs 470 crore proposal. These are different reported stages or rounded figures. This archive does not silently reconcile them.

## Revenue sources and collection evidence

The February 2024 reporting places the 2024-25 property-tax target at Rs 70 crore, planning receipts at Rs 54 crore, user charges at Rs 11 crore, advertisements at Rs 6 crore and water/sewer charges at Rs 2 crore. Planning receipts include building-plan approvals and land-sale receipts; they must not automatically be relabelled EDC or HSIIDC transfers.

Jagran's April 2025 report identifies anticipated 2025-26 stamp-duty income of Rs 150 crore, property-tax income of Rs 37 crore, State Finance Commission-related grants of Rs 46 crore and Central Finance Commission-related grants of Rs 10 crore. These are estimates, not collections. The report's terminology for the finance bodies is paraphrased here rather than treated as a grant sanction.

The February 2024 reports give Rs 195 crore income and Rs 134 crore expenditure against 2023-24 estimates, but publication preceded 31 March. Those are in-year snapshots, not full-year actuals. Times of India then reported Rs 276 crore income and Rs 369 crore expenditure for 2024-25 on 8 April 2025. These remain reported figures pending the signed accounts.

## Works, sanitation and water provisions

| Budget stage | Reported allocation |
| --- | --- |
| 2024-25 proposal | Roads/bridges Rs 200 crore; buildings Rs 40 crore; sewer lines Rs 25 crore; water pipelines Rs 20 crore |
| 2024-25 proposal | Parks Rs 10 crore; micro-STPs Rs 15 crore; sanitation Rs 121 crore |
| 2025-26 House proposal | Development works about Rs 200 crore; sanitation about Rs 115 crore; operation and maintenance Rs 56 crore |
| March 2026 House budget | Development works Rs 480 crore; sanitation and waste management Rs 86 crore |

Jagran also mentions a sanitation discussion revising Rs 105 crore to Rs 116 crore. Since its summary says Rs 115 crore, obtain the adopted schedule before relying on a precise sanitation total. The headings above may overlap and should not be added to reconstruct a budget.

## Official accounts and obtaining missing records

An official DULB-hosted financial statement for 2021-22 states that income and expenditure were accounted for on a cash basis. Its notes report excess income over expenditure of Rs 47.53 crore, bank balance including cash in hand of Rs 299.72 crore at 31 March 2022 and treatment of balances received from gram panchayats as capital balance. These historical notes do not establish the accounting policy or available cash in later years.

Ask the Chief Account Officer for the signed original and revised budget, approval resolution and State sanction for each year. Request separate ledgers for property tax, stamp duty, HSIIDC receipts, EDC, grants, capital works, sanitation and water. The audit branch can identify audit observations and compliance replies. An RTI request should specify the year and demand both budget estimates and actual receipt/payment statements, with opening and closing balances.

# Manesar environmental monitoring, GRAP and sanitation initiatives

## AQI evidence and NCR applicability

Manesar lies in Gurugram district within the National Capital Region. CAQM's Graded Response Action Plan applies across the NCR, subject to each measure's stated geographic scope. Its revised schedule dated 29 September 2026 classifies stages by Delhi AQI and forecast conditions:

| Stage | Delhi AQI band in the revised schedule | Description |
| --- | --- | --- |
| I | 201 to 300 | Poor |
| II | 301 to 400 | Very Poor |
| III | 401 to 450 | Severe |
| IV | Above 450 | Severe+ |

CAQM can invoke stages in advance on forecast deterioration, and higher stages operate cumulatively with lower-stage measures. A resident's nearby AQI reading does not independently switch NCR-wide restrictions on or off. Check both the schedule and the latest invocation/revocation order. The record date identifies the revised schedule, not a declaration that any stage is active on the verification date.

MCM's homepage carries an "Air Quality Index" heading, but the fetched page did not expose a timestamped value or identify its station. This archive therefore reports no live or annual Manesar AQI. When recording readings, preserve the station name, operator, pollutant, observation time and averaging basis. A Delhi city-average figure must not be labelled Manesar AQI.

## GRAP measures and local enforcement evidence

The revised schedule requires the relevant NCR States, pollution-control boards, local bodies and development authorities to enforce measures within their jurisdiction when invoked. Municipal work includes dust control, road cleaning and prevention of waste burning, alongside the construction, transport and industrial restrictions specified for the applicable stage. Exact exemptions and geographic limits should be read in the current order rather than generalized from an older winter notice.

MCM's official homepage lists "Implementation of GRAP Stage 3 in Delhi NCR" dated 11 November 2025. This is historical evidence of municipal circulation, not proof that the 2025 order remains active. CAQM subsequently revised the schedule in September 2026.

A CAQM press release of 17 November 2026 records revocation of Stage III invoked on 6 November. It says Stages I and II remained in force and notes separate restrictions for 5 to 20 November under a 30 October order. That is a dated snapshot, not a claim about the live position on 22 November or later. Specific closure orders on non-compliant sites also require separate attention.

## Swachh Survekshan and zero-waste activity

Swachh Survekshan is MoHUA's urban-cleanliness assessment. Its official 2024-25 portal describes assessment of ULB submissions, documents and field conditions, together with Garbage Free City and sanitation certifications. Those national assessment criteria do not establish Manesar's participation, score or certification.

No Manesar-specific official result row, verified annual rank, GFC star certificate or ODF/Water+ certificate was established from the sources reviewed. Gurugram's result must not be copied to Manesar, which is a separate corporation. Similarly, Manasa and Mansa are different cities, not alternative spellings for a Manesar result.

MCM publishes "Say No to Plastic" material including "Zero Waste Steps" and "Zero Waste Tips", and provides a live sweeping-machine-feed service link. Its October 2026 BWG RFP is further documentary evidence of waste-service procurement. These verify awareness and monitoring/procurement activity, not a measured citywide zero-waste achievement or a formally verified scheme titled "Zero Waste Manesar".

## Ponds, wastewater and waterlogging

Hindustan Times reported on 17 March 2026 that the municipal House approved beautification of village ponds/johads and chaupals, along with micro-STPs on available municipal land and infrastructure including roads, sewers and water pipelines. These are reported approvals. The article does not certify completion of every pond, identify every rejuvenation site or provide before-and-after water-quality results. No village-wide rejuvenation count is supplied here.

For a pond project, request the pond's village/khasra, ownership, sanctioned estimate, tender/work order, wastewater interception arrangement, desilting record and completion certificate. Beautification alone does not demonstrate ecological restoration or safe water quality.

Waterlogging involves connected assets. MCM may maintain local drains, while GMDA, HSIIDC, HSVP, highway agencies or a developer may control the receiving network. A complaint should name the exact junction or street, rainfall/date, duration, blocked inlet or outfall and whether sewage is mixed with stormwater. This review did not establish an official ranked list of Manesar waterlogging hotspots.

## Dumping hotspots and urban greenery

Times of India's 16 November 2025 report identifies four garbage hotspots discussed by MCM: near Hayatpur in Sector 92, in IMT Sector 6, near Greenberg Society in Sector 81 and near Shikohpur in Sector 79. It reports proposed boundary walls and guards. These are garbage-dumping locations, not a verified list of flood hotspots, and the article does not establish that the proposed works were completed.

The same report assigns IMT Sectors 1 to 8 green-belt maintenance to HSIIDC under a reported agreement. MCM's official officer directory separately verifies an SBM/Horticulture Executive Engineer and Horticulture Assistant Engineer. Contact the authority maintaining the particular park or verge before assuming all greenery inside the municipal boundary is an MCM asset.

No verified annual plantation count, survival rate, canopy-cover percentage or horticulture expenditure outcome is asserted. For evaluating a planting drive, obtain a location-wise planting register and survival inspection, not only the number of saplings distributed.

# Nuh, Taoru and Manesar corridor: development plans, industrial estates and Aravalli safeguards

## Plan identity and the 2031 versus 2041 distinction

The Haryana Town and Country Planning Department's development-plan register lists Nuh under the older district heading "Mewat". It records the following instruments. These are town and controlled-area plans, not a single land-use approval covering all of Nuh district. Taoru also appears as Tarou in the register and is commonly written Tauru.

| Planning area | Instrument and perspective year | Notification reference | Date |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Nuh | Draft Development Plan 2021 | CCP(NCR)/DDP/GGN(NUH)/2006/2861 | 18 September 2006 |
| Nuh | Draft Development Plan 2031 | CCP(NCR)/DDP/NUH-2031/2019/117 | 11 January 2019 |
| Nuh | Final Development Plan 2031 | CCP(NCR)/FDP/NUH-2031/2019/1306 | 2 July 2019 |
| Taoru | Revised Draft Development Plan 2021 | CCP(NCR)/DDP/TRU/2018/873 | 9 April 2018 |
| Taoru | Final Development Plan 2021 | CCP(NCR)/FDP/TRU/2019/1387 | 10 July 2019 |

The register lists two controlled areas for each town. Its Taoru final-plan date contains a stray greater-than symbol before 10/07/2019. The reference above preserves the date without that formatting error. The frontmatter date is the Nuh final-plan notification date, not the date of construction or completion of corridor infrastructure.

No Nuh-specific 2041 development-plan notification was identified in the official register reviewed for this record. The NCR Planning Board's December 2021 document is explicitly titled **Draft Regional Plan 2041**. It concerns the wider National Capital Region and must not be described as a notified Nuh Master Plan 2041. Its regional proposals are not a substitute for a parcel's applicable Haryana development plan, zoning regulations or permissions. [1][2]

## Delhi-Mumbai Expressway and the KMP connection

The Union government's Bharatmala factsheet identifies a cloverleaf interchange between the Delhi-Mumbai Expressway and the Kundli-Manesar-Palwal Expressway at **Khalilpur village in Nuh district**. This is the verified location to use when describing the expressway-to-expressway connection. It should not be relabelled as an interchange inside Nuh municipal town merely because it lies in the same district. [3]

The Prime Minister dedicated the 246 km Delhi-Dausa-Lalsot section of the Delhi-Mumbai Expressway on **12 February 2023**. The PIB release records a development cost exceeding **Rs 12,150 crore** for that section. Those figures refer to the entire inaugurated section, not the Khalilpur interchange alone or a Nuh municipal project. [4]

KMP provides the regional road connection toward Manesar; the Sohna-Nuh road and the Taoru road network connect local settlements and industrial areas to the wider corridor. However, expressway access is controlled. Adjacency to an expressway does not establish a lawful plot entrance, a service-road connection, industrial zoning or permission to subdivide agricultural land.

For a proposed warehouse, factory or housing project, obtain the relevant controlled-area boundary, land-use designation and applicable access approval. A road crossing, an interchange and a permitted private access are different things. Neither the 2023 opening announcement nor the Bharatmala factsheet establishes the current access arrangements for a particular property.

## IMT Sohna and Roz Ka Meo industrial area

The Sohna Final Development Plan 2031 was notified on **15 November 2012**, reference **CCP(NCR)/FDP-2031/GGN-SHN/2012/3543**. The official register also links amendments dated **8 December 2016** and **5 February 2018**. Its industrial proposals belong to the Sohna planning framework and should not be extended automatically to land elsewhere in Nuh district. [1]

The explanatory note to the 2012 plan distinguishes the existing **Rozka Meo industrial area and Textile Valley**, approximately **89 hectares** on the Sohna-Nuh road, from the larger industrial development proposed along KMP. It allocates approximately **1,236 hectares** to industrial use and records that HSIIDC had acquired **607 hectares** along KMP for an Industrial Model Township. These are historical plan figures, not a statement that every hectare has since been developed or allotted. [5]

The same note describes the acquired IMT land as low-lying and flood-prone, citing the Irrigation Department and HSIIDC. It requires special measures for urbanisation. It also discusses runoff from the western and north-western Aravallis, the water recharge zone and drainage toward Indri lake through Mehandwara Nadi. Industrial planning here therefore includes drainage constraints as well as road connectivity.

HSIIDC's GIS portal lists **Sohna**, **Roz ka Meo** and **Manesar** as separate estate selections. Use the actual estate, plot number, acquisition boundary, approved layout and allotment documents when identifying a site. The Council for Leather Exports' 25 September 2017 circular describes a proposed **105-acre non-tannery Mega Leather Cluster** at IMT Sohna, formerly called IMT Rojka Meo. That historical proposal is not proof of current construction, occupancy or available plots. [6][7]

## Aravalli continuity adjoining the Manesar side

Research published in 2023 describes an Aravalli ridge running from **Manesar through Naurangpur, Sakatpur, Gairatpur, Bhondsi and Sohna into Nuh district**, where it continues parallel to the Alwar road. This provides regional ecological context for the southern Manesar and Sohna approaches. It does not establish a separately gazetted wildlife corridor with a uniform legal boundary across all these villages. No such corridor notification was verified for this record. [8]

The **7 May 1992 Aravalli Notification, S.O. 319(E)** restricts specified activities in the areas described in its schedule. These include new industries and their expansion or modernisation, tree cutting, specified construction and associated roads, and new transmission lines, subject to the applicable permission regime. Its land categories include forest entries and specified hill or related revenue classifications, including Gair Mumkin Pahar. The notification refers to the then Gurgaon district; a present-day district label alone is insufficient to decide whether a parcel falls within its scope. [9]

Forest status and Punjab Land Preservation Act notifications require a separate check. In its **11 September 2018** Kant Enclave judgment in M.C. Mehta v. Union of India, the Supreme Court treated the notified land at issue as forest land and held the construction unlawful. That judgment concerned Faridabad land, not a finding about every parcel in Nuh or Manesar. It demonstrates why a planning permission cannot be treated as a substitute for the applicable forest-law requirements. [10]

For a corridor proposal, obtain khasra-level land records, the relevant forest and PLPA notification schedules, the applicable conservation-zone map, and drainage information. Check these with the District Town Planner, revenue authorities and Forest Department for the actual site. This record does not assign forest status or development rights to whole villages.

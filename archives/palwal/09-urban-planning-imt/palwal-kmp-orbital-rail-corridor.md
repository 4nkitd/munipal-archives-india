# Palwal-Prithla development plans, KMP and Haryana Orbital Rail Corridor

## Development plan identity and notification history

The Haryana Town and Country Planning Department's development-plan index lists an Integrated Final Development Plan for Palwal-Prithla with perspective year 2041. Its notification is **CCP(NCR)/FDP-2041/Palwal-Prithla/2026/1553 dated 24 August 2026**. The index links both the gazette notification and the final plan drawing. This is a final plan, not merely the March 2024 draft. [1]

The phrase "Palwal Development Plan 2031" needs care. The official index records Palwal town's earlier final plan under 2021, Prithla's under 2031, and the integrated Palwal-Prithla plan under 2041. It does not identify the Prithla 2031 plan as a Palwal-town 2031 plan. Hodal's separately listed 2031 plan must also not be substituted for Palwal-Prithla.

| Prithla | 2031 final development plan | CCP(NCR)IFDP/(PAL)/2011/886, 7 April 2011; amendment 17 April 2018 |

UNI's 26 August 2026 report describes the final plan as covering Palwal, Prithla and nearby controlled areas, with a planned population of 10,56,645 by 2041 and nearly 8,856 hectares allocated across housing, industry, business, transport and other uses. These figures are secondary-source context, not a substitute for the gazette's land-use schedule. [2]

The official index and document links were checked for this record. The final gazette PDF and drawing were not text-extracted or visually interpreted, so no parcel zoning, sector boundaries or road-reservation widths are certified here.

## Haryana Orbital Rail Corridor route and connections

HRIDC describes the Haryana Orbital Rail Corridor as a broad-gauge double railway line for passenger and freight traffic connecting **Palwal to Sonipat via Sohna, Manesar and Kharkhoda**, bypassing Delhi. It is not a Palwal-to-Manesar-only railway. The project follows much of the KMP corridor and connects radial railway routes around Delhi. [3]

HRIDC's October 2025 equity expression of interest describes a new electrified line of approximately **126 km**, with **17 new stations**, a maximum design speed of **160 km/h**, and high-rise overhead electrification suitable for double-stack container traffic. The document specifies connections to the Dedicated Freight Corridor at New Prithla and New Tauru, and Indian Railways at Palwal through New Prithla, Patli, Sultanpur, Asaudah and Harsana Kalan. [4]

For Palwal district, the New Prithla connection is the important interface between HORC, the Western Dedicated Freight Corridor and the Delhi-Mathura railway network. The older DPR also describes connectivity to Asaoti through the Prithla DFC yard. Station names and connection arrangements should be checked against the latest approved drawings rather than treated as a passenger timetable. [5]

Project figures changed during development. The 2021 HRIDC presentation gives 121.742 km and 143.932 km including connections. The February 2025 ESIA addendum gives 125.98 km of new line plus 17.68 km of connections, totalling 143.66 km. The October 2025 EOI rounds the main line to 126 km. These are document-specific figures, not interchangeable measurements. [4][6][7]

The CCEA sanctioned HORC on 15 September 2020. The cited sources establish project approval, design and implementation documentation. They do not establish that the full Palwal-Sonipat corridor or a Palwal passenger service was operational on the verification date.

## KMP, Delhi-Mumbai Expressway and Jewar airport connectivity

Kundli-Manesar-Palwal Expressway is the Western Peripheral Expressway. It provides the western road bypass around Delhi, with Palwal at its southern end. A parliamentary reply dated 22 March 2018 stated that the Manesar-Palwal section had already been completed. PIB records the inauguration of the Kundli-Manesar section on **19 November 2018**. These are historical completion and inauguration records, separate from HORC construction. [8][9]

The Jewar airport connector is another distinct project. On **10 March 2026**, CCEA approved a revised total capital cost of **Rs. 3,630.77 crore** for greenfield connectivity to Jewar International Airport from the Delhi-Faridabad-Ballabhgarh-Sohna spur of the Delhi-Mumbai Expressway. PIB gives its length as **31.42 km**, with about **11 km** to be elevated. Haryana agreed to bear **Rs. 450 crore** of the elevated-corridor cost. [10]

A PIB update dated **19 May 2026** gives the more precise length of **31.425 km**, describing a six-lane, access-controlled road from Chandawali village in Faridabad to near Dayanapur in Gautam Buddha Nagar. It identifies connections with the Eastern Peripheral Expressway and Yamuna Expressway, as well as the DND-Sohna highway and a DFC crossing. [11]

These routes connect the wider Palwal-Faridabad industrial area to regional road and freight networks. The airport connector is not a road starting at Palwal railway station, and KMP is not another name for the Delhi-Mumbai Expressway. An expressway or railway crossing a locality does not establish a local entry ramp, freight siding or passenger stop. The May 2026 PIB release describes the airport connector as under construction; this record does not certify subsequent opening.

## Prithla-Dudhola industrial cluster

The Union MSME Ministry's district industrial profile lists manufacturing addresses at Prithla, Dudhola and Tatarpur Road. Examples include Action Construction Equipments at Dudhola for forklift trucks, HPL Additives at Dudhola for antioxidants, Venus Industrial Corporation at Prithla for sheet-metal components, and Dee Development Engineers on Tatarpur Road, Prithla, for fabricated metal products. This is a historical industrial profile, not a verified current business directory. [12]

The Tribune reported on **10 November 2023** that 43 CLU permissions had been issued in Palwal since 1 January that year for industrial and commercial units. Over 90 percent were reportedly industrial or manufacturing permissions. The report names Prithla, Tatarpur, Dudhola, Bhagola, Gadpuri and Aurangabad as locations where entrepreneurs had acquired land. It attributed a figure of more than 300 functioning units within a 10 to 15 km radius to district-administration sources. These are dated reported figures, not a 2026 census. [13]

The same report described survey work for an HSIIDC dry-port proposal near Prithla, based on an unnamed official's statement. It does not establish a commissioned dry port. Existing manufacturing, a proposed logistics development and a railway interchange are different types of infrastructure.

Infrastructure responsibility also needs a site-specific check. The Tribune's **29 November 2023** report recorded complaints about water, drainage, roads and streetlights. It quoted the District Industries Centre deputy director saying the area did not belong to HSIIDC and identifying PWD and Public Health departments as responsible for civic amenities. Do not assume the entire cluster is a serviced HSIIDC industrial estate or an operational IMT merely because the archive category includes "imt". [14]

## Using the record for a land or infrastructure enquiry

For a property enquiry, obtain the village, revenue estate, khasra numbers and current municipal or controlled-area jurisdiction first. Compare them with the notified final plan and amendments, then check the parcel's CLU or colony licence and applicable building permissions with DTCP. A development-plan land-use colour is not an individual construction permission.

For a railway or highway enquiry, use the acquiring authority's notification and alignment schedule. Check acquisition boundaries, access restrictions and sanctioned connections separately from surrounding land-use proposals. No ward assignment is inferred here because these corridors and industrial villages extend beyond a single municipal ward.

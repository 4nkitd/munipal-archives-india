# City of Panaji Corporation Act, 2002

## Enactment and territory

The *Manual of Goa Laws* identifies this law as Goa Act 1 of 2003, assented to on 21 January 2003 and published in the *Official Gazette*, Series I No. 43, on 23 January 2003. The short title says 2002. The notification reproduced in the manual has a 2002 dateline that conflicts with the manual's Gazette citation and the stated assent date. The archive uses the Gazette publication date listed by the manual.

Sections 1 and 2 refer to Schedule I, allow commencement by notification and set out the transition from municipal council or panchayat law. Schedule I starts with the former Panaji municipal area and specifies exceptions, including Durgawadi survey numbers 1 to 7 and most Taleigao survey numbers, with specified Taleigao parcels retained. It also lists other areas. The manual includes a separate 13 February 2003 notice inviting views before the government issued a notification under section 1. Neither the proposed application of the Act in that notice nor the Schedule I place names alone establish today's limits. Section 360 provides a Gazette process for additions, with consideration of objections.

For ward-level address checks in the 2026 election, use the [2 February 2026 delimitation notification](https://sec.goa.gov.in/assets/media/docs/general-election-to-corporation-of-city-of-panaji-2026/2526-44-SII-EOG-4-2.pdf). The corporation is not the district or an Assembly constituency.

## Municipal authorities and functions

Sections 6 and 7 name the Corporation, Standing Committee and Commissioner as the municipal authorities and establish the corporate name as the Corporation of the City of Panaji. The manual's amended section 9 provides for 30 councillors directly elected at ward elections and five councillors nominated by state notification. The Director of Municipal Administration, with state approval, specifies ward numbers and boundaries by Gazette notification. Under section 11, the State Election Commissioner directs electoral roll preparation and the conduct of municipal elections. Section 17 provides for a five-year term from the date appointed for the first meeting, unless the Corporation is sooner dissolved.

Section 59 covers duties such as public street cleaning, waste disposal, public markets, maintenance of municipal streets and drains, and registration of births and deaths. Its proviso allows specified duties to remain with or be vested in other state authorities or departments. The list of statutory duties is therefore not a directory of which agency handles every service at a particular address.

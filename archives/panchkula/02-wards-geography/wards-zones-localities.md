# Panchkula wards, sectors, villages and colony geography

## Controlling order and scope

Haryana Urban Local Bodies Department order No. 04/53/2025-4C1 dated 30 December 2025 publishes Panchkula's final ward delimitation under rule 10 of the Haryana Municipal Corporation Delimitation of Ward Rules, 1994. It refers to the preliminary notification of 15 December 2025. The schedule must be read with drawing No. 17647/MCPKL dated 9 December 2025. The department's notification index lists the final order on 31 December 2025; that upload date is not the order date.

The accompanying `wards.json` contains all 20 wards from this final schedule. It is a locality index, not a parcel-level map. A sector or village appearing in two entries can be divided by a ward line. No numbered administrative zones or engineering divisions have been established from this order, so those JSON fields are `null`. Geographic descriptions below are not official zone names.

The district's older wardbandi page still links a 14 August 2020 document. Do not mix its ward numbers with the 2025 final order. The district published a separate final municipal electoral-roll page on 28 March 2026 with ward-wise booth links. Use that roll and the statutory drawing to resolve individual addresses.

## HSVP sectors, MDC and the Ghaggar

Panchkula's planned-sector geography and municipal ward geography are separate systems. Haryana Shehri Vikas Pradhikaran is abbreviated HSVP; older boundary descriptions use HUDA. The sector labels within the familiar 1 to 28 planning range do not establish 28 electoral units. Sector 12A has its own label. The final schedule does not provide a standalone locality assignment for Sector 13 or Sector 22, so neither receives an invented JSON mapping. Sector 22 occurs in Ward 13's boundary narrative as an adjoining reference. The schedule also explicitly includes Sectors 30 and 31 in Ward 17, outside the requested 1 to 28 range.

Mansa Devi Complex uses a separate sector namespace. MDC Sector 4 must not be merged with city Sector 4; MDC Sector 6 is not city Sector 6. Ward 1 contains MDC Sectors 2, 4 and 6, while Ward 2 contains MDC Sector 5. The Ward 2 closing sentence omits city Sector 6, although its perimeter follows the Sector 7/6 and Sector 6/5 roads and returns along Sector 6, military and Command Hospital boundaries. The JSON includes Sector 6 with that qualification rather than silently treating the source as internally complete.

Sectors 20 and 21 form the southern extension toward Punjab. They should not be treated as physically identical to the east-bank Trans-Ghaggar belt. Both sectors are split: Sector 20 between Wards 14 and 15, and Sector 21 between Wards 13 and 14. The east-bank sectors commonly described as Trans-Ghaggar are covered here by Ward 17 for Sectors 23 to 25 and Ward 18 for Sectors 26 to 28. The Ghaggar bridges and river boundary occur repeatedly in their legal perimeter descriptions.

## Industrial areas and village belts

Industrial Area Phases I and II are locality names, not sector-number substitutes. Ward 10 expressly includes Phase I, Abheypur and Sector 14. For Ward 9, the English closing sentence prints "Industrial Area Phase-I", but the Hindi closing sentence identifies Phase II. Contemporary December 2025 reporting also assigns Phase II to Ward 9. This archive records Phase II and flags the discrepancy; the attached drawing remains necessary for a disputed industrial plot.

The incorporated village network crosses the planned sectors. Saketri is in Ward 1; Budhanpur in Ward 8; Abheypur in Ward 10; Rally and Railla in Ward 11; Maheshpur in Ward 14. Kharak Mangoli is split between Wards 16 and 17. Nada, associated with Nada Sahib, is named in Ward 17, not carried forward from an older Ward 16 assignment.

The southeast extends through Ramgarh and the Kot belt toward Barwala. Ward 19 covers the Ramgarh, Billa, Bhanu, Jaswantgarh, Mankya and Dabkori group. Ward 20 covers Kot and eight other named villages. Its boundary starts at the Jalauli-Barwala junction with the municipal limit. That does not establish that Barwala town itself belongs to Ward 20.

Spellings vary in the official text: Saketri/Saketari, Bhainsa Tibba/Bhaisa Tibba, Kharak/Kharag/Khadaq Mangoli, Beed/Bir Ghaggar, Billa/Bila, Mankya/Manakya, Dabkori/Dabkauri and Nichli/Nijhwali Kundi. The English Ward 17 summary separates Naggal and Moginand, while the Hindi text groups Naggal Moginand. Ward 20 also names Naggal. Do not deduplicate these entries or identify a revenue estate from spelling alone.

## Colonies and regularization status

Colony names in the ward schedule establish electoral geography, not title, layout approval, building permission or regularization. This distinction applies to Mahadev Colony, Rajiv Colony, Indira Colony, Madrasi Colony and Krishi Colony. Aashiana housing appears in both Wards 10 and 15 at different locations. Tribune Mitra Vihar, ITBP Colony and TBRL Colony also have separate ward entries.

The Haryana ULB regularized-colony portal publishes notification lists and polygon schedules. A matching district name is insufficient: Kalka municipal council colonies can appear under Panchkula district without belonging to Panchkula municipal corporation. No Panchkula MC colony is labelled regularized in this record without a matched notification and polygon.

A 23 January 2026 report stated that the latest batch of 26 regularized colonies included none from Panchkula district and that the earlier one-marla plot proposal had not received approval. It named Gandhi Colony, Indira Colony, Rajiv Colony and Kharak Mangoli among settlements awaiting resolution. This is a dated report, not proof that no later notification exists. For a particular property, obtain the colony notification number, approved polygon, property identification and competent-authority confirmation. Municipal services or electoral registration alone do not prove regularization.

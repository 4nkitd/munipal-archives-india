# Panchkula tenders and infrastructure works, 2024 to 2026

## Tender portals and interpretation

The Haryana Engineering Works Portal states that tenders floated through HEWP are published on the NIC Haryana e-Tendering website for bidder participation. Its live-tender listing is updated the next day after publication on NIC. Search using the exact tender ID and check the organisation chain. Municipal Corporation Panchkula, PMDA, HSVP and other agencies issue separate contracts for works located in Panchkula. A Panchkula location does not establish municipal corporation ownership. [S1]

The amounts below are tender estimates unless expressly described as a reported sanction, project cost or award. Publication and bid-opening dates do not establish award, commencement or completion. Work periods are contractual durations in the listing, not verified completion dates. Historical schedules must not be treated as current invitations.

This is a selected archive, not a complete procurement register. Official portal records provide the strongest tender evidence here. Rows sourced to commercial tender indexes are explicitly identified as secondary listings. Their rounded amounts are retained as published rather than converted into invented exact rupee values. No completion certificate, measurement book or payment record was verified for the listed engineering tenders.

## Jhuriwala waste remediation and processing infrastructure

The Tribune reported on 26 April 2024 that MC officials said bioremediation of more than 90,837 metric tonnes of legacy waste at Jhuriwala had been completed. The report quotes MC SDO Manoj Ahlawat as giving 23 April 2024 as the completion date. It describes the site as four acres and records a plan to restore it through planting trees, shrubs and grass. This is an attributed completion report, not an independently inspected completion certificate. [S9]

Jhuriwala should not be described as a verified commissioned solid-waste processing plant on this evidence. The same April 2024 report says the corporation had been functioning without a waste-processing plant. In April 2025, officials described the cleared Jhuriwala site as a waste-recovery facility. A May 2025 report then said the MC had told the National Green Tribunal that no MRF existed there and that incoming waste was cleared the same day. The descriptions differ and are retained with their dates rather than reconciled into a single plant-status claim. [S9, S10, S11]

The May 2025 report identifies a separate MRF at Alipur on 3.5 acres. It reports construction expenditure of about Rs 2 crore and approval of nearly Rs 1 crore for a boundary wall. The facility was reported almost ready, not operational. Waste was intended to be segregated there before transfer to the Patvi processing plant in Ambala. These are reported infrastructure arrangements and approximate costs, not verified tender awards or commissioning figures. [S11]

## Sector 23 dumping-ground biomining

On 1 April 2025, The Times of India reported commencement of another bioremediation phase at Sector 23. Its published figures were 1.70 lakh tonnes of remaining waste and Rs 11.50 crore project expenditure, with the phase assigned to the existing contractor. It reported that approximately 3.72 lakh metric tonnes had been cleared under the earlier contract over three years. The stated clearance deadline was 31 August 2025. [S10]

A separate Hindustan Times report of 2 April 2025 also gives Rs 11.5 crore for the reallocated tender, but quotes councillor Akshay Deep Chaudhary as saying a government drone survey found approximately 2.5 lakh metric tonnes remaining at a ten-acre site. These quantities conflict with the 1.70 lakh-tonne figure in the other report. They are not combined into a single verified balance or used to calculate a unit rate. [S12]

The Times of India reported on 26 December 2025 that the corporation had missed both the August deadline and a later 25 December deadline, with waste still remaining. Accordingly, the April commencement and announced deadlines do not establish that Sector 23 was cleared. No later verified completion record is included here. [S13]

## Citywide LED streetlight programme

The Times of India reported on 24 June 2024 that MC Panchkula proposed replacing 18,500 conventional streetlights with LEDs and installing command, control and monitoring system panels. The report gives administrative and financial sanction of Rs 9.90 crore for the programme under Divya Nagar Yojana, with half the amount to be borne by the Urban Local Bodies department. It separately mentions cable-laying procurement. This is a programme sanction reported in the press, not the value of the park-lighting contract above. [S14]

The planned citywide quantity and sanctioned amount do not prove installation of every light or operation of the control centre. The source described tender allotment as forthcoming. No final installation count, energy saving or commissioning date is asserted in this record.

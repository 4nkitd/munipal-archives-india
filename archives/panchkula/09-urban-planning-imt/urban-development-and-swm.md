# Panchkula development jurisdictions, Ghaggar ecology and solid waste performance

## Metropolitan area and institutional limits

PMDA's official “About PMDA” page, published 10 June 2022, states that the authority was established under Haryana Act No. 23 of 2021 through notification dated 9 September 2021. It describes a metropolitan area of 388.06 square kilometres, 143 villages and the main towns of Panchkula, Pinjore, Kalka and Barwala. This is not a statement of MC Panchkula's municipal boundary.

The page assigns PMDA integrated planning, infrastructure development, transport and traffic coordination, urban amenities and sustainable management of the urban environment, in coordination with MC Panchkula, HSVP and HSIIDC. Its population paragraph refers to a “2021 census”. That attribution is not reproduced here as a verified census count.

No consolidated, current, asset-by-asset handover register was retrieved. The matrix below is a routing guide based on official institutional descriptions and dated reporting. It is not a legal determination that every road, park or sewer in a named sector belongs to one agency.

## PMDA, MC and HSVP jurisdiction matrix

| Matter | MC Panchkula | PMDA | HSVP | Evidence or caution |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Municipal budget and property tax | Municipal budget and tax administration | Separate authority budget | Separate estate finances | MC and PMDA allocations must remain separate |
| Door-to-door collection and local sanitation | Municipal collection and sanitation responsibilities | Coordination and infrastructure interface | Sanitation continued in some retained sectors in July 2025 | A proposal to transfer service is not proof of a completed handover |
| Metropolitan roads, drainage and green infrastructure | Local assets and works within its assigned responsibility | Metropolitan projects, major roads, drainage and green-space works | Developer and maintainer of retained estate assets | Check work order and transfer record for each asset |
| Plots, allottee accounts and estate development | Municipal functions do not replace HSVP allottee administration | Regional infrastructure coordination | Acquisition, development, disposal and estate administration | HSVP's official historical-background page describes these functions |
| Building matters | Relevant municipal jurisdiction and permissions | Do not presume PMDA is the universal building-plan authority | Estate construction controls and allottee matters | Determine land ownership, estate status and competent authority first |
| Ghaggar riverfront and protection works | Waste collection and dumping complaints within assigned limits | Reported riverfront planning and riverbank protection projects | Earlier riverfront sponsor and developer of some bridges | River regulation and pollution enforcement also involve other departments |

Hindustan Times reported on 24 July 2025 that HSVP had requested MC to take over cleanliness in additional areas, including trans-Ghaggar sectors, Sector 31 and the MDC area, along with 32 employees. This records the proposal at that date, not its final acceptance. The report also describes PMDA road repairs and a city drainage master-plan exercise.

For a disputed complaint, request the asset identifier, latest handover order, executing division and maintenance contractor. Municipal inclusion alone does not settle ownership of an HSVP or PMDA asset.

## Ghaggar riverfront and flood-sensitive development

The Times of India reported on 7 February 2025 that a proposed six-kilometre riverfront from Majri Chowk to Sector 28 had moved from HSVP to PMDA. A consultant-selection process was being prepared. The report describes proposed cafes, seating and lighting, together with bank cleaning. It does not establish completed construction, environmental clearance or a final sanctioned design.

A separate 5 January 2026 report records PMDA administrative approval of about Rs 12.9 crore for riverbank protection informed by IIT Roorkee recommendations. Locations included spurs 1 and 5 facing the Multi Feature Park in Sector 24, spurs 19 to 25 facing Sectors 26 to 28, and a damaged apron at Herbal Park in Sector 26. Administrative approval is not proof of execution or completion.

Riverfront amenities, erosion protection, sewage interception and floodplain protection are different work streams. A project file should identify flood levels, channel boundaries, hydraulic assessment, land title, pollution-control obligations and relevant permissions. Beautification should not be read as evidence that the river is safe for bathing.

The district administration's official profile describes the northeast-to-southwest slope and rainfed torrents carrying gravel and pebbles. Reporting on 29 March 2025 records 17 garbage-dumping locations identified along the Ghaggar and directions for pollution-board action-taken reports every ten days. These conditions require coordination with irrigation, district administration and HSPCB, beyond the three urban agencies.

## Morni and Shivalik ecology

Morni is in Panchkula district's lower Shivalik hills. Haryana Tourism describes the Tikkar Tal lakes, hill vegetation, wildlife and trails towards the Ghaggar. District geography and tourism descriptions do not establish that all of Morni falls within MC Panchkula or the notified PMDA area.

Haryana's Wildlife Department lists Khol-Hai-Raitan Wildlife Sanctuary at 4,882.66 hectares and Bir Shikargah Wildlife Sanctuary at 767.30 hectares in Panchkula district. These protected areas and their eco-sensitive zones introduce restrictions separate from municipal building and sanitation rules.

The department's web table and other retrieved accounts differ on some eco-sensitive-zone notification dates and widths. This archive therefore does not prescribe a single buffer distance. For a particular parcel, consult the Gazette notification, coordinate annexures and applicable zonal master plan rather than applying a uniform radius to all Shivalik land. Hill slopes, drainage channels, forest land and riverbanks need separate scrutiny.

## Swachh Survekshan performance

Results announced on 17 July 2025 placed Panchkula 219th nationally in the 50,000 to 3 lakh population category and 22nd in Haryana, according to The Indian Express and Hindustan Times. News reports call the exercise both “Swachh Survekshan 2024” and “2024-2025”. These refer here to the same July 2025 results, not two surveys.

| Indicator | Previous survey, reported as 2023 | July 2025 results |
| --- | ---: | ---: |
| National rank | 139 | 219 |
| Door-to-door collection | 99% | 61% |
| Source segregation | 61% | 20% |
| Waste processing | 98% | 51% |
| Public toilet cleanliness | 100% | 50% |
| Dumpsite remediation | 0% | 99% |

The published figures are secondary-source accounts of survey indicators, not independently measured service coverage. Population categories, participating cities and scoring totals changed, so ranks alone are not like-for-like performance measures.

The Indian Express also reports full marks for market-area and water-body cleanliness. That indicator does not certify river-water quality. The Times of India questioned the water-body score on 21 July 2025, citing polluted channels and HSPCB measurements.

On 17 June 2026, Hindustan Times reported a proposed Rs 97 crore, five-year collection, transport and processing plan at the first general House meeting under Mayor Shyam Lal Bansal. Councillors described collection coverage as about 50%. This later meeting claim is not a revised official survey result, and the proposed plan is not a completed facility.

# Panipat tenders and infrastructure works, 2024 to 2026

## Finding municipal tenders and engineering works

The [MC Panipat tenders page](https://nagarnigampanipat.in/tenders/) links to [Haryana eProcurement](https://etenders.hry.nic.in/nicgep/app). The municipal page contains placeholder tender buttons, not a usable dated register of contracts. Search the state portal using the tender ID and the organisation chain `Haryana Government / Urban Local Bodies / MC Panipat`. HSIIDC, HSVP, PHED and PWD also issue works affecting Panipat. Their contracts should not be described as MC awards.

The [Haryana Engineering Works Portal FAQ](https://works.haryana.gov.in/FAQs.aspx) states that creation of a contractor ID is mandatory for participation in Haryana government department, board and corporation tenders. Creating an account is free. Registration for eligible contractors is distinct from account creation and provides an EMD-exemption benefit subject to applicable conditions. Check the individual tender for eligibility, exemption documents and society-only restrictions.

HEWP provides a public [works under village/town search](https://works.haryana.gov.in/HEWP_Login/Works_Under_Village.aspx) with department and approval-year filters. No Panipat-specific expenditure total was verified from that search. Statewide dashboard totals are not municipal figures.

## Nimbri legacy waste and the Murthal processing cluster

HSPCB's [monthly progress report up to March 2024, forwarded on 31 May 2024](https://nmcg.nic.in/writereaddata/fileupload/ngtmpr/1_Haryana%20-%20MPR%20March%202024.pdf), records that the Sonepat-Panipat integrated solid-waste cluster's 700-tonne-per-day waste-to-energy plant was commissioned on 16 August 2021. The report states approximate electricity generation of 6.77 MW. These are cluster figures, not Panipat-only waste generation or generation measured in 2026.

The [environmental-clearance application for Nimbri](https://environmentclearance.nic.in/auth/FORM_A_PDF.aspx?cat_id=IA%2FHR%2FMIS%2F202327%2F2021&pid=New), proposal `IA/HR/MIS/202327/2021`, describes remediation and reclamation of the existing dumpsite and construction, operation and maintenance of a sanitary landfill. It gives a project cost of Rs 28 crore and approximately 4.09 lakh tonnes of accumulated waste. These are historical application figures. They do not establish a 2024-26 award, expenditure or successful remediation.

The [published reproduction of the NGT judgment dated 7 November 2025, Ashish v. State of Haryana, OA 810/2024](https://research.lawsathi.in/national-green-tribunal/2025/ia-no-25-2025-original-application-no-810-2024-ashish-0701116000662025-2025110701/), records the concession arrangement for Sonipat, Panipat, Samalkha and Gannaur and processing at Murthal. It records removal of waste from the disputed Binjhol collection site and directs MC Panipat to comply with Rule 15 of the Solid Waste Management Rules and submit an action-taken report within three months. This linked text is a third-party judgment reproduction, not the official tribunal PDF.

The reproduction contains an internally inconsistent paragraph describing capacity, generation and a shortfall. Its calculated gap is not adopted here. Nimbri remediation, temporary collection-site clearance and Murthal processing are separate activities and should not be combined into one completion claim.

## Dye-house effluent, CETPs and damaged sewer lines

The HSPCB March 2024 report identifies Sector 29 Unit I and Unit II CETPs as HSVP facilities of 21 MLD each. Both were marked complying against analysis reports dated 26 March 2024. The same table identifies the HSIIDC Panipat Refinery Complex CETP as 2.5 MLD and says it could not be functional because sufficient effluent was unavailable. These are dated regulatory observations, not a guarantee of present operation or compliance.

For municipal sewers near the dyeing area, [The Tribune, 24 February 2024](https://www.tribuneindia.com/news/haryana/rs-4-7-crore-to-be-spent-to-repair-sewer-line-in-panipat-593870/), reports administrative approval and invitation of a tender for approximately 300 metres of sewer rehabilitation using cured-in-place pipe lining at an estimated Rs 4.70 crore. The report names MC Executive Engineer Rajesh Kaushik and links repeated failures in Sector 29 Part II to chemical effluent and crown corrosion. It does not establish the awarded price or completion. Its inconsistent references to Sector 20 and the much longer citywide sewer length are not used as engineering quantities here.

The HSPCB report separately records Panipat's existing STP capacity as 136 MLD and utilised capacity as 42 MLD, with six STPs complying. STPs treating sewage and CETPs treating industrial effluent are not interchangeable assets.

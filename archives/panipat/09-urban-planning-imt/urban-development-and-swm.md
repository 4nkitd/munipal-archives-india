# Panipat development plans, textile estates, regional transit and waste ecology

## Development plan 2031 and 2041 status

No authenticated notification establishing a final Panipat city Development Plan 2031 or 2041 was located in this research. A commercial article dated 30 September 2025 describes a "Panipat Master Plan 2031", but gives no usable gazette notification reference sufficient to establish statutory adoption. Its population and land-use claims are not adopted here. [P1]

Official DULB listings provide a more specific reference. A public notice published on 3 June 2026 concerns a restaurant in industrial Sector 28 of "Panipat-DDP to amend FDP-2021 AD". Another published on 8 July 2026 refers to industrial Sector 12 of "Panipat DDP to amend FDP2021 A.D." These titles establish that the department still used the 2021-plan amendment terminology for those notices. They do not establish the status of every later planning proposal. [P2]

The NCR Planning Board's December 2021 Draft Regional Plan 2041 is a regional document, not a notified Panipat municipal master plan. A regional planning horizon must not be substituted for a city-level development-plan notification. For a plot-specific decision, obtain the applicable gazette, zoning regulations, controlled-area map, amendments, sector layout and change-of-land-use approval from the competent planning authority. This record provides no parcel-level land-use certification. [P3]

The frontmatter date is a year-only archival placeholder for the 2026 compilation. Source documents have their own dates, including the November 2023 water-reuse study and the 2025 NCRTC annual report.

## Sector 29 textile and dyeing estate

The November 2023 India-EU Water Partnership study hosted by the National Mission for Clean Ganga describes Sector 29 Parts I and II as a major textile industrial cluster. It reports canal water supplied through a 40-million-litre tank and pipeline distribution, with some industries also using permitted groundwater sources. Its historical industrial-area figures are 485 hectares and 624 units in production for Sector 29, and 367 hectares and 570 units for Sector 25. These figures are study inputs, not a November 2026 industrial census. [P4]

The study identifies two Sector 29 common effluent treatment plants, each of 21 MLD capacity. It lists both under HSVP, not HSIIDC. At the report's reference date, Unit I was complying and Unit II was non-complying according to the HSPCB information cited. Do not label those historical compliance findings as current laboratory results, or assume that every industrial estate utility is operated by HSIIDC.

The study proposes reuse of secondary-treated sewage from Sewah Road I and II STPs, with combined design capacity of 60 MLD, for the Sector 29 textile cluster. A separate proposal links the 15 MLD Refinery Road STP to IOCL cooling-water demand. These are assessed business models, not proof that tertiary treatment, pipelines or purchase agreements were commissioned. The report assesses ultrafiltration and reverse osmosis costs and identifies industrial water-quality requirements. Suitability must be established for each process, particularly where dissolved salts matter. [P4]

## HSIIDC and the IOCL refinery corridor

Environmental appraisal minutes from 6 and 7 April 2017 describe HSIIDC's proposed industrial estate on Refinery Road near the Panipat refinery, approximately 5 km from the then NH-1. The proposal covered 373 hectares, with net planned area of 361.32 hectares, and land from Bohli, Rajapur, Dadlana and Begampur. Intended industries included plastic packaging, polyester fibre and woven sacks. These are historical appraisal particulars, not confirmation of final built-out area. [P5]

The proposal included a 10 MLD CETP in two 5 MLD phases. The committee sought further clarity on industrial categorisation and a proposed plastic park. The minutes must not be presented as an unconditional clearance or evidence that the proposed facilities operate today. Sector 29 textile estate, HSIIDC's Refinery Road estate and IOCL's own refinery facilities are distinct assets with different approval and utility responsibilities.

For a more recent environmental record, the Joint Committee's report dated 6 January 2026 in NGT OA 411/2025 concerns IOCL's 2G ethanol plant. It records visits on 3 October and 26 December 2025, identifies inadequate separation of storm water and process wastewater, and recommends permanent removal of the untreated-water discharge arrangement to Drain No. 2, separate storm drains and a sludge-management plan. These are committee findings and recommendations. They are not a finding that every refinery-corridor unit has the same problem or proof that remediation was completed. [P6]

## Delhi-Panipat RRTS and the Karnal extension

NCRTC's 24 December 2020 release records Haryana's approval on 23 December 2020 of a 103 km Delhi-Panipat RRTS corridor with 17 stations. That is the original proposal, not the final description of the later extended corridor. [P7]

NCRTC's 2024-25 annual report says a revised Delhi-Panipat-Karnal DPR was submitted to the Government of India in April 2025 and was under approval at the report date. The current project-details page describes approximately 136 km and 17 main-line stations excluding Sarai Kale Khan, and expressly states that its map and information are tentative and subject to final approval. [P8, P9]

The project page names Sonepat, Gannaur, Samalkha, Panipat and Karnal. Do not describe this proposed corridor as an operating service, reuse the original 103 km as the extended alignment length, or infer construction completion from a DPR or state approval. Exact station land requirements and access roads need approved alignment and acquisition records.

## Swachh Survekshan and solid waste

Swachh Survekshan 2024-25 results were announced on 17 July 2025. The published survey report records Panipat as "PANIPAT (M CL)" at rank 39 in the medium-city population category of 50,000 to 3,00,000, with 9,959 points out of 12,500. Components were 8,489 for the assessment, 500 for garbage-free-city certification and 1,200 for ODF certification. Preserve the report's ULB label and category rather than treating rank 39 as an unrestricted ranking across every city size. [P10, P11]

The category reflects survey classification, not a replacement for the corporation's legal status or current population. The Tribune's 18 July 2025 account corroborates rank 39 and third position among Haryana's medium cities. Rankings alone do not establish complete waste segregation, scientific disposal or compliance of sewage treatment plants. [P12]

The FY 2026-27 budget meeting report includes Rs 650 lakh for a construction and demolition waste plant at Nimbri and Rs 890 lakh for eight road sweepers with a five-year operation and maintenance provision. They are reported provisions, not verified operating assets. Household solid waste, construction debris, textile effluent, CETP sludge and domestic sewage require separate collection, treatment and compliance records. [P13]

## Drain No. 1, Drain No. 2 and the Yamuna

Drain No. 1 runs through the city from Kabri Road towards Chautala Road, joins Drain No. 2 near Chautala Road, and the combined drainage reaches the Yamuna at Khojkipur. The Tribune's 12 December 2025 report, citing an HSPCB survey, identified 50 untreated sewage discharge locations on Drain No. 1, compared with 43 in February. Responsibility was attributed to MC Panipat for 42 points, HSVP for five, PHED for two and HSIIDC for one. These are survey-date counts, not a current closure certificate. [P14]

The November 2023 NMCG-hosted study documents much higher BOD, COD and coliform loads downstream of the municipal area than at upstream sampling locations, using HSPCB 2023 data. It distinguishes industrial effluent treatment from domestic sewage treatment. A treatment plant's installed capacity does not establish how much sewage reaches it or whether its effluent complies. [P4]

Reporting on 31 May 2025 describes HSPCB show-cause notices concerning five municipal STPs and estimates about 78 MLD reaching STPs against more than 100 MLD city discharge. Later reports use different sewage-generation estimates, so they should not be combined into an exact time series. On 22 May 2026, HSPCB's chair again called for inspections and enforcement concerning Panipat's drains 1 and 2. Neither a notice nor an inspection instruction demonstrates subsequent compliance. Verify drain tapping, flow meters, bypasses, plant test reports and receiving-water samples separately. [P15, P16]

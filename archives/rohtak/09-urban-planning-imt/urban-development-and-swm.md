# Rohtak development planning, IMT industries, sanitation and water ecology

## HSIIDC IMT Rohtak and institutional responsibilities

The [HSIIDC estate profile](https://hsiidc.org.in/activities-and-services/infrastructure-imts/imt-rohtak), checked on 22 November 2026, describes IMT Rohtak on the Delhi-Rohtak-Hisar route using the older NH-10 designation. It labels phases I, II and III "Planned & Developed" and gives a total area of 3,680.64 acres.

| Phase | Area in acres | Establishment year listed by HSIIDC |
| --- | --- | --- |
| I | 859 | 2007 |
| II | 1,893.19 | 2008 |
| III | 928.45 | 2013 |

HSIIDC lists internal roads, stormwater drainage, water supply, sewerage, electrical distribution, street lighting and green belts, plus a 10 MLD common effluent treatment plant. This is an estate infrastructure listing, not an independently verified measurement of present operation or treatment compliance. Industrial effluent capacity must not be confused with municipal sewage treatment capacity.

HSIIDC's estate management, TCP land-use controls and municipal sanitation responsibilities are separate. For a plot-specific question, consult the [HSIIDC layout plans](https://hsiidc.org.in/investor-zone/layout-plans), development plans and allotment conditions. Do not infer municipal ownership of an IMT road or drain solely from its location within the wider urban area.

## Maruti Suzuki R&D and Asian Paints

HSIIDC identifies Maruti Suzuki India Limited as an allottee for R&D facilities and a test course. Maruti Suzuki's [R&D centre description](https://www.marutisuzuki.com/engage/technology/suzuki-advantage.html) describes a 600-acre integrated facility. Its [testing facilities page](https://www.marutisuzuki.com/engage/technology/suzuki-advantage/research-and-development.html) lists endurance, noise and vibration, high-speed, braking, ride and handling tracks, nearly 70 vehicles testable simultaneously and 250 laboratories. These are company-published facility descriptions, not municipal project approvals. The Rohtak R&D centre should not be described as a vehicle assembly factory.

For Asian Paints, HSIIDC lists integrated paint manufacture with a final capacity of 400,000 kilolitres per year. A [February 2025 environmental impact assessment](https://www.scibpaints.com/content/dam/asianpaints/website/secondary-navigation/about-us/corporate-citizenship/EIA_Report-Rohtak.pdf) concerns proposed expansion of the existing plant at Plot No. 1, Sector 30B, HSIIDC IMT Rohtak. It records an existing project cost of Rs 1,111.82 crore, proposed expansion cost of Rs 125 crore and total of Rs 1,236.82 crore.

The EIA cites environmental clearance letter J-11011/1158/2007-IA-II (I), dated 9 April 2008, and a consent to operate dated 22 February 2023, valid to 30 September 2027. Those are statements in the proponent's EIA. The expansion proposal is not proof that expansion clearance was issued or the additional capacity commissioned. HSIIDC's capacity listing and the EIA must be read with their respective dates and approval stages.

## Development plan 2031 and the separate 2041 regional framework

A publicly accessible [reproduction of the TCP Rohtak map](https://www.rohtakproperties.com/public/uploads/rulesregulation/master-plan-rohtak-2031.pdf) is explicitly titled "Revised Draft Development Plan of Rohtak - 2031 AD for controlled areas within & outside municipal corporation limit." It carries drawing number D.T.P. (R) 2007/20, dated 30 June 2020, and says it incorporates changes approved by the state-level committee on 3 September 2019. It refers to section 346(4) of the Haryana Municipal Corporation Act, 1994 and section 5(4) of the Punjab Scheduled Roads and Controlled Areas Restriction of Unregulated Development Act, 1963.

The map distinguishes municipal and controlled-area boundaries, residential and industrial uses, public utilities, green belts, water bodies, canals and drains. Its legend includes water works, disposal works and solid-waste disposal. A reproduced draft map is not sufficient evidence of the operative land use for a khasra or plot. A secondary-hosted document titled [Rohtak Final Development Plan 2031](https://www.scribd.com/document/900516494/Gazette-154-2022-Ext-14335-28-53) is a retrieval lead, but its notification text was not verified here. Obtain the latest gazette notification, map and zoning regulations from TCP before treating either copy as legally controlling.

No separately notified city-level "Rohtak Development Plan 2041" was verified in this research. The [NCRPB Draft Regional Plan 2041, December 2021](https://ncrpb.nic.in/pdf_files/DraftRegionalPlan-2041_English.pdf) is a multi-state regional framework, not a replacement title for Rohtak's 2031 map. [Hindustan Times, 17 June 2026](https://www.hindustantimes.com/india-news/new-cities-expressways-and-zones-for-environment-rules-ncr-regional-plan-2041-explained-101781685746257.html) reported Board clearance for further approval, with later ministerial approval still required. That report does not establish final gazette notification. The final notification status was not independently established as of this record's verification date.

## Swachh Survekshan and solid-waste management

[The Tribune, 3 March 2026](https://www.tribuneindia.com/news/haryana/rohtak-mc-gears-up-to-improve-swachh-survekshan-rank/) reports that Rohtak moved from 109th to 25th nationally in the preceding survey but fell to fifth within Haryana. It describes preparations for Swachh Survekshan 2025-26, not the results of that forthcoming assessment. The report does not establish the population-category denominator, so the rank should not be presented as a category-free national league table.

The report quotes an MC official giving the following preceding-survey indicators:

| Indicator | Reported score |
| --- | --- |
| Market-area and water-body cleanliness | 100% each |
| Door-to-door collection | 96% |
| Residential cleanliness | 95% |
| Dumpsite remediation | 93% |
| Public-toilet cleanliness | 80% |
| Waste generation versus processing | 75% |
| Source segregation | 58% |

These are newspaper-reported survey indicators, not current measurements or audited daily processing rates. They show why collection coverage and segregation must be kept separate. Commissioner Dr Anand Kumar Sharma also directed bulk waste generators to manage waste responsibly and enter a formal agreement where they use municipal collection, transport and disposal services.

An [Amar Ujala report dated 14 June 2025](https://www.amarujala.com/haryana/rohtak/municipal-corporation-will-collect-garbage-from-every-house-for-five-years-in-88-crores-rohtak-news-c-17-roh1019-671705-2025-06-14) describes a tender of Rs 88 crore for five years of door-to-door collection. This is a reported procurement proposal, not proof of contract award, service commencement or expenditure of Rs 88 crore.

## Tilyar Lake, stormwater and drainage ecology

The official-hosted [City Sanitation Plan 2019 & 2031](https://www.ulbharyana.gov.in/Website/Rohtak/Images/7c3808e3-e548-48fe-877c-7487cadcc6f3.pdf) covers water supply, sewerage, solid waste and stormwater drainage. Its baseline says there was no natural drain within the city, drainage covered 60% of the approved municipal area, 34 newly added colonies lacked stormwater coverage and one master stormwater drain existed. These are historical plan observations. The document's references to an ODF target of March 2018 show that its 2019 and 2031 horizons are planning horizons, not evidence of a 2026 service survey.

The 2031 map reproduction separately marks the JLN Feeder, Bhalaut sub-branch and named link drains. The sanitation plan's statement about the city therefore must not be expanded into a claim that the wider controlled area has no drainage network. Preserve the distinction between canals supplying raw water, stormwater outlets, sewage lines and industrial effluent systems.

[The Tribune, 7 December 2024](https://www.tribuneindia.com/news/haryana/rohtak-admn-plans-to-develop-tank-in-25-acres-to-store-surplus-potable-water/) reports a PHED proposal under AMRUT 2.0 for a sedimentation and storage tank on 25 acres within the Tilyar Lake campus. It states that the proposal had been sent for state approval and was intended to provide 16 days of storage. PHED Executive Engineer Tarun Garg reported city drinking-water demand of about 80 MLD, rising by up to 20% in summer.

This was a water-storage proposal, not a verified completed lake-restoration project. The same report mentions Rs 160 crore for increasing storage at water works in eight nearby villages; that amount should not be assigned to the Tilyar tank alone. No current Tilyar water-quality series, ecological condition assessment or proof of separation from polluted inflows was verified here. A clean-water-body survey score cannot substitute for lake chemistry, sewage-inflow checks or an ecological assessment.

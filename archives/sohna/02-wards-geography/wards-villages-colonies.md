# Sohna wards, villages, mohallas and planning sectors

## What the ward index establishes

The District Administration's 2025 municipal electoral-roll page lists Ward 1 through Ward 21 and 47 booths. Its PDF filenames are dated 28 January 2025; the page was last updated on 17 February 2025. The State Election Commission's notification SEC/2ME/2024/2033 of 30 December 2024 set 28 January 2025 for final publication. That notification concerns preparation of rolls, not a new boundary schedule.

The accompanying `wards.json` contains one record per ward. Its booth references come from the district index. Localities are included only where a cited municipal work title supplies an association. Empty arrays mean no verified allocation in this research, not an absence of settlements. Zone and engineering division are unknown. Mohallas in the `colonies` field are locality names, not assertions of statutory colony regularisation.

No complete, current gazetted boundary description was verified. Do not use this index for parcel-level jurisdiction or infer that every village in Sohna tehsil belongs to the council. Census 2011 ward data covers the earlier 15-ward municipal committee and cannot be relabelled as the present 21 wards.

## Old Sohna town and mohallas

The 2020 study *Integrating Built Heritage in Development Planning: Small and medium towns of Gurgaon District* places Shiv Kund in the old-town core. It associates Ward 16 at the time of study with Thakurwada, Pathanwada, Gujjar Ghati and Library Chowk. It calls the area around the Kund Mahajan Mohalla. These are historical geographical descriptions, not a verified 2026 ward map.

Municipal work descriptions supply other dated references. Tender `2024_HRY_359020_1`, published 26 February 2024, names Mohalla Darjiwada and Big Bazar in Ward 15. Tender `2023_HRY_303335_1` names the Bhurpara Saini Chaupal to Pahar route in the same ward. Tender `2023_HRY_297711_1` names Bhagat Wada in Ward 18; `2023_HRY_298461_1` names Balmiki Mohalla behind Bari Masjid there. Tender `2026_HRY_497577_1` names Ram Mandir Chowk and Saini Mohalla lanes in Ward 17.

Published Bar Council address lists also contain Kayasth Wara, Khati Wara, Madna Wara, Khajuri Wara, Lohiya Wara, Mirza Wara, Chippi Wara, Baniawara and Mohalla Gurudwara. Addresses sometimes give conflicting ward numbers for the same locality. They are useful spelling and locality leads, not legal delimitation evidence. Personal names, house numbers and telephone numbers from those lists are not reproduced here.

## Planning sectors 1 through 38 are not electoral wards

The Sohna planning-sector series, commonly described as Sectors 1 through 38 and marketed as South of Gurugram, is separate from the municipal ward series 1 through 21. Sector numbers do not identify wards. A licensed housing colony, revenue village, municipal ward and development-plan sector can overlap without sharing a boundary.

The Final Development Plan Sohna 2031 was notified on 15 November 2012 as `CCP(NCR)/FDP-2031/GGN-SHN/2012/3543`, with drawing `DTP(G)2103/2012` dated 9 November 2012. Its explanatory note covers 45 controlled areas totalling 45,687 hectares. Proposed urbanisation is 5,600 hectares; existing town and village abadi within that proposed urbanisation add 391 hectares. These are planning areas, not a statement of municipal area.

| Plan reference | Stated use or provision |
| --- | --- |
| Sector 15 | Rozka Meo industrial area and Textile Valley context |
| Sectors 17 and 25 | Housing adjoining industrial sectors for industrial workers |
| Sector 25 | 50-hectare pocket for low and medium income housing |
| Sector 31 | 16-hectare pocket for low and medium income housing |
| Sector 27 | Approximately 37 hectares for mandi and heavy building materials; 99 hectares for Transport Nagar |
| Village abadi edges | Approximately 120-metre strip for village expansion and amenities |

The plan places growth mainly north-east, east and south-east of the existing town. Aravalli hills constrain the other sides. It identifies the Mehandwara Nadi drainage route towards Indri lake and warns that acquired IMT land is low-lying and flood-prone. The plan's projected population of 640,000 for 2031 is not a census count. No sector-to-ward mapping is supplied here.

## Villages and the wider Sohna area

Census records identify Sohna Rural Part, Zakupur, Raipur, Sampki Nagli, Mohammadpur Gujar, Dhunela, Berka and Raisena as distinct geographical units in the Sohna area. Nearby Alipur, Hariahera, Ghamroj and Bhondsi also appear in the Census geographical list. Census classification alone does not establish present municipal membership.

Baluda has direct Ward 4 municipal-work evidence. The ULB regularisation register places notified colony polygons under municipality Sohna in the revenue estates of Dhunela, Sohna, Zakupur and Jakhopur. That is evidence about those polygons, not necessarily the entirety of each revenue estate.

Spellings vary between records. Mohammadpur Gujjar corresponds to the Census spelling Mohammadpur Gujar; Raiseena and Raisena occur alongside Raisina. The ULB register uses Zakupur for Amtek Colony Extension but Jakhopur and Sohna for Amtek Colony. Preserve those source distinctions when matching khasra numbers. Lakhuwas, Sancholi, Silani and Damdama are relevant to wider Sohna-area research, but proximity or a Sohna postal address does not establish council jurisdiction.

## IMT Sohna and the electronics manufacturing cluster

The 2012 development plan records 607 hectares already acquired by HSIIDC along KMP for an Industrial Model Township. It also discusses future expansion. The industrial planning belt must not be assumed to fall wholly within a particular municipal ward.

Haryana's *Make in Haryana Industrial Policy 2025* describes an Electronics Manufacturing Cluster of approximately 500 acres at IMT Sohna under the Government of India's EMC 2.0 scheme. The policy places it along KMP and the Delhi-Mumbai Dedicated Freight Corridor, with expressway and rail connectivity. This is an industrial-development policy statement, not proof that every proposed facility is operating. IMT Sohna is distinct from IMT Manesar. No ward assignment for the EMC was verified.

## Regularised colonies, notification evidence from 2023 to 2026

The Haryana ULB civic-amenities register lists five Sohna entries, each attributed to notification `09/45/2024-4C-II` dated 6 March 2024. Its colony IDs, revenue estates and layout polygons are more reliable identifiers than informal colony names.

| ULB code | Colony ID | Register name | Revenue estate | Acres |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| SOH58 | 8015 | Unnamed Colony C-4 | Dhunela | 10.480 |
| SOH59 | 8035 | Hari Nagar Colony | Sohna | 3.820 |
| SOH60 | 8039 | Unnamed Colony C-13 | Sohna | 16.170 |
| SOH61 | 8041 | Amtek Colony Extension | Zakupur | 7.660 |
| SOH62 | 8120 | Amtek Colony | Jakhopur, Sohna | 31.330 |

These entries total 69.460 acres. Each register row links a layout and lists regularised khasra numbers. Verify a property against that polygon and khasra schedule. A similar colony name outside the polygon is not covered by association.

The ULB notification index includes 2023, 2024 and 2025 batches and an aggregate status document uploaded on 13 October 2025. The upload date is not the notification date. A February 2024 news report described five Sohna colonies using the spelling MTech, whereas the ULB register calls the two named industrial-side colonies Amtek. The register's names and identifiers are used here.

No additional Sohna-specific 2025 or 2026 colony notification was verified in this research. The Governor's address of 20 February 2026 gives a statewide total of 858 notified colonies, not a Sohna list. Separate 2023 DTCP reporting about colonies outside municipal limits must not be merged with the five ULB entries above. Regularisation does not itself establish a ward allocation, ownership title or building-plan approval.

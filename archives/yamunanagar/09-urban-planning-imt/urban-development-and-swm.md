# Yamunanagar-Jagadhri development plans, industrial estates, canal pollution and solid waste management

## Development plan chronology

The September 2019 report of the state-level planning committee concerns a draft Yamunanagar-Jagadhri development plan for 2031. Its projected population was 13,13,500, with a proposed average residential density of 250 persons per hectare. Reported allocations included 3,339.58 hectares residential, 376.86 hectares commercial, 1,070.55 hectares industrial, 953.33 hectares transport and communication, 181.89 hectares public utilities, 315.46 hectares public and semi-public uses, and 854.87 hectares open space. These belong to that draft and must not be merged with the later 2041 allocations. [1]

The reproduced Haryana Gazette plan drawing identifies the final development plan for 2041 as notification CCP(NCR)/YNR/FDP-2041/2021/1548 dated 28 September 2021. It cites sections 346(7) of Haryana Act 16 of 1994 and 5(7) of Haryana Act 41 of 1963. The draft 2041 notification was CCP(NCR)/YNR/DDP-2041/2020/1542 dated 18 November 2020. The final drawing is DTP(Y)-104/2021 dated 13 July 2021. The drawing available for this record is a third-party-hosted reproduction, not a certified gazette copy. [2]

Hindustan Times' October 2021 account of the final plan reports 9,866.96 hectares of planned land and a projected 2041 population of 14,86,000, compared with 4,78,574 in 2011. It reports 2,977.7 hectares allocated to industrial use and 304.99 hectares for public and semi-public use, including proposed education-city and medi-city uses. The plan also proposed roads, open space and green belts on the de-notified Dadupur-Nalvi canal alignment. These are planning proposals, not proof of completed development. [3]

## Industrial clusters and HSIIDC Manakpur

The municipal website identifies metal, utensil and plywood industries and names Shri Gopal Paper Mills and Saraswati Sugar Mills among large industries. The final-plan reporting describes concentrated metal-utensil and plywood markets, auto-component units and heavy machinery associated with sugar mills. These citywide industrial activities are not all inside the Manakpur estate. [3][4]

Manakpur is an HSIIDC industrial estate on NH-907, the Jagadhri-Chhachhrauli road. The Government of India MSME district profile records Phase I at 54.50 hectares with 232 plots. Its plot allotment, occupancy and price figures are historical profile data and should not be used as a current availability register. [5]

On 20 December 2024, The Tribune reported that HSIIDC proposed Phase II over 258.04 acres at Sector 30 and Sector 30A, Manakpur. HSPCB had held an environmental-clearance public hearing on the preceding Wednesday, 18 December 2024. The proposed estate included industrial, residential, institutional, public-building and commercial plots, roads, water supply, sewerage and stormwater drainage. The hearing account described a proposed municipal contract for solid-waste disposal, boundary green belts and water-quality monitoring. A public hearing is not environmental clearance, allotment or commissioning. This record does not verify those later milestones. [6]

The 2021 planning report proposed additional industrial development along NH-344, NH-907 near Manakpur and state highway 6A. An industrial land-use designation does not by itself confer a building permission or pollution-control consent. [3]

## Western Yamuna Canal riverfront proposal

The riverfront described in March 2025 reports is on the Western Yamuna Canal, also called Western Jamuna Canal or WJC. It should not be described as a completed riverfront on the main Yamuna channel.

Both reports describe an approximately 4.5 kilometre western-bank corridor from OP Jindal Park toward the old Jagadhri-Saharanpur road or Fatehpur bridge, under Divya Nagar Yojana. Municipal Commissioner Ayush Sinha was quoted as saying administrative approval had been granted. Proposed works included paths, drainage, fencing, gardens, seating, lighting and bank protection. Azad Nagar, Chitta Mandir Road and Shanti Colony were named as nearby localities. [7][8]

The reported cost is unresolved. The Tribune gives Rs 128.78 crore, while Hindustan Times gives Rs 12.87 crore for apparently the same corridor. Neither amount is adopted here as the definitive sanctioned cost. The administrative approval, detailed project report and tender should settle the discrepancy. The reports' statements about preventing garbage accumulation and sewage discharge describe intended outcomes, not verified water-quality improvements or completed interception works. [7][8]

## NGT proceedings and dated effluent evidence

The Tribune's April 2022 report links ditch-drain pollution to Chaudhary Ompal and others versus State of Haryana before the National Green Tribunal. It describes a state report filed on 4 April 2022 that estimated total twin-city sewage at 134.5 million litres per day, of which 68.5 MLD reached treatment plants and approximately 66 MLD remained untreated. A 75 MLD treatment proposal was reported at that time. The account quotes the NGT's 6 April direction to prevent pollution and submit compliance status as of 31 July 2022 by 15 August 2022. This is secondary reporting of the proceeding, not a retrieved certified order. [9]

A later primary source is HSPCB's Haryana monthly progress report for June 2025, transmitted on 6 October 2025 and hosted by NMCG. It is submitted in NGT OA 673 of 2018. It reports the following town-level status. [10]

| Location or facility | Capacity | Reported June 2025 status |
| --- | ---: | --- |
| Jagadhri STP capacity | 24 MLD | Operational, utilized at 24 MLD and complying |
| Yamunanagar aggregate STP capacity | 67.5 MLD | Operational, utilized at 67.5 MLD; five STPs reported complying |
| Manakpur CETP, HSIIDC | 3 MLD | Complying, analysis dated 7 May 2025 |
| Proposed Jagadhri/Yamunanagar CETP | 19.5 MLD | Proposed |
| Yamuna Nagar treatment project, PHED | 77 MLD | 2% physical progress; stated completion timeline 31 March 2027 |

The June report also records ULB bio/phyto-remediation pilots in MC Yamunanagar-Jagadhri drains. Its compliance tables are dated assessments of listed plants, not a certification that all city drains or the WJC meet standards. The 2022 proposal and the 2025 project capacities should not be assumed to be the same project without the sanction documents. [10]

## Solid waste and Swachh Survekshan

The Swachh Survekshan 2024-25 report lists "YAMUNANAGAR (M CL)" at rank 47 in the 3 lakh to 10 lakh population category, with a total score of 8,267 out of 12,500. The published row shows SS2024 assessment score 7,647, Garbage Free City score 0 and ODF score 1,000. These columns have different stated maxima and should not simply be added without the report's scoring method. The result is a category rank, not rank 47 across every Indian city regardless of population. The report copy consulted is hosted by OpenCity; PIB confirms that the edition's awards took place on 17 July 2025. [11][12]

MCYJ's FY 2026-27 budget reporting allocates Rs 34.02 crore to sanitation and waste management, covering door-to-door collection, vehicle and machinery upkeep and material recovery facility centres. An allocation is not evidence of waste processed, source segregation achieved or a remediated dumpsite. The official municipal site links the Swachhata citizen app and Haryana ULB grievance portal for sanitation complaints. No verified local processing-tonnage series or completed legacy-waste remediation certificate was retrieved for this record. [4][13]
